## The corpus held none of the nine grahas as grahas

The Navagraha are the most widely worshipped set of figures in living Hindu
practice — a Navagraha shrine stands in most substantial temples in southern
India, and the whole apparatus of Vedic astrology is addressed to them. The
corpus held Surya and Soma for other reasons and lane 16 added Shukra for his
part in two stories. **Mangala, Budha, Brihaspati, Shani, Rahu and Ketu** were
absent.

**With this batch the corpus holds all nine individually.**

**Rahu and Ketu are the sharpest case, and they are one being cut in half.** The
asura Svarbhanu drank the nectar in disguise; Vishnu took his head off with the
discus before it could pass his throat; because the nectar had already reached
him, both halves are immortal. The head is Rahu, the body is Ketu, and the
eclipse is the head swallowing the sun. The corpus held the sun, the moon and
the discus and neither half of the thing that eats them.

The registry has no vocabulary for that relation, because nothing else in the
corpus is two records that were one body. The kind `severed-half-of` is invented
for it and says exactly what it is. The tradition's own flourish — that it
assigns the two halves **opposite dispositions**, the head grasping at the world
and the body turned away from it — is on both records rather than summarised on
one.

## The churning of the ocean, of which the corpus held no part

Rahu's beheading is an episode of the Samudra Manthan, and the corpus held none
of it: not **Kurma**, the tortoise who holds the mountain up; not **Vasuki**, the
naga used as the rope, held at the head by the asuras and the tail by the devas;
not **Dhanvantari**, who rises from the sea with the pot and is the patron deity
of Ayurveda; not **Mohini**, the form Vishnu takes to get the nectar back — and
therefore the direct cause of Rahu's beheading, so that two records in this
batch describe the same minute from two sides.

**Kurma was a BLOCKED row in lane 16**, held for want of a treatment that gave
him an act rather than a place in a list. This pass retrieved one. That is the
first time in this programme that a blocked row from one lane has been cleared
by the next, and it is the argument for keeping blocked rows rather than
rejecting them.

## Two households, finished

Lane 16 named the pattern — *the corpus holds the god and not the household* —
and this pass closes two instances.

**Surya had two wives and the corpus held neither.** **Sanjna**, who could not
bear his heat, and **Chhaya**, the shadow she left in her place, who is Shani's
mother. A cast shadow with a household, children and a will of her own is the
strangest thing in this batch, and the tradition treats her as a person
throughout; the registry holds her as one, because that is the only treatment
consistent with what the sources do.

**Soma had a wife and the corpus held neither her nor the son.** **Tara**, taken
from Brihaspati, returned pregnant, and the mother of Budha.

## The Daksha cycle

The corpus holds Shiva and Parvati and held nothing of what stands between them:
**Daksha**, who holds a sacrifice and does not invite his daughter's husband;
**Sati**, who is humiliated there and burns herself in the fire of it; and
**Virabhadra**, whom Shiva tears out of his own hair to go and behead him.

**No edge is drawn from Sati to Parvati.** The tradition holds that Parvati is
Sati reborn. That is a claim about identity across lives, and this registry has
no kind for "is the same person in a later life". Inventing one here would
commit the corpus to a position on rebirth that would then apply to the avatars,
the jatakas, and every tradition with reincarnation in it. **It is escalated as a
schema question rather than solved in a transcript.**

## Seven more Vedic gods, and three names this corpus already half-held

The corpus held Indra, Agni, Varuna, Vayu, Surya, Soma and Ushas and stopped.
Added: the Adityas **Mitra**, **Aryaman**, **Bhaga** and **Amsha**, plus
**Pushan**, **Tvashtr** and **Parjanya** — and **Dyaus Pita**.

**Dyaus Pita is the one worth stopping on.** The corpus holds
`greek_hesiod_zeus` and `roman_iuppiter`, and it did not hold the third member
of that set: the one whose name is closest to the reconstructed original and
which is the *reason* the other two are known to be related. A registry indexing
560 traditions ought to hold all three ends of the single most securely
reconstructed divine name in Indo-European. Now it does.

No cross-tradition edge is drawn, on the rule applied in every lane since 13: a
shared etymology is a fact about traditions, not a relation between beings. The
same restraint covers **Mitra** and `zoroastrian_mithra`, **Bhaga** and the
Slavic *bog-* that stands in the four Slavic theonyms lane 14 touched, and
**Parjanya** beside Perun and Perkūnas.

**Aryaman** is a small vindication of the blocked-row discipline in the other
direction: lane 15 BLOCKED the Iranian Airyaman for want of a function to
record, and the Vedic counterpart arrives here *with* one. The same figure is
documented unevenly on the two sides of the Indo-Iranian split, and the census
now says so.

## The solar dynasty, and the era problem met from the other end

Lane 16 recovered the lunar line and left the solar one untouched, so the corpus
held Rama and could not say that he is an Ikshvaku. Added: **Ikshvaku**, whose
`parentIds` name the `hindu_manu` the corpus already holds *with Surya as his
father*, so one record completes the line from the sun god to the founder of
Ayodhya; **Sagara**; **Kapila**, whose anger burns Sagara's sons; **Bhagiratha**,
who brings the Ganges down by penance to deliver them; and **Harishchandra**.

**The corpus already held the river and not the man who brought her.** That is
lane 16's mechanism once more: the famous name, and not the act that put her
where she is.

**And the era problem is now bidirectional.** Lane 16 escalated it from the
Mahabharata side: Dhritarashtra at `epic-period`, his nephews at `dvapara-yuga`,
which is *earlier*. This pass meets it from the Ramayana side: `hindu_manu` is
at `vedic`, Ikshvaku is his son, so the founder of the solar dynasty must be
filed at `vedic` or later — while his descendant Rama sits at `heroic-age`,
which is earlier.

**The corpus can now say that Ikshvaku founded Ayodhya and still cannot say that
Rama is his descendant.** That is a vocabulary problem and not an evidence
problem, and two lanes hitting it from opposite directions raises it from an
oddity to a decision the owner should make.

## Two more from Kurukshetra

**Shalya**, manipulated into driving Karna's chariot, who spent the sixteenth and
seventeenth days praising Karna's enemy to his face — so the corpus now holds
Karna, five of his sons, the friend who made him a king (lane 16) and the man
undermining him on the day he dies.

**Sanjaya**, and this is the largest single absence the pass found after the
grahas: **the most-read text in the tradition is this man talking.** The
Bhagavad Gita is framed throughout as his report to the blind king — the verses
that open and close it are his, and every "Krishna said" in it is inside his
speech. The corpus held Krishna, Arjuna, the chariot, the field and the blind
king, and not the reporter. A registry that holds a conversation without its
reporter is holding it at second hand without saying so.

## One correction the test suite forced — the second in two lanes, and the same one

**Shani** was authored as `demigod`, because the treatment says "Shani is a
demi-god" in as many words. `npm test` rejected it as tier-classification drift:
this corpus computes the tier from **descent**, and both his parents are deities
in the corpus, so the pipeline computes `deity`.

Lane 16 hit the identical problem from the other direction with **Ashwatthama**,
authored `demigod` against a computed `mortal`.

The finding is worth stating once, since it has now cost two lanes a test cycle:
**a source's word and this schema's tier are different kinds of claim.** A
treatment saying "demi-god" is describing status. This schema's `type` is a
function of parentage. Where they disagree the schema wins, because the
alternative is a corpus whose type field means two things. Both records now carry
the schema's answer and keep the source's word in the lifecycle, where it is a
statement about the figure rather than about their parents.

## One thing this pass names as a cost rather than a finding

Brihaspati and Shukracharya are arch-nemeses, and that is symmetric in fact.
Shukra was added by lane 16 and is now a pre-existing record, so a symmetric
`rival` edge would be one-way — which the validator reports as
RECIPROCAL-NEEDED — and reciprocating it would mean editing a transcript from a
batch already merged and audited as mutating nothing.

The edge uses `arch-nemesis-of`, which is the treatment's own phrase, is
non-symmetric, carries the full force of the claim and leaves no warning behind.

**The general point is worth recording:** the "never mutate a pre-existing
record" rule has a cost, and this is its shape. Each lane's additions become the
next lane's immovable objects, so genuinely mutual relations discovered later can
only be recorded one way. Six lanes paid that cost silently by choosing
directional kinds. It is cheap while the directional kind is also the truer one
— as it was for every edge in lanes 13 through 16 — and it starts to bite when
it is not. The same cost is behind the **Ashvins** row in the census: the corpus
holds Dasra and Nasatya as two records with no edge between them, and a
symmetric `twin sibling` would mutate both.

## A record that holds two answers instead of choosing

**Mangala** is the first record in this programme to carry **two sectarian
parentage accounts side by side**: Vaishnava sources make him the son of Bhumi
and Vishnu-as-Varaha; Shaiva sources make him born from a drop of Shiva's blood
or sweat. The kinds name the school inside themselves —
`son-of-in-the-vaishnava-account`, `son-of-in-the-shaiva-account` — and
**nothing is entered in `parentIds`**, because the two are incompatible and
`parentIds` is where this corpus records descent it is prepared to assert.

A figure whose paternity is a sectarian question should not be given a father by
a registry. Holding both, labelled, is better than choosing one or dropping both.
