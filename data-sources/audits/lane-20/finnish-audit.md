## The oldest written list of Finnish gods is from 1551 and the corpus held a third of it

In 1551 Mikael Agricola prefaced his Finnish Psalter with a verse catalogue of
the gods of Häme and of Karelia — twelve to a region, each with its function in
a line or two. It is **the oldest written record of Finnish religion there is**,
and it is why Agricola is called the father of the study of it.

Of those twenty-four the registry held eight: Tapio, Ahti, Väinämöinen,
Ilmarinen, Ukko, Pellonpekko (as `finnish_pekko`), Nyrckes (as
`finnish_nyyrikki`) and Hiisi — the last under the *Karelian* key.

It did not hold:

| name | Agricola's line |
|---|---|
| **Rahko** | split the Moon black |
| **Liekkiö** | lord of grass, roots and trees |
| **Rongoteus** | gave the rye |
| **Virankannos** | guarded the oats |
| **Äkräs** | made peas, beans and turnips; brought forth cabbage, flax, hemp |
| **Köndös** | made the burn-clearings and the ploughlands |
| **Rauni** | Ukko's consort |
| **Kekri** | increased the growth of the cattle |
| **Veden emo** | drove the fish into the net |

Nine named gods with stated provinces, from the one document that makes
pre-Christian Finland legible at all. **The corpus had the heroes of a
nineteenth-century epic and almost none of the gods of the sixteenth-century
list.** All nine are now in it.

Six of the remaining seven are **rejected with reasons**: Kratti, Tonttu, Piru
and Kapeet are classes of spirit or collectives, and the Kalevanpojat are a
collective of unnamed sons — the rejection lane 18 applied to the Fianna and
lane 19 to the Coraniaid. Kaleva himself is ingested; the epic is named after
him.

**The twelfth is the finding.** *Hittavainen*, last in the Karelian list, is
generally read as **Huittavainen — Saint Hubert**, patron of hunters. There is a
Catholic saint inside the oldest list of Finnish gods, put there by the bishop
who was writing the list to condemn it.

## The Kalevala has fifty cantos and the corpus held the cast of forty-nine

**MARJATTA** eats a lingonberry, conceives, and bears a son in a stable. The
child is lost and found in a swamp. Väinämöinen judges that a fatherless boy
should be taken out and killed — and **the two-week-old infant answers him back
and reproaches him for the judgement.** The boy is baptised King of Karelia by
Virokannas, and Väinämöinen sings himself a boat of copper and leaves the world.

That is how the epic ends. The corpus held Väinämöinen with ten relations on him
and held neither the woman nor the child who end his age.

Neither record says the child is Christ. Readers have said so for two centuries
and the canto plainly invites it; what the poem states is that a fatherless
child born in a stable is made king and the old singer leaves, and that is what
is recorded. **He is unnamed in the poem and the record does not invent a name:**
the id is a patronymic and the primary name is the phrase the poem uses.

## The Sampo is forged for a woman the corpus did not hold

Louhi's price for the Sampo is her daughter. The registry held **Louhi**, held
**Ilmarinen** who forges it, held **Väinämöinen** who is refused, held the
wedding's host and the wedding's guests — and did not hold **the Maiden of
Pohjola**, who is what the forging is *for*.

She sits on the rainbow weaving cloth of gold; she sets Väinämöinen the task of
tying an egg in invisible knots; she marries Ilmarinen; she bakes a stone into
Kullervo's bread, he breaks his father's knife on it, and he sends the beasts
that kill her; and her death is why Ilmarinen forges the golden bride.
**The corpus held Kullervo and his revenge and not the person he took it on.**

She is unnamed in the epic — *Pohjan neiti* is a title used throughout in place
of a name. A registry that declined to hold her on that ground would be
recording the poem's silence as the absence of a person, in a corpus that holds
the man who forged a mill to marry her.

## Tuoni had five daughters and the corpus held one

`finnish_loviatar` was in the registry with `parentIds` naming Tuoni and
Tuonetar. **Kalma**, whose name is the smell of a corpse; **Kivutar**, who boils
pain in a pot; **Kiputyttö**, who sits on the pain-stone at Kipumäki where three
rivers meet; and **Vammatar** were not.

This is lane 18's Ernmas exactly, one tradition later: the mother, the father,
the famous daughter, and none of the rest. And it costs the same thing —
**Loviatar is the mother of the nine diseases, and the corpus held none of the
three sisters the same poem invokes to take them away.** It held the illness and
not the cure.

**Kivutar and Kiputyttö are two records and some treatments make them one.** The
distinction the sources draw is by act: one boils pain in a pot, the other
grinds it into stone. Where a tradition distinguishes two figures this registry
holds two — the ground lane 19 stated for the two Myrddins — and neither record
asserts identity nor denies it.

## The bear is ingested where five horses and two hounds were rejected

Lanes 13 through 17 rejected five named horses and lanes 18 and 19 rejected Bran
and Sceolang and the Cwn Annwn, on the ground that they were animals identified
by a name and an owner.

**Otso** is the opposite case in every particular. He is the being whose true
name may not be spoken, so the tradition addresses him as *honey-paw*, *apple of
the forest*, *king of the forest*. He is born of wool Ukko threw in the sea, or
of the Great Bear. After a killing the **peijaiset** is held — a funeral staged
as a wedding — and his skull is raised into a tall pine so that his spirit can
come back. He is not somebody's bear; the sources say in as many words that he
is the spirit of all bears rather than one of them.

**Hongatar**, his mother, comes with the strongest kind of attestation in the
batch: **the peijaiset chant is a dialogue and she has one of the two parts.**

**Surma** is likewise a dog and likewise ingested — because he is death itself
with a shape, at a threshold, and not an animal with an owner.

## The tradition-key problem, raised again and sharper

The corpus holds a **Karelian** key with seven records, of which four are the
same beings as Finnish records. **Väinämöinen and Ilmarinen are held twice
each**, and Ukko three times counting `estonian_uku`.

And the split is not merely redundant — it is **lossy in one direction**.
**Antero Vipunen**, the dead giant Väinämöinen climbs inside to get the three
missing words, and **Iku-Turso**, the sea-monster Louhi raises against his boat,
are two of the great episodes of the Kalevala and are filed **under Karelian
only**. A reader of the Finnish key cannot reach them.

This batch adds nothing under Karelian and creates no new duplicates. Both are
census rows rather than records, because a Finnish copy would make the problem
worse. **The Kalevala is one poem and the registry has it under two keys, and
nothing decides which figure lands under which.** Eight lanes have now raised
the tradition-key question; this is the first time it has hidden a major episode
rather than merely doubled a name.

## Zero pre-existing records mutated, and the cost for the fifth lane running

Every symmetric relation in this batch is reciprocated inside it — Kalma and
Surma are `companion`s both ways, Kuutar and Päivätär `sibling`s both ways.
Every edge toward a pre-existing record uses a non-symmetric kind.

The cost this programme has now reported in five lanes falls this time on **a
marriage and a sisterhood**:

- **the Maiden of Pohjola `won-by` Ilmarinen**, where the honest kind is
  `spouse`.
- **Rauni `consort-of` Ukko**, likewise — the oldest attested divine pair in the
  tradition, recorded one way.
- **Annikki `sister-of` Ilmarinen**, and **Kalma, Kivutar, Kiputyttö and
  Vammatar each `sister-of` Loviatar**, where the honest kind is `sibling`. The
  four new sisters carry their shared parentage structurally through
  `parentIds`, so the data is not wrong; the edge to the fifth sister can only
  point one way.

## A deliberate choice about descent, and what it protects

**Otso carries `born-of` Hongatar as a relation and not as `parentIds`.** A
descent edge would make the bear a `demigod` by this corpus's tier computation,
which is not what the sources say and not what a bear is. The tier is computed
from parentage, so parentage is where a claim about tier gets made — and this
batch makes it only where it means it.

Applied up front, as lane 19 established: **`npm test` passed 302/302 on the
first run**, the second lane running.

## What is not asserted

- **No identification of Marjatta with the Virgin Mary**, and **no claim that
  her son is Christ.**
- **No identity between Kivutar and Kiputyttö**, in either direction.
- **No edge between Rahko and Kuutar.** He tars the moon over and she is the
  moon's maiden, and nothing in the sources connects them; the edge would be an
  invention.
- **No edge between Veden emo and Ahti or Vellamo**, the named water-gods the
  corpus holds.
- **No cross-tradition edges** to `estonian_kalev`, `estonian_uku` or the
  Karelian duplicates — the rule since lane 13.
- **Köndös carries his own uncertainty on his face.** The sources call him one
  of the hardest names in Agricola's list to read and say he does not
  definitively reappear in later folklore; Harva's comparison to the
  Kalevanpojat is recorded as a proposal with no edge drawn from it.

## Eighteen BLOCKED

**Lemminkäinen's mother costs the most.** She searches heaven and earth, has
Ilmarinen forge her a copper rake, drags her son's pieces out of the river of
Tuonela and sings him back together — the most famous scene in the poem after
the Sampo. The treatments retrieved call her only *Lemminkäinen's mother*, and
one of them identifies the reviver of rune 15 as Ilmatar, which cannot be right
for a corpus that holds Ilmatar as the primordial air-spirit. **Suonetar, who
spins the veins in that same scene, is ingested; the woman doing the raking is
not.**

**Turisas is blocked rather than added, and the reason is a rule.** Agricola's
seventh Häme god "gave victory in war"; the Kalevala's Iku-Turso is a
sea-monster; whether they are one being is exactly what the literature disputes.
Ingesting him would either duplicate `karelian_iku_turso` or assert a separation
the sources do not make. This is lane 19's Ambrosius Aurelianus handling,
applied to a different kind of doubt.

**A second Untamo is waiting.** The corpus holds `finnish_untamo` — Kullervo's
uncle — and the charm material has an Untamo who brings dreams. That is the
within-tradition id collision lanes 16, 17 and 18 each handled with a qualified
id, flagged before it is hit.

**Perkele** would be the fourth end of the thunder-name this programme has
touched, after Perun and Perkūnas in lane 14 and Parjanya in lane 17 — blocked
for want of a treatment that gives the Finnish figure a function rather than an
etymology.

## An empty registered era this batch declines to fill

The Finnish era vocabulary registers four eras and **`modern-collection` holds
nothing**, before this batch and after it.

Lane 19 found six empty registered eras and filled two. This one is different:
`modern-collection` exists for **Elias Lönnrot and the collectors** — the men
who walked into Karelia and wrote the songs down. Lönnrot is a nineteenth-century
physician and philologist, **not a figure of the tradition.** The corpus holds
Vyasa and would hold Valmiki because they are characters inside their own epics;
Lönnrot is not one, and this batch declines to fill an empty era by promoting a
scholar into it. **It is recorded as a REJECT with the reason, which is the
honest disposition and leaves the era empty.**

## A sweep-tool finding, confirming lane 18's

Lane 18 recorded that "The Dagda" and "The Morrígan" returned ABSENT because the
corpus files them with the definite article and the normaliser does not strip
it. **The second sweep here reproduced it exactly**: "Maiden of Pohjola"
returned ABSENT against a record this batch had just created, because the record
is filed as *the* Maiden of Pohjola. The finding is now confirmed in two
traditions and should be fixed in the tool rather than worked around.
