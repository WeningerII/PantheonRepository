================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added five individuated, well-attested Otomi divinities missing from the corpus: the pre-Hispanic rain lord Muye (Otomi counterpart of Tlaloc; Wright Carr 2007, Méndez Gómez 2023), the wind god and universal creator Eday/Edähi of the Jilotepec-Querétaro Otomi (Relación geográfica de Querétaro 1582, Carrasco 1950), the oracle serpent-goddess Acpaxapo of Lake Xaltocan (Anales de Cuauhtitlan tr. Bierhorst 1992, Carrasco 1950, Béligand 1998), and the two great Sierra Otomi lords still worshipped today, Maka Xumpø Dehe the Lady of the Waters ("la Sirena") and Maka Häi, Lord Earth (Dow ed. 1995, Galinier 1990/2004, Gallardo Arias 2012). Yocippa was deliberately not added because scholarship identifies Yocippa/Yozipa as another name of Otontecuhtli-Xocotl, already in the corpus. Also excluded were unindividuated spirit classes (antiguas, judíos, seed spirits, the plural muye thunder-lords, which are subsumed under the singular colonial rain lord) and post-contact syncretic individuations such as Moctezuma and Santa Rosa. No attested genealogies or personal relations link these five to each other or to the existing four figures, so each carries a cited solitary justification instead of speculative wiring.
