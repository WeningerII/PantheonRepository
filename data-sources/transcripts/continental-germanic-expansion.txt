================================================================================
THE GAP — EIGHT GODDESSES AND NOT ONE GOD
================================================================================

A 62-name check across the WHOLE registry returned 14 hits and 48 absences.

The Continental Germanic key held **eight records**, and every one of them is a
goddess: Baduhenna, Beda, Boudihillia, Fimmilena, Friagabis, Nehalennia,
Nerthus and Tamfana — Tacitus and a set of Roman-era altars.

**There was no god in it at all.**

Not Wodan. Not Donar. Not Saxnot. **Those three are the exact list a Saxon was
made to renounce by name at baptism** — *ec forsacho Uuoden ende Thunaer ende
Saxnote* — in a ninth-century manuscript from Mainz, and the registry held none
of them.

Nor **Tuisto** and **Mannus**, the earth-born god and his son whom Tacitus says
the Germanic peoples sang about as their origin — the first Germanic figures
named in any written source at all.

Nor a single figure from **the Merseburg Incantations**, which are the only
surviving text in Old High German that names the heathen gods: Phol, Wodan,
Sinthgunt, Sunna, Frija and Volla, in twelve lines, in a manuscript from Fulda.

================================================================================
AND THE ROMAN-ERA ALTARS DO NOT STOP AT EIGHT
================================================================================

The eight held records are a sample of a much larger body of dedications. Also
attested, each on its own stone or tablet, and none of them in the registry:

    Hariasa        Cologne, 187 CE
    Vagdavercustis Cologne, 2nd century
    Hludana        five inscriptions, Rhineland and Frisia, 197–235 CE
    Sandraudiga    North Brabant, 2nd century
    Vihansa        a bronze tablet near Tongeren
    Harimella      Birrens, Dumfriesshire

**Hludana is attested five times and was not in a corpus that held Beda and
Fimmilena, who are attested at one site each.**

================================================================================
A VOCABULARY EXTENSION, MADE DELIBERATELY AND DOCUMENTED
================================================================================

The Continental Germanic era vocabulary registered **exactly one era**:
*Continental Germanic religion under Rome (1st c. BCE – 3rd c. CE)*.

That bucket cannot honestly hold this material. The Merseburg charms are 9th or
10th century; the Old Saxon Baptismal Vow is 9th; the *Origo gentis
Langobardorum* is 7th; Tacitus's origin songs are about a time before all of it.
Filing any of them under a label that ends in the third century would be putting
a date on a record that the record's own sources contradict.

So this batch **adds two era keys** to `ERA_ORDER` and `ERA_DATES` in
`app/data.js`, which is the maintenance path the file's own integration-points
comment documents:

    continental-germanic-mythic-origins                    textual −50 … 100
    continental-germanic-migration-and-conversion (4th–10th c.)  textual 350 … 1000

The existing era key is **unchanged, in the same position, with the same dates**.
Nothing already filed moves.

================================================================================
GODAN AND FREA ARE NOT NEW RECORDS, AND THAT IS THE POINT
================================================================================

The *Origo gentis Langobardorum* is the textual source of the Lombard theonym
**Godan**, and names **Frea** beside him: Gambara asks Frea to help her sons,
Frea tells her to have the women tie their hair across their faces and stand
where Godan will see them at dawn, Godan asks who the long-beards are, and the
name of a people is fixed by a joke.

Godan is Wodan and Frea is Frija. **They are recorded as aliases on the two
records this batch creates and not as two further records**, and the Lombard
episode is on those records' lifecycles. Lane 20 found the corpus holding
Väinämöinen twice under two keys and said that duplicating a figure to record a
second attestation makes the registry worse. This is the same decision taken in
advance, inside one tradition key, where the temptation is strongest.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BATCH DOES NOT INGEST
================================================================================

The **Ingaevones, Herminones and Istvaeones** are peoples; Tacitus names Mannus's
three sons only by the peoples descended from them, and there is nothing to
ingest but three ethnonyms. The **Matronae** — Aufaniae, Vacallinehae and the
rest — are collectives of unnamed mothers, hundreds of them, and belong in a
different kind of record. The **Irminsul** is an object. **Arminius**,
**Marbod** and **Civilis** are historical commanders, not figures of the
tradition.

**Viradecdis** and **Ricagambeda** are BLOCKED rather than ingested: the sources
consulted describe them as Celtic or Germanic without settling which, and this
registry files by tradition.

**Balder of the Merseburg charm is a census row and not a record.** Whether Phol
and Balder are one figure or two is the charm's central textual question; the
corpus holds `norse_baldr`; and creating a Continental Germanic Balder would
either duplicate that record or assert an answer. **Phol is ingested and Balder
is not, and neither record decides it.**
