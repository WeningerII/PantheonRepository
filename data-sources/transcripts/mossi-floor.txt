================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added the five individuated ancestral figures of the Mossi dynastic founding legend — Yennenga, Rialé, Ouédraogo, Zoungrana, and Oubri — which the core ethnographies (Skinner 1964; Izard 1970 and 2003; Tiendrébéogo/Pageard 1964; Delobsom 1932; Guirma 1971) document as named, venerated royal ancestors whose cult (kiimse observance and the naam ideology) is integral to Mossi traditional religion alongside the existing deities Wende and Tenga. All are typed mortal/deceased and wired into a single genealogical chain; symmetric spouse links occur only between the two new figures Yennenga and Rialé. The kinkirsi bush spirits and generic kiimse ancestors were deliberately excluded as unnamed spirit classes, and Yennenga's father Nedega was left out as belonging to the Dagomba/Mamprusi rather than the Mossi tradition. No further individuated Mossi divinities are attested in the scholarship consulted.
