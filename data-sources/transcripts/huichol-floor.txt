================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added five genuinely individuated, well-attested Huichol figures beyond the existing set: Kieri (the wind-and-sorcery plant-god of Solandra/Datura, adversary of Kauyumari), Tatéi Haramara (Our Mother the Sea), Tatéi Werika Uimari (the double-headed eagle sky-goddess), Tatéi Niwetsika (Our Mother Corn and mother of the five corn-maidens), and Watákame (the first-farmer flood hero and ancestor of the Wixárika). Each carries at least two real scholarly citations drawn from the standard ethnographic corpus (Lumholtz 1900/1902, Zingg 1938, Furst & Myerhoff 1966, Myerhoff 1974, Knab 1977, Schaefer & Furst 1996, MacLean 2012). Where the record supports it I wired new figures into the existing pantheon: Werika Uimari as emerging from and attending Kauyumari, and Watákame as the mortal devotee subordinate to Nakawé who saves him from the flood. I stopped at five because the remaining Huichol theonyms in the literature are either aliases of existing figures (e.g. Maxa Kwaxi for Kauyumari) or generic collective classes such as the plural rain 'Our Mothers' (Tateima) that scholarship does not further individuate into named, separately-attested persons.
