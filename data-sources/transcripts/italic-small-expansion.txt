================================================================================
SOURCING NOTE
================================================================================

No direct outbound web access in this session; research went through
server-side web search, which returns the substance of reference pages without
fetching them. Citations name the attesting object or the reference work.

The Oscan additions come almost entirely from ONE object: the Agnone Tablet
(Tabula Osca / Tabula Agnonensis), a bronze of the 3rd-2nd century BCE found
near Agnone in Molise in 1848 and in the British Museum since 1873
(G_1873-0820-149). It carries two lists of divine names in the dative, all
honoured in the "Garden of Ceres" (húrtúm Kerríiúm), and is one of the longest
Oscan texts we have. The existing `oscan_kerres` record already cites it.

The readings of several Agnone names, and almost all of the identifications
proposed for them, are contested. The discipline followed here is the one the
Lane 1 Umbrian pass established: the THEONYM is ingested, because the tablet
securely attests it; the gloss is recorded as a proposal attributed to the
literature, never as the figure's settled character.

Three entries in the Agnone list are deliberately NOT ingested, because they
name classes rather than individuals — Diumpaís Kerriíaís (the spring-nymphs,
Latin Lymphae), Anafríss Kerriíúís (the rain-powers, Imbres) and Maatúís
Kerriíúís (the dew-powers). This registry does not ingest creature or power
classes. Hereklúí Kerriíúí is the existing `oscan_hercle` under his Cereal
cult-title and is dispositioned ALIAS, not added again.

Cult-title variants are SPLIT into their own records rather than folded in,
following this corpus's own practice in these traditions: Messapian already
keeps `messapian_zis`, `messapian_zis_menzanas` and `messapian_zis_batas` as
three records joined by `manifested-as`, and Umbrian keeps Tursa Cerfia and
Tursa Iovia apart. The two Agnone forms of Oscan Jupiter are therefore recorded
beside the base theonym on the same pattern.
