================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added five further individuated Old Prussian deities that scholarship attests beyond the existing core: Pergrubrius (spring/vegetation, from Meletius's 1551 feast account), Pilnitis (wealth and full granaries), Bardoayts (ships and seafarers), Puskaitis (chthonic earth god of the sacred elder, lord of the Barstukai), and Auschauts (health and sickness). All five are named in the sixteenth-century Sudovian Book and/or Meletius (1551) and are treated in standard scholarship (Mannhardt 1936; Gimbutas 1963; Bojtár 1999; Kregždys 2020), so each carries at least two real citations. The four agrarian/household gods are wired to one another as companions in the productivity-fertility-health cluster reported by the sources, while Bardoayts is placed in solitary because the record ties him to no other figure. I stopped here deliberately: remaining names in the Sudovian list (e.g. Swayxtix, Pockols) and Grunau's added figures are either bare functional labels or of contested authenticity, and generic classes such as the Barstukai are spirit-collectives rather than named individuals, so authoring them would be padding rather than documented individuation.
