================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added two genuinely individuated, well-attested figures beyond the existing set. Ohworhu is a named southern-Urhobo water divinity (edjo) honoured in the two-day Ohworu masquerade festival at Evwreni, documented by Perkins Foss's Museum for African Art catalogue and Nabofa's ethnography, and wired to Oghene as one of the edjo held to be his children/messengers. Ubiesha Etarakpor is the historical prophet of Kokori who founded the Igbe dance religion in 1858, an indigenous Urhobo monotheism, attested by Nabofa's chapter in Studies in Urhobo Culture and Afatakpa's 2021 Yoruba Studies Review article; he is wired to Oghene, the God he preached. I deliberately excluded borderline or non-individuated items: Osonobrughwe (an alias of the already-registered supreme god Oghene), Erivwin (the ancestral realm/cult rather than a named being), Eravwe Oganga (a masquerade aspect of Ohworhu, recorded as materialCulture), a putative war-deity "Oranre" (the sources only confirm it as a calendar/festival month), and Olokun (whose Urhobo-specific attestation is too entangled with the pan-regional Edo/Yoruba deity to author safely here).
