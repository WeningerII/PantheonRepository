================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added the four remaining well-individuated Totonac divinities documented in the core ethnographic literature: the Sun Chichiní and his brother the Moon Papa' (creation narrative and sibling rivalry per Ichon 1973 and Beaucage and Rojas Mora 2021), the mother goddess Natsi'itni who fashions children's souls and is mother of the Sun (Ichon 1973; Pérez González 2011), and Kiwikgolo, the Old Man of the Forest and Owner of wild nature (Ellison 2004; Beaucage and Rojas Mora 2022). Chichiní is wired to Natsi'itni (mother) and Papa' (reciprocated sibling). I deliberately excluded the Morning Star/Lucero (attested for the Sierra de Puebla generally but without a secure individuated Totonac name in the sources checked), the unnamed dueños as spirit classes, and the Papantla vanilla-legend figures (Tzacopontziza/Xanat), whose attestation is modern folkloric rather than scholarly ethnography. Beyond these, Ichon's monograph and the Handbook of Middle American Indians treat remaining Totonac supernaturals as owner-classes rather than named individuals.
