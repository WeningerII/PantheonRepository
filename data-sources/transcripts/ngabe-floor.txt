================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added four individuated figures: the twin celestial brothers Ñänä (Sun) and Sö (Moon), attested both in the Noncomala-Rutbe cycle (Edwardes & Spence 1912, p. 127; Alexander 1920) and in the modern bilingual etnohistoria "Ñaglon bata Sö / El Sol y la Luna" (Séptimo & Joly 1986, digital ed. 2021); Evia, the named mother of the twins in that modern recension (single-collection attestation, flagged in her notes); and the prophetess Mama Chi (Delia Bejerano de Atencio / Besikö Kruningrobu, d. 1964), founder of the Mama Tatda religion of Ngöbö (Young 1971; Guionneau-Sinclair 1987; Young in Encyclopedia of World Cultures 1995). Deliberately excluded after verification: "Tibi Tolero" (maize-seed guardian, blog-attested only with no traceable scholarly publication), the Metobo, Mironombo and Cronombo (classes of rival shamans in Séptimo & Joly, not individuals), Rey Montezuma and the brothers Midi and Juite Chali (post-contact legendary-historical narratives rather than divine or heroic beings), and Cuasrán (a Boruca, not Ngäbe, figure). Young's Encyclopedia of World Cultures entry also reports an unnamed lightning god and unnamed culture heroes, which cannot be individuated from the accessible scholarship; note that Gutenberg, archive.org and most publisher sites were blocked by the sandbox proxy, so verification relied on search-engine extraction of the cited works.
