================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added five well-attested, individuated Yakut figures absent from the corpus: Kün Dzhösögöi Toyon (horse-and-cattle god of the Ysyakh festival), Kydai Bakhsy (the primordial lower-world blacksmith who also reforges shamans), Baai Baianai (spirit-master of the taiga and patron of the hunt), Aan Alakhchyn Khotun (earth-goddess of the world tree Aal Luuk Mas), and Ilbis Khaan (spirit-lord of bloodshed and battle-frenzy). Each is documented across classic ethnographies and folklore scholarship (Holmberg/Harva 1927, Sieroszewski 1896, Jochelson 1933, Ksenofontov, Alekseev 1975, Emelyanov 1980) rather than being a spirit-class. Where sources support it I wired them into the existing pantheon — Dzhösögöi as a bright aiyy under Ürüng Aiyy Toyon, and Ilbis among the fierce spirits under Uluu Toyon — while the smith-, hunt-, and earth-deities stand solitary because attestation gives them no firm kinship. I stopped at five rather than padding with vaguer names (e.g. an unindividuated fire-spirit or thunder figure) whose attestation as discrete named individuals is weaker.
