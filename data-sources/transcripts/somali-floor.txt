REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added the three named individuals that Somali-studies scholarship genuinely individuates beyond the existing divine core (Waaq, Huur, Nidar, Ayaanle): the legendary pre-Islamic queen Arraweelo (Hanghe 1988; Mukhtar 2003, s.v. 'Araweelo'; Lilius in Bildhaan), the cannibal ogress Dhegdheer (Hassan 2007; Lilius; Ali Jimale Ahmed, Minnesota Humanities Center), and the pagan priest-king Bu'ur Ba'ayr of the Aw-Barkhadle legend, whom Sada Mire's peer-reviewed archaeology (African Archaeological Review 2015; Divine Fertility 2020) links directly to the cult of the existing sky god Waaq, wired here via a directed relation. The widely web-listed giants Habbad ina Kamas and Biriir ina Barqo were excluded because searches found no verifiable print scholarship behind them, as were the werehyena Qori-ismaris (a shapeshifter class, not an individual) and Islamic-era saints and wit-tale heroes (Aw Barkhadle, Wiil Waal, Igaal Shidaad), which fall outside the traditional-religion frame. With these three additions the individuated, citable record of Somali traditional religion and legend appears exhausted.
