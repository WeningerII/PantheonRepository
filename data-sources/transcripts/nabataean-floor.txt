================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added five well-attested, individuated deities beyond the existing set, each documented in the standard Nabataean epigraphic scholarship: Shaiʿ al-Qawm (guardian of caravans and the tribe, 'the god who drinks no wine'), al-Kutbā (patron of writing, commerce and divination, wired to the existing al-ʿUzzā via the Venus–Mercury consort pairing), Obodas the God (the divinised king Obodas I worshipped as a healer/Zeus Obodas), Aʿrā (tutelary god of Bostra and the royal house, later fused as Dushara-Aʿrā), and Hubal (attested in Nabataean epigraphy at Hegra, invoked with Dushara and Manōtu in a tomb curse). Each carries at least two real citations, principally Healey's The Religion of the Nabataeans (2001) and Tomb Inscriptions of Madaʾin Salih (1993), Alpass's The Religious Life of Nabataea (2013), Teixidor (1977), Negev (1986) and Fahd (1968). I stopped at these five because the remaining Nabataean divine names in the record are either aspects of figures already in the corpus, foreign imports (e.g. Atargatis, Baalshamin) whose centre of gravity lies in other traditions, or unnamed spirit-classes that do not meet the individuation bar.
