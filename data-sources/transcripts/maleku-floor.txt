================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

The existing corpus already held the three principal Malecu gods (Nharíne Cha Cónhe, Aóre Cha Cónhe, their daughter Jáfara) and the two chief demons (Orónhcafa of the sky, Jára of the earth). Beyond these, the individuated record scholarship documents lies almost entirely on the demonic side: Andrés Solano-Fallas's descriptive inventory of the Malecu demon-pantheon (drawing on Constenla Umaña and Castro's 'Laca Majifijica', 1993) names several further individual maíca, of whom I added the four most clearly attested as single named beings — Pilhinanhque, Cáramurulhca and Lhíca Lhafára (sky demons of the celestial place of torment, wired as subordinates of Orónhcafa) and Cumáserr (a terrestrial demoness under Jára). I deliberately excluded the demonic and divine spirit-CLASSES that carry the plurifier '-maráma' (yóricofa maráma, aúrunanhque maráma, nujteífa maráma, porélecsuífa maráma, tócu maráma, etc.), the unnamed 'son of Jára', and the name Nálhunálhu, which the sources describe inconsistently as a collective rather than an individual; no additional individuated named god survives scrutiny, since the river-headwater divinities are referred to only by locale ('the one of the headwaters of X') and their proper names were traditionally unspeakable.
