REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added three individuated, cited figures: the personified brother peaks Kibo and Mawenzi (numina, from Gutmann's Volksbuch der Wadschagga 1914, Dundas's Kilimanjaro and its People 1924, and Hutchinson's 'The Meaning of Kilimanjaro', Tanganyika Notes and Records 1965) and the mortal Tone, whose offence against Ruwa brought famine and whose death upon Kibo the legend uses to explain the landscape (Hutchinson 1965, p. 65, translating Gutmann's collections). I deliberately excluded Werner's one-legged sky-stranger 'Mrule' as a probable variant of the existing Murile, and the irimu ogres and warimu ancestors as unindividuated spirit classes. Chagga religion is otherwise strongly centred on Ruwa and the ancestor cult, with no further well-attested named deities in the ethnographic record (Dundas 1924; Gutmann 1909/1914).
