================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added the Sun, the male celestial being who with the Moon (Kana) procreated the sky people (yalyakali) and to whom the 1940s Cult of Ain sacrificed pigs en masse (Meggitt 1965, 1973; Feachem 1973; Jacka 2002; Biersack 2011), and Enda Semangko, the named Fertility Goddess of the Kyaka Enga documented by Bulmer (1965) and the Encyclopedia of Religion; the Sun's Enga-language name could not be independently verified in accessible sources, so native-term fields are left empty rather than guessed. Beyond these, the attested Enga record consists of unindividuated spirit classes — timango/semangko ghosts, kilyakai demons, epalirai forest spirits, yama enda wild women, kewanambo ogres, and the kepele/yupini cult complex whose woven figures and stones embody generic clan ancestors (Gibbs 1978; Wiessner and Tumu 1998; Dlugosz 1998) — which the scope rules exclude. The only further named individual recovered, the Kyaka stranger-ghost Komba Ralingki (Bulmer 1965), was excluded as single-source and the object of a single great-clan's cult, and the historical cult prophets such as Ain fall outside the registered mythic eras.
