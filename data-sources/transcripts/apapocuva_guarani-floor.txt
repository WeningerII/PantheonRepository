REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added one genuinely individuated figure, Charía (Xariá / the celestial "blue" jaguar), the eternal adversary of the divine twins in the Apapocúva-Guaraní world-cycle recorded by Nimuendajú (1914): the fishing enemy who clubs the younger twin (the Moon, jasy) before the elder resurrects him, and who as the celestial jaguar accounts for eclipses and threatens the world's destruction until restrained by Ñanderuvuçú (namandu). He is wired in via a directed slayer-of relation to jasy and subordinate-to namandu, and cited to Nimuendajú, Schaden and Métraux. Beyond this the Apapocúva record appears essentially exhausted relative to the existing five: Tupã is already carried as an aspect/alias of Ñanderu Mba'ekuá, the twins and their parents are already in the corpus, and the añay/jaguar-ogres and the eschatological jaguars are unnamed spirit-classes rather than individuated beings; I found no securely named additional prophet or deity in the sources checked (Nimuendajú 1914/1987, Schaden 1962/1974, Métraux 1928 & 1946, H. Clastres 1975/1995) to justify padding the set further.
