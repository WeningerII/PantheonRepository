PANTHEON REGISTRY — BANYARWANDA (RWANDA-RUNDI) CORE
Tradition string: Banyaruanda
Authored: 2026-06-23

Scope of this batch: the well-attested supreme-creator and dynastic-founder CORE that the
existing corpus (the Ryangombe / kubandwa-imandwa cult cluster: banyaruanda_babinga,
banyaruanda_ryangombe, banyaruanda_kalimulore, banyaruanda_binego) was missing. Three new
figures are authored:

  1. banyaruanda_imana   — Imana, the supreme creator High God of Rwanda AND Burundi
                           (Banyarwanda + Barundi). THE central absent figure.
  2. banyaruanda_gihanga — Gihanga (Gihanga Ngomijana), the divine culture-hero / founder-king
                           of the Nyiginya dynasty; introducer of cattle, fire, ironworking,
                           pottery, hunting, and the dynastic drum.
  3. banyaruanda_nkuba   — Nkuba ("Lightning/Thunder"), Umwami wo Hejuru ("King of the Sky"),
                           head of the Ibimanuka celestial line (Nkuba > Kigwa/Sabizeze > Muntu
                           > ... > Gihanga). The sky-father that wires the dynastic line.

Source basis (real scholarship, verified):
  - Alexis Kagame, Inganji Karinga (1943/1959) and the dynastic poem Ubucurabwenge — the
    classic native genealogical/dynastic recension (Nkuba > Kigwa > Muntu > ... > Gihanga;
    Gihanga the founder of the cow and the drum).
  - Marcel d'Hertefelt & A. Coupez, La royauté sacrée de l'ancien Rwanda: texte, traduction
    et commentaire de son rituel (Tervuren: Musée royal de l'Afrique Centrale, 1964) — the
    sacred-kingship ritual corpus; Nkuba as king of the sky and father of Gihanga's lineage;
    the sacred Fire of Gihanga.
  - Jacques J. Maquet, The Premise of Inequality in Ruanda (Oxford UP / IAI, 1961) — "Imana,
    the maker, created Kazikamuntu, the common ancestor of all humans"; the king as the
    incarnation of Imana.
  - Pierre Smith, Le récit populaire au Rwanda (Paris: Armand Colin, Classiques africains 17,
    1975) — the popular-narrative corpus (apologues, legends, tales) in which Imana and the
    Ibimanuka origin cycle are transmitted.
  - Jan Vansina, Antecedents to Modern Rwanda: The Nyiginya Kingdom (Madison: Univ. of
    Wisconsin Press, 2004) — critical history treating Gihanga as the legendary founder-
    archetype of the Nyiginya kingdom and the ibimanuka ("those fallen from the heavens").
  - Supporting: A. C. Werner, Myths and Legends of the Bantu (1933), ch. on Rwanda ("Imana and
    the Childless Couple": Imana gives Kigwa and Lututsi to Nyinakigwa).

Genealogical / theological notes used in the wiring:
  - Imana is the transcendent High God and Creator of Rwanda and Burundi; he is the principal
    agent behind the celestial origin of the royal line ("the sky was the point of departure
    ... for which the Creator is the principal agent": Habyarimana = "it is Imana who
    engenders"). He is not the biological father in the genealogy but the creator/origin of the
    line and of the first ancestor (Kazikamuntu, per Maquet). Hence Imana is wired creator-of
    Gihanga (origin of the line) and creator-of Nkuba (the Creator above the king of the sky),
    NOT as a genealogical father.
  - Nkuba (Lightning), king of the sky, is the head of the Ibimanuka line: "Nkuba (the
    thunder) begat Kigwa (the fallen one), who begat Muntu," and the line continues to Gihanga.
    Nkuba is therefore wired ancestor-of Gihanga (multi-generational celestial descent), NOT a
    direct father (the intervening links Kigwa/Sabizeze, Muntu, Kazikamuntu, etc., are not in
    this corpus, so directed ancestor-of/descendant-of is used rather than father/father-of).
  - Gihanga's type = demigod: a divinely-descended founder-hero of the Ibimanuka line who is
    venerated with a perpetual sacred fire and royal cult, but whom Vansina treats as a
    legendary archetype rather than a god proper; his divine progenitor (the sky-line) is
    several generations removed and not an in-corpus direct parent, so parentIds is left empty
    and the descent is carried as relations.

Note on the era key for Nkuba: the celestial-origin cycle and the descent to Gihanga belong to
the deep mythic past, so Nkuba is placed in `mythic-prehistoric` (alongside Imana); Gihanga,
the dynastic founder, is `heroic-age` per the registry instruction.

Note on omitted candidates: Nyabingi (the borderland Kigezi/Mpororo spirit-queen possession
cult) and Kigwa/Sabizeze were considered but NOT authored here. Nyabingi is a peripheral
borderland figure whose cult is largely a 19th–20th c. (colonial-era) anti-colonial resistance
phenomenon distinct from the central kubandwa and dynastic core, and is better treated in a
separate borderland batch. Kigwa/Sabizeze and Kazikamuntu are intermediate genealogical links
that would pad the batch without adding to the well-attested CORE the corpus was missing; they
can be materialized later as a genealogical bridge between Nkuba and Gihanga.
