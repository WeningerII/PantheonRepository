Zapotec (Ben'Zaa, the Cloud People of the Valley of Oaxaca) pantheon — the well-attested
creator-pair and the specialist gods of earthquake, omen and dream still missing from the
registry.

Six Zapotec figures are already in the corpus: zapotec_coqui_xee (Coqui Xee / Pije-Tao, the
infinite uncreated source of the gods), zapotec_cocijo (the great rain-and-lightning god),
zapotec_pitao_bezelao (Coqui/Pitao Bezelao, the lord of the dead and the underworld, patron of
Mitla), zapotec_pitao_copijcha (the sun-and-war god), zapotec_pitao_cozobi (the maize-and-harvest
god) and zapotec_xonaxi_quecuya (the death-goddess, Bezelao's consort). What is still missing is
the primal creator-couple from which the rest of the pantheon proceeds, and three of the
specialist "Pitao" deities recorded by Córdova.

I verified every figure by web search and cross-checked the standard scholarship: Fray Juan de
Córdova, *Vocabulario en lengua çapoteca* (1578) — which preserves the authentic theonyms and
their Spanish glosses; Fray Francisco de Burgoa, *Geográfica Descripción* (1674); Joseph W.
Whitecotton, *The Zapotecs: Princes, Priests, and Peasants* (1977); Michael Lind, *Ancient Zapotec
Religion: An Ethnohistorical and Archaeological Perspective* (2015); Guido Münch Galindo, *La
Teogonía Zapoteca y sus vestigios en Tehuantepec* (1983); and, for the effigy-urn iconography,
Alfonso Caso & Ignacio Bernal, *Urnas de Oaxaca* (1952) together with Nelly Robles García,
Leobardo Pacheco Arias & Mireya Olvera Sánchez, "El Señor Ocho Temblor y la Señora Agua: dos
urnas zapotecas de élite en Atzompa, Oaxaca" (*Arqueología* 49, 2015).

Five figures are firmly attested and authored below:
  • zapotec_pitao_cozaana   — Pitao Cozaana (Coqui Cozaana), the creator/multiplier of animals,
                              men and ancestors, patron of hunters and fishermen; the divine
                              progenitor, paired with Huichaana. Córdova glosses cozaana as
                              "produzidora o engendradora de los animales" (verb zàana, "to
                              give birth/engender").
  • zapotec_pitao_huichaana — Pitao Huichaana (Nohuichana), goddess of water, rivers and fish, of
                              fertility, generation, conception, childbirth and the human life-
                              cycle; patroness of midwives, women and children; "la Engendradora,"
                              "Madre de todas las criaturas," the foremost female deity, linked to
                              the moon and (in the colonial cult) equated with the Virgin Mary.
  • zapotec_pitao_xoo       — Pitao Xoo, the god of earthquakes (Zapotec xoo, "earthquake/tremor"),
                              the living force of the moving earth; rendered on the great Atzompa
                              urn as "El Señor Ocho Temblor."
  • zapotec_pitao_peeze     — Pitao Peeze, the god of omens, auguries, fate and fortune, to whom
                              the colaní (diviner) traced the sign or dream he read in the ritual
                              books; his name is glossed "god of omens."
  • zapotec_pitao_xicala    — Pitao Xicala (Pitao Pecala), the god of dreams (both xicala and pecala
                              gloss "dream"), reckoned an aspect of Pitao Peeze; bound up with the
                              love/lechery demon Pixèe Pecàla through Córdova's "owl-dream"
                              difrasismo (Smith-Stark).

DELIBERATELY NOT AUTHORED (to keep names distinct and avoid padding):
  • Pitao Pezeelao — NOT a separate figure. Córdova's "Pitào Pezèelào, dios del infierno" is the
    death-god already in the corpus as zapotec_pitao_bezelao (which carries "Pitao Pezeelao" as an
    alt name). The brief's "wealth/merchant" gloss for "Peeze" actually belongs to a *different*
    god, Pitao Paa (the god of merchants, wealth, good fortune and happiness, who enriched
    traders); Pitao Peeze proper is the god of omens/fate. To keep the names clean I author Peeze
    only in its attested omen/augury/fortune sense and leave the firmly-attested but out-of-scope
    Pitao Paa for a later batch.
  • Pixèe Pecàla (love/lechery) and Pitao Ziy (misery/misfortune) surfaced in the sources but fall
    outside the assigned candidate set; Pecàla is noted within Xicala's entry as its near-twin.
