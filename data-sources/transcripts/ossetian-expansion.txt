PANTHEON REGISTRY — FIGURE-AUTHORING TRANSCRIPT
Tradition: Ossetian
Eras (registered, exact): cosmogonic; classical-nart; departure; post-nart
Author pass: Ossetian held 10 records. This adds 27.

================================================================================
THE GAP — TEN RECORDS, NINE OF THEM ONE FAMILY, AND NO PANTHEON AT ALL
================================================================================

The Ossetian key held **ten records**:

    Donbettyr                       the water-lord
    Wærxæg                          the first Nart
    Akhsar, Akhsartæg               his twin sons, one of whom kills the other
    Dzerassæ                        Donbettyr's daughter, who marries Akhsartæg
    Uryzmæg, Khæmyts, Satana        their three children
    Bytsenon                        Khæmyts's wife
    Batraz                          her son, the steel hero

**That is one household.** Nine of the ten records are four generations of a
single Nart lineage, and the tenth is the water-god that lineage married into.

**The entire Ossetian pantheon was absent.** Not a thin patch — the whole layer.
The supreme god Xucau, who rules every other spirit in the system, had no record.
Neither did Uastyrdzhi, the patron of men and guarantor of oaths, who is the most
invoked figure in Ossetian religion. Neither did Uacilla the thunderer, Safa of
the hearth chain, Kurdalægon the heavenly smith, Æfsati of the hunt, Tutyr of the
wolves, Fælværa of the sheep, Alardy of the smallpox, Barastyr of the dead,
Aminon at his gate, Galægon of the winds, or Khur the sun.

**And the epic itself was missing its two most important figures after Batraz.**
Soslan, the stone-born hero whose cycle is the largest in the Ossetian Nartiada,
and Syrdon, the trickster Dumézil called the closest known Indo-European parallel
to Loki. The corpus held the Nart family tree and not the two Narts the stories
are mostly about.

================================================================================
THREE OF THIS BATCH'S FIGURES WERE ALREADY IN THE CORPUS — AS OTHER TRADITIONS'
ALIASES
================================================================================

The corpus-wide NFKD sweep over sixty-two candidate names returned **three hits,
and all three were alias strings on records belonging to other traditions**:

    Soslan   ->  circassian_sosruquo carries "Soslan" in its alt list
    Afsati   ->  karachay_balkar_apsati and svan_apsat both carry "Afsati"
    Totur    ->  karachay_balkar_totur

So a corpus search for the central Ossetian hero answered with a **Circassian**
record; a search for the Ossetian hunting god answered with a **Karachay-Balkar**
or **Svan** one. These are the legitimate one-cult-many-keys pattern — the same
figure genuinely is held under several tradition keys, and lane 33's gate
deliberately does not fire on cross-tradition shadows, because 685 of 710 of them
are correct. But they make an **absence invisible**: the name resolves, so nothing
looks missing, and the Ossetian figure has no record. Each of the three is added
here under its own key with an explicit `cross-tradition-counterpart-of` link.

A second sweep pass, on substring containment rather than exact match, was run to
catch the false-absence failure mode documented in lane 27 — a bare candidate name
sitting inside a longer recorded primary. It returned eighteen near-matches and
**every one was a coincidental collision in an unrelated tradition** (Uacilla
against Koryak "Illa", Taranjelos against "Tārā" and "Aran", Ælbeg against a Tuvan
"Yelbeghen"). No Ossetian figure was hiding under a longer name.

================================================================================
ONE GENEALOGICAL CONTRADICTION, FLAGGED RATHER THAN RESOLVED
================================================================================

The sources say plainly that **Uastyrdzhi is the father of Satana** in the Nart
epic. The pre-existing `ossetian_satana` record gives her parents as **Akhsartæg
and Dzerassæ**.

This batch does not change it. Rewriting a pre-existing record's `parentIds`
would also change its computed tier — Satana would go from **quartigod** (mortal
father 0 + demigod mother 0.5, halved) to **demigod** (deity father 1.0 + demigod
mother 0.5, halved to 0.75) — and a lane's authorisation to edit **alias lists**
does not extend to rewriting genealogy and silently reclassifying a figure.

So the claim is recorded where it can be seen, as a relation on Uastyrdzhi's own
record with the kind `named-as-father-of-in-the-nart-epic`, carrying a note that
states the conflict in full, and it is escalated in the audit. **The registry now
holds both readings and says which record asserts which**, instead of holding one
and pretending the other does not exist.

================================================================================
BLOCK 1 — THE SUPREME GOD AND THE GREAT DZUARS
================================================================================

Five figures: **Xucau**, who rules the zædtæ and dawdžytæ and without whom the
Ossetian system has no top; **Uastyrdzhi**, the patron of men, whose name women
may not say, so the tradition gives them a second one; **Uacilla** the thunderer,
under whose lightning being struck is a choosing; **Kurdalægon** the heavenly
smith, whom the corpus had already named inside Batraz's own domain label without
holding him; and **Safa** of the hearth chain, the most important domestic deity
of the Ossetians, from a household layer of which the corpus held nothing.

Safa and Kurdalægon are **identified with each other** in the sources. Both are
kept, linked by a reciprocated `equated-with`, because the hearth chain and the
forge are distinct cults and folding either into the other loses one of them.

================================================================================
BLOCK 2 — THE HERD, THE HUNT, THE WOLF, THE SICKNESS AND THE SUN
================================================================================

Six figures, and one of them explains another. **Fælværa** protects the sheep and
has one eye; **Tutyr** is the lord of the wolves, and it was Tutyr who struck out
Fælværa's left eye **so that wolves could come at the flock from the blind side**.
Holding either without the other leaves the shepherd-god's injury with no cause,
so both are authored, with a reciprocated `enemy` relation that is load-bearing
rather than decorative.

**Æfsati** is the hunt, and game is his cattle — Æfsatijy fos. **Alardy** is the
smallpox, a god you may not mourn in front of, placated with songs and bright
colours around a sick child. **Galægon** is the wind you pray for at winnowing,
a god with exactly one job and a real one. **Khur** is the sun, and he enters the
corpus only because his daughter marries into the Narts; nothing is claimed for
him beyond what the sources give, which is that Atsyrukhs is his daughter.

================================================================================
BLOCK 3 — THE DEAD, AND GOD'S THREE TEARS FOR BATRAZ
================================================================================

**Barastyr** rules the underworld and is judge and jailer to some of the dead and
a hospitable host to others; **Aminon** keeps its iron gates, which can only be
reached before sunset, and questions each arriving soul. The test is a
**confession, not a weighing**: a liar is beaten on the lips with a blood-dipped
broom and thrown in the river, and under a truthful speaker the log over that
river widens into a bridge. The corpus had no Ossetian underworld at all,
although the Soslan cycle it was missing turns on a journey into it.

Then three figures the corpus had already half-recorded. `ossetian_batraz` carries
the domain **"holy spring(s) arising from God's tears"** — God wept three tears
over Batraz's death. What the corpus did not hold is **what grew where they
fell**: **Rekom**, **Mykalgabyrtæ** and **Taranjelos**, the three great
sanctuaries of Ossetia, which the epic treats as **heavenly-dwellers** and not as
mere places. That is why they are authored as figures rather than as sites.

================================================================================
BLOCK 4 — THE SOSLAN CYCLE
================================================================================

**Soslan** is the largest absence in the Ossetian key: born from a stone, tempered
by Kurdalægon so that only his knees were left uncovered, married to the Sun's
daughter, and killed at last by a wheel aimed at exactly those knees. He is
authored **demigod**, matching the two records of the same hero the corpus already
holds under the Circassian and Karachay-Balkar keys, and with **empty parentIds** —
because the Ossetian tradition makes him stone-born of an unnamed shepherd, and
the corpus's own Satana record calls her his **foster**-mother, not his mother.
Rather than pick a side the tradition does not settle, the record states the split
and carries `fostered-by` instead of a parent.

**Atsyrukhs**, "true light", is the Daughter of the Sun; the bride-quest that wins
her runs through the Land of the Dead for the leaves of the aza tree, so the solar
marriage and the underworld journey turn out to be one story. **Bedukha** is the
first wife of the widower Soslan and one of the rulers of the Land of the Dead,
who tells him she watches over him from the other world. **Totradz** son of Ælbeg
is the man Soslan kills in the episode the sources single out as showing Soslan at
his worst.

**Syrdon** is the trickster, and the corpus did not hold him either. He is the son
of **Gætæg**, a water spirit who blackmailed Dzerassæ, and he is the reason the
Ossetian harp exists: the twelve-stringed fændyr is strung from the bones, hair
and veins of his own sons, after Khæmyts killed them. He contrives Soslan's death
and Soslan, from the grave, impales him.

Syrdon's **demigod** tier is computed, not asserted: Gætæg is typed `numen` (the
registry's category for a non-pantheon divine race, the same call the corpus
already makes for the Circassian water-sprite Lady Isp) and so contributes 1.0,
Dzerassæ contributes 0.5, and the registry averages them to 0.75.

================================================================================
BLOCK 5 — THE NART HOUSEHOLDS, THE FEUD, AND THE GROVE
================================================================================

**Atsamaz** plays a pipe given him by the gods to win **Agunda**, and breaks it
when she reads his refusal to hand it over as proof he does not love her. Agunda's
father is **Saynag-Aldar**, the owner of the black mountain — and that is where the
courtship tale meets the blood-feud, because Saynag-Aldar is the man who killed
Khæmyts, at the asking of **Burafærnyg** of the Borata, whose wife and seven
daughters-in-law Khæmyts had seduced.

**The corpus held both ends of that feud and neither of its killers.**
`ossetian_khaemyts` is recorded as murdered and `ossetian_batraz` as the avenger
who "brutally took revenge"; the man who did it, and the man who paid for it, were
both missing. The blow is also an etiology: it struck Khæmyts's tooth Arkyz, a chip
flew to the sky, and the moon has been there since.

**Hetag** closes the batch and is the only post-nart figure in it — a prince's son
who converted, fled his kinsmen, prayed to Uastyrdzhi, and had the forest close
around him. The Grove of Khetag is still one of the main Ossetian sacred sites.
The legend sits exactly on this tradition's seam: **a Christian convert saved by
the pagan patron of travellers, who is himself Saint George.**

VERIFICATION NOTES
================================================================================

  * Every figure carries at least one cited source on the top-level `sources`
    claim, on its lifecycle phase, and on every domain, relation and
    materialCulture entry.
  * Source references record the SUBSTANCE found rather than a bibliographic
    citation that was not read, following the house pattern; where a search result
    explicitly attributed a claim to a named work — Dumézil's 1948 Loki for the
    Syrdon/Loki identification, Dumézil on the iron wheel as a solar echo, the
    Encyclopaedia Iranica articles ŠOŠLAN, ŠỊRDON and UASTYRDŽI — that work is
    named.
  * Cyrillic script values are supplied ONLY where the research actually returned
    them (Хуыцау, рæхыс, ацы, рухс, Мыкалгабыртæ). Every other record's term value
    is left empty rather than reconstructed, and every unresolved etymology says
    "not resolved in the sources consulted" instead of guessing.
  * Three records carry parentIds, and each was checked against the registry's own
    tier arithmetic before authoring, so the tier-classification-drift test cannot
    fire: Atsyrukhs (Khur, deity 1.0, halved = 0.5 -> demigod), Syrdon (Gætæg
    numen 1.0 + Dzerassæ demigod 0.5, halved = 0.75 -> demigod), Agunda
    (Saynag-Aldar mortal 0.0 -> mortal). The other twenty-four author no
    parentIds at all.
  * Symmetric relations inside the batch are reciprocated (Safa/Kurdalægon
    equated-with, Fælværa/Tutyr enemy, Soslan/Atsyrukhs and Soslan/Bedukha spouse,
    Soslan/Syrdon enemy). Every link to a PRE-EXISTING record uses a
    NON-SYMMETRIC kind, written from this end only, so that no pre-existing record
    is mutated and no one-way symmetric relation is left dangling.
