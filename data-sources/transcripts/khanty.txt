Pantheon Registry — Khanty (Ostyak) Ob-Ugric pantheon, primordial core
========================================================================

Tradition: Khanty (the Ostyak of the middle and lower Ob, western Siberia).
Era key used: primordial (the single registered Khanty era; these are primordial cosmic deities).

Scope of this batch. The supreme sky-father (khanty_num_torum), a sky-son
(khanty_yli_torum) and the underworld lord (khanty_kul_otyr) already exist in the
corpus. This batch adds the two figures the brief identifies as the missing core —
the central Ob-Ugric intermediary, the World-Watching Man, and the birth/mother
goddess Kaltes — plus one firmly attested brother of the intermediary, the
"Fast / Golden Old Man" Postajankt-iki, so the family of Num-Torum is coherent.

A note on the Khanty/Mansi split. The Khanty (Ostyak) and Mansi (Vogul) share this
pantheon almost entirely, and the corpus already carries Mansi forms of the first
two figures (mansi_mir_susne_khum, mansi_kaltes_ekwa). The figures below are
authored under the Khanty label and cite Khanty/Ostyak attestation specifically:
the World-Watching Man is documented as one of the best-liked gods "among both the
Khanty and the Mansi," with the Northern (Kazym) Khanty name Mĭr šawijti xŭ
("the man who watches the world / surveys mankind"); Kaltes/Kaltaś is recorded in
Khanty as Kaltaś-aŋki and Kattaś-imi; and Postajankt-iki bears transparently Khanty
names (iki = "old man," sorńi = "gold"). Primary documentation is K. F. Karjalainen,
Die Religion der Jugra-Völker (FF Communications 41/44/63, 1921–1927), supplemented
by Uno Holmberg/Harva, Finno-Ugric [and] Siberian Mythology (Mythology of All Races
IV, 1927) and the modern synthesis V. Kulemzin, N. Lukina, T. Moldanov & T. Moldanova,
Khanty Mythology (Encyclopaedia of Uralic Mythologies 2; ed. Hoppál / Napolskikh /
Siikala, 2006). Where a motif is best recorded in the Mansi (Vogul) parallel the
citation says so (Munkácsi's Vogul corpus).

Not authored (verified-then-omitted, to avoid padding):
  • Sänke / Num-Sänke as a figure distinct from Num-Torum — not attested as a
    separate Khanty deity in the sources consulted; omitted.
  • Num-Torum's unnamed "one daughter" — variously identified (river-mistresses such
    as Kazym-imi, As-toy-imi) and not stably a single named primordial figure; omitted.
