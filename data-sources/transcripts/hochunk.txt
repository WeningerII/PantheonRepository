Ho-Chunk (Winnebago) core pantheon — research transcript.

Sources verified by web search against the standard scholarship: Paul Radin, *The Winnebago Tribe* (1923, Bureau of American Ethnology, 37th Annual Report, 1915–1916, pp. 35–550); Paul Radin, *The Trickster: A Study in American Indian Mythology* (1956); Paul Radin, *The Road of Life and Death: A Ritual Drama of the American Indians* (1945); Paul Radin, *Winnebago Hero Cycles: A Study in Aboriginal Literature* (1948); Paul Radin, *The Winnebago Myth of the Twins* (BAE notebooks); and Richard L. Dieterle's *Encyclopedia of Hočąk (Winnebago) Mythology* (hotcakencyclopedia.com), which the existing Red Horn corpus already cites and which compiles and cross-references Radin's Winnebago Notebooks at the American Philosophical Society.

The well-attested core that the existing corpus (which holds only the Red Horn cycle: `redhorn`, `redhorn_son`, `white_beaver_woman`) is missing is the creator Earthmaker and the series of saving "culture-hero" spirits he fashioned and sent down to make the world safe for humanity. In the canonical Hare-cycle series these spirits are, in order, Trickster (Wakdjąkaga), Bladder (Wadexúga), Turtle (Kecągega), Redhorn (already in the corpus), and Hare (Wašjįgega / Wacdjįgega), the fifth and last, who founded the Medicine Rite. To these I add Disease-Giver (Hošereʼųwahira), the dispenser of life and death attested across Radin's war-bundle material, and the two heroes of the Twin Cycle, Flesh (Warora) and Stump / Little Ghost (Wanaghi). The Winnebago themselves draw a clear distinction between Trickster and Hare, so they are kept as separate figures. Native-language spellings follow Radin/Dieterle; where a specific term is not securely attested for a given attribute it is omitted rather than invented. I omit the Thunderbirds (Wakąja) as a standalone figure because in the sources they are a collective clan-class of upper-world war spirits rather than a single named individual.
