RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISIONS
================================================================================

PARENT CLUSTER. The directed parent edge for every child is greek_eurytus_oechalia
(registered; type mortal, king of Oechalia and famed master-archer, teacher of
Heracles in the bow, killed with his sons at the sack of Oechalia). Eurytus's own
record states in prose: "Father of Iole and of sons (Iphitus, Clytius, Toxeus,
Deioneus, Molion) by Antiope daughter of Pylon," and adds that "His sons (Iphitus,
Clytius, Toxeus, Deioneus, Molion) and wife Antiope daughter of Pylon are named in
the tradition but have no corpus entries, so they are described here rather than
wired." Confirmed by grep that none of the five exists yet as a registered figure
(no id 'greek_iphitus' / 'greek_clytius' / 'greek_toxeus' / 'greek_deioneus' /
'greek_molion' nor any greek_*_<name> variant; the only corpus occurrences of these
names are prose mentions inside other figures' notes/power-lists — e.g. Eurytus's
own notes, greek_apollod_heracles's lover-relation to Omphale "as expiation for the
murder of Iphitus," and greek_odysseus's bow-item "Gift of Iphitus, once
Eurytus's"). parentRoles {greek_eurytus_oechalia:"father"}.

SECOND PARENT — ANTIOPE / ANTIOCHE. The mother of the sons is Antiope, daughter of
Pylon (son of Naubolus) in the dominant Apollodoran-onomastic tradition; Hesiod's
Catalogue calls Eurytus's wife Antioche. There is NO registered figure for her: the
several "Antiope" entries in the corpus are wholly distinct women (Antiope of Thebes
daughter of Nycteus, mother of Amphion and Zethus; and Antiope the Amazon), none of
them the wife of Eurytus. She is therefore an UNREGISTERED second parent. Per the
corpus convention already used for the children of Minos × (unregistered) Pasiphae —
where each child's parentIds list only the registered parent and the mother is
carried as an externalRef relation and in notes — Antiope/Antioche is NOT added to
parentIds. She is given as an externalRef "mother" relation on each son and named in
each son's lifecycle and notes.

TYPE BY DESCENT (anti-drift rule). Eurytus = mortal (divinity fraction 0). The
second parent Antiope/Antioche is unregistered and is counted as mortal (0), with
the sum still divided by 2: (0 + 0) / 2 = 0. This lands below the scion band, i.e.
mortal. Independently, the brief flags these explicitly as "mortal heroes," and the
"author tribal/dynastic founders and historical humans as mortal" carve-out, plus
the LOWER-tier-when-unsure rule, all converge on the same result. All five sons are
accordingly authored as type "mortal" — matching their registered father Eurytus and
their registered sister greek_apollod_iole (both "mortal"). (Mythic note, recorded
but NOT used to inflate tier: Eurytus is reckoned a grandson of Apollo through his
father Melaneus in the Thessalian tradition, but the corpus already fixes Eurytus as
mortal, so descent from him yields mortal.)

SIBLINGS. The sons of Eurytus across the sources are Iphitus, Clytius, Toxeus,
Deioneus (Deion), and Molion (with Didaeon and Hippasus added in some late lists, and
the daughter Iole). The source traditions divide the set: Apollonius Rhodius's
Argonaut-catalogue and Hyginus pair Clytius and Iphitus; Diodorus Siculus 4.37.5
names the slain sons as Toxeus, Molion, and Clytius; the Hesiodic Catalogue (via the
Scholiast on Sophocles, Trachiniae 354) names Deion, Clytius, Toxeus, and Iphitus
(omitting Molion). Apollodorus 2.7.7 has Heracles slay "Eurytus and his sons"
collectively at the sack of Oechalia. The five authored sons are wired as siblings to
one another RECIPROCALLY within this batch; each also carries a ONE-WAY sibling
relation to the already-registered sister greek_apollod_iole (the brief's rule for an
already-registered sibling — Iole's own entry is not edited here). Didaeon and Hippasus
(late-list brothers) are given as externalRef sibling notes only, not as resolvable
personIds.

PER-FIGURE NOTES.

  • IPHITUS (greek_iphitus) — the famous son: an Argonaut (Apollonius Rhodius,
    Argonautica 1.86-89, with his brother Clytius; Hyginus, Fabulae 14), and above all
    the guest-friend of both Odysseus and Heracles. Seeking his father's strayed mares
    (twelve mares and mule-foals), he met young Odysseus in Messene at the house of
    Ortilochus and gave him the great bow of Eurytus, receiving a sword and spear — the
    very bow of the Odyssey 21 suitor-contest (Hom. Od. 21.13-41). Believing Heracles
    innocent of the theft of the mares, he came as his guest to Tiryns; Heracles, in a
    fit of madness (or treacherously), hurled him down from the walls/towers of Tiryns
    and killed him (Apollod. 2.6.1-2; Diod. Sic. 4.31.3). For this murder of a
    guest-friend Heracles was struck with disease and, purified, sold into servitude to
    Omphale queen of Lydia (Apollod. 2.6.2-3). He DIES within the heroic-age, so the
    lifecycle carries a death stage (era heroic-age) and vitalStatus is "dead"; death
    block records the hurling from Tiryns. Distinct from Iphitus son of Naubolus, the
    Phocian Argonaut and father of Schedius and Epistrophus (Il. 2.518) — disambiguated
    in notes.

  • CLYTIUS (greek_clytius) — son of Eurytus, brother of Iphitus and an Argonaut in the
    same catalogue (Apollonius Rhodius, Argonautica 1.86-89; Hyginus, Fabulae 14). He
    was killed by Heracles in the war against Eurytus per Diodorus Siculus (4.37.5,
    "Toxeus, Molion, and Clytius"); a variant in a possibly-corrupt passage of Hyginus
    (Fab. 14) has him slain by Aeetes on the Argonaut voyage. DIES at the sack of
    Oechalia (dominant tradition) → lifecycle death stage, vitalStatus "dead." Distinct
    from the many other Greek figures named Clytius (the Giant; the Trojan elder son of
    Laomedon; et al.) — disambiguated in notes.

  • TOXEUS (greek_toxeus) — son of Eurytus, named first among the slain sons by
    Diodorus Siculus 4.37.5 ("Toxeus, Molion, and Clytius") and in the Hesiodic
    Catalogue list (Sch. Soph. Trach. 354). His name ("bowman," from τόξον) marks the
    archer-house of Eurytus. Killed by Heracles at the sack of Oechalia → lifecycle
    death stage, vitalStatus "dead." Distinct from Toxeus son of Oeneus (whom his father
    Oeneus killed for leaping the ditch at Calydon) — disambiguated in notes.

  • DEIONEUS (greek_deioneus; "Deion") — son of Eurytus, attested in the Hesiodic
    Catalogue list of the sons (Sch. Soph. Trach. 354, citing Hesiod's Ehoiai and the
    Capture of Oechalia). A distinct tradition in Plutarch (Theseus 8.3) has Theseus
    give Perigune, daughter of Sinis (and mother of Theseus's son Melanippus), in
    marriage to "Deïoneus son of Eurytus the Oechalian" — the only individuated myth for
    this son. He falls with his brothers when Heracles sacks Oechalia (Apollod. 2.7.7,
    "Eurytus and his sons") → lifecycle death stage, vitalStatus "dead"; the Perigune
    marriage is recorded as an externalRef spouse-relation and in notes (Perigune is
    unregistered). Distinct from Deioneus/Deion son of Aeolus (father of Cephalus) —
    disambiguated in notes.

  • MOLION (greek_molion) — son of Eurytus, named among the slain sons by Diodorus
    Siculus 4.37.5 ("Toxeus, Molion, and Clytius") and a brother of Clytius and Toxeus;
    omitted from the Hesiodic four-son list, included in the late full lists. Killed by
    Heracles at the sack of Oechalia → lifecycle death stage, vitalStatus "dead."
    Distinct from the Molione/Moliones (Eurytus and Cteatus, the conjoined twin sons of
    Actor/Poseidon and Molione) — disambiguated in notes.

PRIMARY/SECONDARY SCHOLARSHIP USED (real, non-fabricated):
  - Homer, Odyssey 21.13-41 (Iphitus gives Odysseus the bow of Eurytus, met at
    Ortilochus's house in Messene; Iphitus killed by Heracles in violation of guest-law),
    8.224-228 (Eurytus the archer); Iliad 2.518, 2.596 (the distinct Iphiti).
  - Apollodorus, Bibliotheca (Library) 2.4.9 (Eurytus teaches Heracles the bow), 2.6.1-3
    (archery contest for Iole; theft of the cattle/mares; Iphitus thrown from the walls of
    Tiryns; Heracles's disease and servitude to Omphale), 2.7.7 (Heracles sacks Oechalia,
    slays Eurytus and his sons, carries off Iole).
  - Diodorus Siculus, Library of History 4.31.3 (Iphitus thrown from the tower of Tiryns),
    4.37.4-5 (the sons Toxeus, Molion, and Clytius slain at Oechalia; Iole taken).
  - Apollonius Rhodius, Argonautica 1.86-89 (Clytius and Iphitus, "warders of Oechalia,
    sons of Eurytus the ruthless," in the Argonaut-catalogue; the bow of Apollo motif).
  - Hyginus, Fabulae 14 (Argonaut-catalogue, Clytius and Iphitus sons of Eurytus); the
    Aeetes-variant for Clytius.
  - Plutarch, Theseus 8.3 (Perigune, daughter of Sinis, given by Theseus in marriage to
    Deioneus son of Eurytus; the Ioxid asparagus-custom).
  - Sophocles, Trachiniae 260-285 (the contest and the wrong done to Heracles), 351-365,
    476-478 (the sack of Oechalia and the capture of Iole); Scholia to Trachiniae 354
    (the Hesiodic Catalogue / Capture of Oechalia list of Eurytus's sons: Deion, Clytius,
    Toxeus, Iphitus).
  - Hesiod, Catalogue of Women (Ehoiai), frr. on the Oechalian house (preserved via the
    Trachiniae scholia), naming Eurytus's wife Antioche and his sons.
  - Timothy Gantz, Early Greek Myth: A Guide to Literary and Artistic Sources (Johns
    Hopkins Univ. Press, 1993), pp. 434-437 (Heracles, Eurytus, Iphitus and the sack of
    Oechalia).
  - William Smith (ed.), A Dictionary of Greek and Roman Biography and Mythology (1849),
    s.vv. "Eurytus," "Iphitus," "Clytius," "Toxeus," "Deioneus," "Molion."
