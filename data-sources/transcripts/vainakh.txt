Figure-authoring batch — the Vainakh (Chechen–Ingush / Nakh) divine core still missing.

The corpus already holds the head of the pantheon and the best-known figures around
him: vainakh_dela (Dela/Diela, the supreme creator-god), vainakh_seela (Seela/Sela,
god of thunder, lightning, the stars and the sun), vainakh_maetsill (Maetsill, the
harvest-god and protector of the weak), vainakh_tusholi (Tusholi, the spring-and-
fertility goddess, daughter of Dela), and vainakh_pkharmat (Pkharmat, the fire-stealing
Nart smith). What is still missing is the rest of the well-attested functional core of
the Vainakh divine family and the elemental "mother" goddesses of the high Caucasus.
This batch adds six firmly attested figures:

  • ELTA (Эльта) — god of the hunt and the wild animals, and (before Maetsill assumed
    the office) of the grain-harvest; a son of the supreme god Dela, blinded in one eye
    by his father for disobedience. As a child of Dela he is the brother of the existing
    Tusholi.
  • SELA SATA (Села Сата) — goddess of crafts and especially women's handiwork
    (spinning, weaving, embroidery), the daughter (in other versions the wife) of the
    thunder-god Sela, the Vainakh counterpart of the Northwest-Caucasian Nart matriarch
    Satanaya; she guides the Nart Pkharmat to the fire on the mountain. She is wired to
    the existing vainakh_seela (parent) and vainakh_pkharmat (companion in the fire-theft).
  • MOLYZ-YERDY (Молыз-Ерды) — the war-god "who brought the Vainakh victory"; his name
    carries the -yerdy / -erda element (the standard theonymic suffix of Vainakh patron-
    deities and of the stone sanctuaries, e.g. Tkhaba-Yerdy, dedicated to them).
  • ISHTAR-DELA (Иштар-Дела) — also Eshtar; lord of life and death and ruler of the
    subterranean world Deeli-Malkhi, the realm of the dead, where he is responsible for
    punishing the wicked. (Distinct from the supreme Dela despite the shared del- element;
    this is the figure behind the prompt's "Mater-Dela / Dika-Dela" slot — the attested
    death-lord of the Vainakh, not a separate "good/mother Dela.")
  • DARTSA-NANA (Дарца-нана, "Blizzard-mother") — goddess of blizzards and avalanches,
    who dwells on the snowy summit of Mount Kazbek (Bashlam), around which she has traced
    a magic circle no mortal of sense dare cross.
  • MOKHA-NANA (Мох-нана, "Wind-mother") — goddess of the winds, one of the elemental
    "-nana" (mother) deities of the Vainakh natural world.

Candidates from the brief that are NOT entered, to avoid padding or invention:
"Mehkan-Nana" ("Mother of the Country/land") is not separately attested in the
accessible literature — the attested forms are the "father of the country" title
Mehkan-Da and the earth-mother Latta-Nana, not a distinct tutelary "Mehkan-Nana," so
it is skipped. "Una-Nana" (a disease/plague mother) is not attested in the consulted
sources and is omitted. "Galer-Erda" / "Khagia-Yerda" are named only as bare entries in
deity-lists (local patron-erdy) with no individuated function, family, cult-item or
narrative recoverable from the accessible sources, so they are not authored. "Mater-Dela
/ Dika-Dela" is not a securely distinct figure; the death-lord role it gestures at is
covered by the attested Ishtar-Dela above. The other elemental "-nana" mothers
(Latta-Nana earth, Khi-Nana water) and the further bare -erdy entries (Hal-Erda sky,
Mago-Erda magic/wisdom, etc.) are real list-entries but, like Galer-Erda, lack the
enrichable detail to author responsibly here; the two -nana mothers entered (Dartsa-Nana,
Mokha-Nana) are the ones for which the sources preserve concrete description.

Sources are the standard reference literature on Vainakh (Chechen–Ingush) religion,
all verified to exist as real works: Amjad Jaimoukha, *The Chechens: A Handbook*
(RoutledgeCurzon, 2005), whose religion/mythology chapter reconstructs the pre-Islamic
pantheon and gives the list of Vainakh deities; Mariel Tsaroïeva, *Anciennes croyances
des Ingouches et des Tchétchènes* (Paris: Maisonneuve & Larose, 2005), the monograph by
an Ingush historian of religion that underlies the standard inventory of the gods;
David Hunt, *Legends of the Caucasus* (Saqi Books, 2012), for the Dartsa-Nana / Kazbek
material and the fire-theft; and B. K. Dalgat, *Pervobytnaya religiya chechentsev i
ingushey* [The Primitive Religion of the Chechens and Ingush] (the early-20th-century
ethnography, Moscow: Nauka ed. 2004), already cited in the corpus. Tertiary syntheses
(the Vainakh-religion survey on Wikipedia and the batsav.com pantheon page, which
explicitly draw on Tsaroïeva 2005 and Jaimoukha 2005) are cited where they carry the
specific wording.

The Vainakh divine world is headed by Dela, beneath whom stand functional gods (Sela
of the storm; Elta of the hunt; Maetsill, formerly Elta, of the harvest; Molyz-Yerdy of
war) and the elemental "mothers" (-nana) and patron "saints" (-erdy) of the landscape,
with Ishtar-Dela ruling the underworld Deeli-Malkhi. Theonyms are given in Cyrillic with
romanization. Etymological notes: Vainakh nana 'mother' (Dartsa-nana 'blizzard-mother',
Mokha/Mokh-nana 'wind-mother'); yerdy / erda, the suffix of the patron-deities and their
stone shrines (elgyts); del- 'god' in Dela and Ishtar-Dela.

Elta (son of Dela) is the brother of the existing vainakh_tusholi (also Dela's child):
a symmetric sibling tie toward an existing corpus figure, flagged RECIPROCAL-NEEDED.
Sela Sata is the daughter of the existing vainakh_seela (parentage encoded on her
parentIds) and the companion of the existing vainakh_pkharmat in the fire-theft — a
symmetric companion tie toward an existing corpus figure, flagged RECIPROCAL-NEEDED.
Molyz-Yerdy, Ishtar-Dela, Dartsa-Nana and Mokha-Nana have no individuated kin, spouse or
parentage in the sources and are flagged SOLITARY with cited reasons.
