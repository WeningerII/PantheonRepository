================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added three genuinely individuated, named Lenape beings absent from the existing set: Maxa'xâk the Great Horned Serpent (a malevolent underwater manitou), Kichichaxkal the Great Toad or 'Water Keeper' (the Creator-appointed ruler of the waters), and Snow Boy (the fatherless spirit of snow and winter). The serpent and toad are wired to each other as reciprocal enemies over dominion of the waters, and the toad is bound as a subordinate to the existing Creator (Kishelemukong); Snow Boy stands alone as documented. I deliberately excluded Nanapush, which even the recorded modern creation account borrows but which scholarship (native-languages.org, following the Walam Olum critique) identifies as an Anishinaabe culture-hero wrongly transplanted into Lenape tradition, and Rainbow Crow / Mànàka'has, whose Lenape authenticity is denied by the Lenape-Nanticoke Museum as a post-1989 reworking of a Cherokee tale; the earth-bearing Great Turtle was judged a cosmological element rather than an individuated deity. Citations rest on Harrington (1921), Speck (1931), Bierhorst (1995), Kraft (2001), and the Delaware Tribe's recorded creation narrative told by Chief Robert Red Hawk Ruth.
