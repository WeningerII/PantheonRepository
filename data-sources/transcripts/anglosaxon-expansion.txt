================================================================================
THE GAP — THE REGISTRY HELD BEOWULF AND NOT GRENDEL
================================================================================

A 91-name check across the WHOLE registry returned 25 hits and 66 absences.

The corpus held **Beowulf**. It held his father **Ecgtheow**, his king
**Hygelac**, Hygelac's queen **Hygd**, Hygelac's father **Hrethel**, Hrethel's
three sons **Herebeald**, **Haethcyn** and Hygelac, Hrethel's daughter,
Hygelac's son **Heardred**, and the two men who are with Beowulf at the end,
**Wiglaf** and **Weohstan**. Eleven records: **the whole Geatish royal house.**

**It did not hold Grendel.**

Nor Grendel's mother. Nor the dragon. The poem is in three parts and each part
is a fight with one of them; the registry held the man who fights and not one
of the three things he fights.

And it did not hold **any Dane at all**:

- no **Hrothgar**, whose hall the poem is about
- no **Wealhtheow**, his queen
- no **Unferth**, who challenges Beowulf at the feast
- no **Æschere**, whose death sends Beowulf into the mere
- no **Scyld Scefing**, who arrives as a child alone in a boat and is sent back
  out to sea in one, in the passage the poem opens with
- no **Freawaru**, **Hrothulf**, **Heorogar**, **Halga**, **Beow** or
  **Healfdene**

The corpus held one side of a poem about two courts.

It also held, in the Swedish half of the story, **Weohstan** — and not
**Eanmund**, the man Weohstan kills, whose sword Weohstan takes and whose sword
is the one Wiglaf carries into the dragon fight. The registry held the sword's
second owner and not its first.

================================================================================
AND THREE GODS BEDE, ASSER AND A FIELD-CHARM ATTEST
================================================================================

The corpus held Woden, Thunor, Tiw, Frig, Eostre and Seaxneat. It did not hold:

- **Hretha**, whom Bede names in the SAME SENTENCE-PATTERN as Eostre — the month
  Hrēþmōnaþ "is named for their goddess Hretha, to whom they sacrificed at this
  time." Bede gives two goddesses and the registry took one.
- **Erce**, called on three times in the Æcerbot field-charm — *Erce, Erce,
  Erce, eorþan modor*, "Erce, mother of earth."
- **Ing**, whom the Old English Rune Poem says was first seen among the East
  Danes and went east over the wave with his wagon after him.
- **Geat**, of whom Asser, King Alfred's biographer, says the pagans long
  worshipped him as a god.

================================================================================
A CROSS-LANE JOIN
================================================================================

Lane 19 ingested **Vortigern** under the Welsh key. This batch ingests
**Hengist** and **Horsa**, the men he invited, and draws the edge. The corpus
can now state the event from both ends.

================================================================================
TWO MEN NAMED HENGEST, AND THIS BATCH ASSERTS NOTHING ABOUT THEM
================================================================================

**Hengest** appears in the Finnsburg episode of *Beowulf* and in the *Finnsburg
Fragment* as a Danish retainer who takes a winter's truce with the Frisians and
breaks it in the spring. **Hengist** appears in Bede, the *Historia Brittonum*
and the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle* as the Jutish leader who lands in Kent.

Tolkien argued at length that they are one man. Others do not. **The two are
ingested as two records under qualified ids**, each built only from its own
sources; **neither asserts they are the same man and neither asserts they are
not.** That is lane 19's handling of the two Myrddins, arrived at from the
opposite direction: there the tradition distinguished two figures and later
scholarship merged them; here the sources are simply separate and later
scholarship proposes the merger.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BATCH DOES NOT INGEST
================================================================================

Heorot, Finnsburg, Ravenswood and the barrow are **places**. The Scyldings, the
Geats, the Heathobards, the Wægmundings and the Heodenings are **peoples**.
Nægling, Hrunting and the giant sword are **objects**. Bede, Asser, Alcuin,
Ælfric and Cædmon are **historical writers**, not figures of the tradition —
the restraint lane 20 applied to Elias Lönnrot.

**Wyrd is BLOCKED and not ingested.** The word is fate and it is grammatically
feminine and its Norse cognate *urðr* is the name of a Norn; whether there is a
*figure* under the Old English word is exactly what the scholarship disagrees
about, and a registry that ingested her would be taking a side. The corpus
already holds `norse_urdr` and the sweep resolves "Wyrd" onto it, which is a
fact about the cognate and not about the English goddess.
