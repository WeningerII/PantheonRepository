================================================================================
THE GAP — THE CORPUS HELD SANDALPHON AND NOT GABRIEL
================================================================================

The rabbinic–kabbalistic key held **twelve records**:

    Metatron, Sandalphon, Raziel      three angels
    Samael, Lilith, Naamah,           five demons
    Agrat bat Mahlat, Ashmedai
    Behemoth, Leviathan, Ziz          the three great beasts
    Adam Kadmon                       the primordial anthropos

**It had no Michael, no Gabriel, no Raphael and no Uriel.**

Those four are, by an enormous margin, the most attested angelic figures in the
whole tradition — the four chief angels the Midrash sets around the divine
throne. The corpus held Metatron and Sandalphon, who are esoteric, and not the
four a reader would look up first.

It gets sharper. The corpus held **Samael** — and the midrashic Michael exists
largely to be set *against* Samael, as Israel's heavenly defender against its
accuser. **The registry held the accuser and not the defender.**

And it held three of the four demon queens. Lilith, Naamah and Agrat bat Mahlat
are all records; **Eisheth Zenunim, the fourth, is not.** A set held incomplete,
with nothing in the data to mark that anything was missing.

================================================================================
THREE OF THE FOUR ARCHANGELS WERE FINDABLE BY NAME — UNDER SOMEBODY ELSE'S KEY
================================================================================

The corpus-wide NFKD sweep over eighty-six candidate names returned this:

    Michael   ->  beta_israel_mikael      [Beta Israel]
    Gabriel   ->  beta_israel_gabriel     [Beta Israel]
    Raphael   ->  beta_israel_rafael      [Beta Israel]
    Suriel    ->  beta_israel_suriel      [Beta Israel]
    Rahab     ->  yahwism_rahab           [Ancient Israelite religion / Yahwism]

**This is the fourth sweep failure mode, found in lane 34 and now confirmed in a
second tradition in a row.** The name resolves; nothing looks missing; and the
figure under the key you are actually working in has no record. A search for the
rabbinic Michael answered with the Ethiopian Beta Israel Mika'el — a correct
record of a real figure, and the wrong answer to the question.

**Uriel did not resolve at all.** He is the control case: the one of the four
chief angels with no cross-tradition record to hide behind, and the only one
whose absence a name search would actually have surfaced.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS LANE REFUSED TO INGEST, AND WHY IT MATTERS MOST HERE
================================================================================

Searching for the kabbalistic angelic hierarchy returns, over and over, a tidy
table: Metatron–Kether, Raziel–Chokmah, Tzaphkiel–Binah, Tzadkiel–Chesed,
Kamael–Geburah, Raphael–Tiferet, Haniel–Netzach, Michael–Hod, Gabriel–Yesod,
Sandalphon–Malkuth. It is clean, it is everywhere, and it would have yielded five
more records — Tzaphkiel, Tzadkiel, Kamael, Haniel and Jophiel — for no work.

**That table is Hermetic Qabalah**: a Western occult system of the Renaissance
and after, not rabbinic and not classical kabbalah. The source that produces it
says so itself. Ingesting those five under a key labelled *rabbinic–kabbalistic*
would be exactly the "unsupported modern inventions" this programme's founding
rules bar, dressed up as scholarship because it arrives in a table.

**So they are censused REJECT.** One of the five, **Yofiel**, IS authored — but
on a completely different basis: Hekhalot literature names Yofiel among the
Princes of the Presence, which is a rabbinic attestation. The record says so
explicitly, so that nobody later mistakes his presence for an endorsement of the
table.

================================================================================
BLOCK 1 — THE FOUR CHIEF ANGELS
================================================================================

**Michael**, prince of chesed and water, defender of Israel against Samael.
**Gabriel**, prince of gevurah and fire. The midrash makes them a matched pair and
sends each on the errands his half of the divine temperament requires; the corpus
held neither. **Raphael**, over the spirits of men, the healer, the third of the
three who visited Abraham. **Uriel**, over the world and over Tartarus.

================================================================================
BLOCK 2 — THE REST OF THE SEVEN OF 1 ENOCH 20
================================================================================

1 Enoch 20 names seven holy angels who watch and gives each a jurisdiction.
Michael, Gabriel, Raphael and Uriel are in block 1; the other three are
**Raguel**, who takes vengeance on the world of the luminaries, **Saraqael**, set
over the spirits who sin in the spirit, and **Remiel**, set over those who rise.
**Sariel** is named in the same list and — alone among the seven — is given no
sphere in the text the research returned, so **none is supplied for him here**.

The seven are a division of labour: the sky, the incorporeal, the resurrection,
the underworld, mankind, Paradise, the spirits of men. The corpus held none of it.

================================================================================
BLOCK 3 — THE PRINCES OF THE PRESENCE AND THE DOORS MOSES HAD TO GET PAST
================================================================================

**Akatriel** is the numinous entity a named rabbi says he met in the Temple —
first-person testimony, which is a different order of evidence from a name in a
list, and the reason he is authored while thinner names from the same passage are
blocked. **Anafiel** keeps the divine crown, guards the entrance to the chariot,
and **shares with Metatron** the office of shielding the other angels from the
divine presence: the corpus held one half of a two-man job. **Yofiel** is here on
his Hekhalot attestation alone.

Then the ascent. The midrash builds Moses' climb as a series of refusals:
**Kemuel** the porter is the first door, and Moses strikes him; **Hadarniel**
guards the second. In the last heaven stand **Af** ("Anger") and **Hemah**
("Wrath"), five hundred parasangs each, forged of black fire and red fire —
emotions given a height and a body. They are never named apart, and both are
authored, because authoring one would have made a pair into a singleton.

================================================================================
BLOCK 4 — THE WORKING ANGELS AND THE PRESENCE
================================================================================

**Lailah** is the angel of conception, whose name is "Night": she carries the drop
up and *asks* what will become of it. **Ridya** is the angel of rain, who appears
as a three-year-old calf with cleft lips, reported in the first person by a
Talmudic traveller. **Dumah** is the angel of silence who receives the soul when
it leaves the body — **the corpus had no Jewish afterlife at all**, twelve records
and not one that held the dead.

**Shekhinah** closes the block: the indwelling Presence, genderless in early
rabbinic usage and God's Bride in the Zohar, the tenth emanation, who declares she
will leave her spouse and go into exile with her children. She is the one figure
in this key whose story is a separation from God rather than a service to Him.

A note on who was here already: of the twelve pre-existing records, **three are
female and all three are demon queens**. This batch adds three more women —
Lailah, Shekhinah and Eisheth — and only one of them is a demon.

================================================================================
BLOCK 5 — THE ADVERSARIES, THE SEA, THE NOONDAY AND THE GIANT
================================================================================

**Ha-Satan** is authored as one record carrying three identities, on the
tradition's own authority: Reish Lakish says Satan, the yetzer hara and the Angel
of Death are one. The Angel of Death is therefore an **alias**, not a second
record — and **Azrael**, the name popular sources attach to that office, is
censused BLOCKED, because the research found he appears in neither the Hebrew
Bible nor the Talmud nor the Midrashim.

**Azazel** and **Shemhazai** are the two leaders of the fallen, and the Talmud
pairs them to contrast them: Shemhazai hangs himself upside down between heaven
and earth in perpetual atonement, and Azael refuses and is bound in the desert as
the source of sorcery. Holding one without the other destroys the contrast the
story exists for. **Mastema** asks God for his host of demons and is given it.

**Rahab, minister of the sea** is the Talmud's development of the name: commanded
at the creation to swallow the waters, he refused, and God killed him. He carries
a **qualified primary name** — not the bare "Rahab" — because the corpus already
holds `yahwism_rahab`, and a needless collision is a defect this programme has
spent four lanes repairing.

**Ketev Meriri**, the noonday demon, is a named individual with a folio, a
description and a season, not a class. **Og of Bashan** is the one mortal-bodied
figure here, measured by his own bedstead and then, the later rabbis argue, by his
own cubits. **Eisheth Zenunim** is the fourth demon queen.
