REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added three genuinely individuated figures beyond the existing five. Uhlakanyana is the archetypal Zulu trickster hero, extensively documented in Callaway's Nursery Tales (1868), Werner (1933), and Scheub (2000); he stands alone with no attested tie to the creator gods. Unwabu (the chameleon) and Intulo (the lizard) are the two named divine messengers of the well-attested origin-of-death myth recorded in Callaway's Religious System of the Amazulu (1868–70), analysed comparatively by Abrahamsson (1951) and Werner (1933); both are wired to the existing Unkulunkulu as his messengers and to each other as rivals in the race that fixed human mortality. I treated them as discrete named mythic actors rather than generic animal-classes because each is a specific narrative agent in a canonical episode. I deliberately excluded non-individuated spirit-classes (tikoloshe familiars, impundulu lightning-bird, generic amadlozi ancestors) as outside the individuated scope.
