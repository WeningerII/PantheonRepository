Ainu kamuy pantheon — figure-authoring transcript
==================================================

Tradition: Ainu (Hokkaido / Sakhalin / Kuril "Ainu Mosir"). Registered era keys: primordial, legendary.

Research scope
--------------
The Pantheon Registry already holds five Ainu figures: Kamuy Fuchi the hearth-fire goddess
(ainu_kamuy_fuchi), Kim-un Kamuy the bear/mountain god (ainu_kim_un_kamuy), Kotan-kar-kamuy
the land/village creator (ainu_kotan_kar_kamuy), Okikurmi the culture-hero (ainu_okikurmi), and
Repun Kamuy the orca/sea god (ainu_repun_kamuy). The task was to add any further well-attested,
INDIVIDUATED core kamuy still missing — and to enrich each with domains, faculties (powers) and
materialCulture (objects) as fully as the sources attest.

The Ainu kamuy system is documented in three foundational ethnographies — John Batchelor, The
Ainu and Their Folk-Lore (1901) and his An Ainu–English–Japanese Dictionary (1905); N. G. Munro,
Ainu Creed and Cult (1962); and Carl Etter, Ainu Folklore (1949) — and compiled with named,
per-deity entries in Michael Ashkenazi, Handbook of Japanese Mythology (Oxford, 2003), the
standard reference that the modern encyclopaedic accounts of these kamuy derive from. Donald
Philippi, Songs of Gods, Songs of Humans (1979) translates the kamuy-yukar (god-epics) in which
several of these deities speak in the first person.

AUTHORED (six firmly-attested, individuated kamuy)
  * CHUP KAMUY — the luminary deity of sun and moon. Ainu "chup" = luminary; tokap-chup ("day
    luminary") = the sun, kunne-chup ("black/night luminary") = the moon (Batchelor, Dictionary,
    1905). The widely-recorded aetiological myth has the moon-deity, unable to bear the sight of
    human misconduct by night, beg her sibling to exchange places, so that she became the sun.
    Distinct from every existing figure → AUTHORED as ainu_chup_kamuy.
  * WAKKA-US KAMUY — goddess of fresh water (wakka "water" + us "possessing" + kamuy), also
    Pet-ru-ush-mat / Petorush-mat ("watering-place woman"). Ashkenazi, Handbook, p. 283. In the
    famine-feast kamuy-yukar she is petitioned by Okikurmi and, by dancing and singing, gathers
    the kamuy of the rapids, fish, game, the hunt-goddess Hasinaw-uk and the land-overseer
    Kotan-kar-kamuy to win back the withheld salmon and deer → AUTHORED as ainu_wakka_us_kamuy,
    with relations to ainu_okikurmi, ainu_kotan_kar_kamuy and ainu_hash_inau_uk_kamuy.
  * HASH-INAU-UK KAMUY — goddess of the hunt (the kamuy "who holds the hunting-inau"); also
    Isosange-mat ("game-bringing-down woman") and Kamuy Paseguru ("potent kamuy"). Born from the
    fire-drill and named a sister of Kamuy Fuchi the hearth-goddess (and of Shiramba Kamuy, the
    vegetation god). Guides hunters to game and mediates with the masters of the animals →
    AUTHORED as ainu_hash_inau_uk_kamuy, sibling of ainu_kamuy_fuchi.
  * YUSHKEP KAMUY — the spider goddess (yushkep "spider"); also Ashketanne-mat ("long-fingered
    woman"). Ashkenazi, Handbook, p. 294. Tutelary familiar of female shamans and helper at
    childbirth, her long fingers drawing out the infant; in her kamuy-yukar she escapes the
    demon-suitor Poronitne Kamuy by turning into a reed and setting her servant-spirits on him
    → AUTHORED as ainu_yushkep_kamuy.
  * KANNA KAMUY — the thunder/storm god (kanna "thunder; that which is above"), recorded in
    flying-dragon form with the lesser storm-kamuy as his children. In the most common account
    he accompanies Kamuy Fuchi in her descent from heaven and is named among the parents of the
    fire-goddess; the existing corpus figure ainu_okikurmi already records the culture-hero as
    "born of thunder-god Kanna-kamuy and the elm spirit" → AUTHORED as ainu_kanna_kamuy, parent
    of ainu_okikurmi (RECIPROCAL-NEEDED) with a cited tie to ainu_kamuy_fuchi.
  * PAUCHI KAMUY — the demon of madness (pawci/pauchi), born of the Willow-Soul River in Pikun
    Kando (the high heaven) and descending to afflict humans with insanity, food-poisoning,
    seizures and frenzied dancing. A specific, named, individuated malevolent kamuy (not the
    generic nitne-kamuy spirit-class) → AUTHORED as ainu_pauchi_kamuy.

VERIFIED-AND-REJECTED
  * AEOINA KAMUY / AINURAKKUR — NOT authored: these are not distinct from the existing
    ainu_okikurmi, which already carries "Ae-oyna-kamuy", "Aeoina Kamuy" and "Ainurakkur" among
    its alt-names. Confirmed by inspecting the corpus entry directly.
  * NITNE KAMUY — NOT authored: a spirit-CLASS (the "evil kamuy") with no single named
    individual; the madness-demon Pauchi was authored in its place per the task's "or" clause.
