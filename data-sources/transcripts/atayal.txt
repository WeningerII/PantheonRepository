The word names at once the soul of a living person, the spirit of a dead one, the collective ancestral spirits (utux bnkis), and the highest supernatural power, sometimes distinguished as Utux Kayal, the spirit of the sky. The utux are not depicted in image and are rarely individualized; they are experienced as a watching, judging presence that upholds gaga, the interwoven code of custom, taboo and ritual by which Atayal groups live. Prosperity, illness, success in the hunt and the fate of the soul after death are all referred to the pleasure or anger of the utux, who receive the dead at the rainbow bridge and admit the worthy to the ancestral land. Offerings, expiatory rites and the reading of omens keep the living in right relation with them."

  "notes": "The siliq, a small mountain bird identified with the grey-cheeked fulvetta, is the great oracle of Atayal life. Its call and the manner of its flight are read as direct communication from the utux: a clear, favourable sign opens the way for a hunt, a journey, a betrothal or a war-party, while an adverse cry or a crossing flight halts the enterprise and sends the people home. So binding is its authority that no serious undertaking was begun without consulting it, and the reading of the siliq stood beside the keeping of gaga as a daily discipline linking the Atayal to the ancestral spirits. Bird augury of this kind is shared across the Formosan peoples, but the siliq holds a particularly central place in Atayal practice."
