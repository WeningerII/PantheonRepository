REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added four individuated, well-attested figures: Ga-gorib, the pit-monster 'thrower down' slain by Heitsi-eibib (Bleek 1864; Hahn 1881; Lynch 2004); ǁKhâb the Moon and the Hare of the origin-of-death myth, the most heavily documented Khoekhoe narrative (Bleek 1864; Hahn 1881; Schapera 1930; Harley 1885; Hartmann & Sukdaven, HTS 2024), wired to each other as reciprocal enemies; and the ǀKhunuseti, the Pleiades 'Stars of Spring', attached as daughters of the existing Tsui-ǁgoab (Hahn 1881; Snedegar 1995; Slotegraaf, MNASSA 2013; Alcock 2014). Deliberately excluded: ǁGamab/Gamab as a Damara–Haiǁom variant of the existing ǁGaunab; the ogres Aigamuxa, Hai-uri and Bi-blouk as folk-tale bogey figures rather than divine or heroic beings; and the unnamed husband-star (Aldebaran), insect messenger, and fable Sun, none of which is an individuated named divinity. Beyond these, the record surveyed (Bleek 1864; Hahn 1881; Schapera 1930; Hartmann & Sukdaven 2024) names no further individuated Khoekhoe divine or heroic figures.
