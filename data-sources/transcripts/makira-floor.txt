================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added a single figure, Oharimae, the only further individuated Makiran being the attested record yields: Fox and Drew's foundational JRAI account (1915) names it as a hi'ona/figona with serpent incarnation "only known to particular persons," and the passage is reproduced verbatim in G. Elliot Smith's The Evolution of the Dragon (1919). Systematic checks of Codrington (1891), Fox & Drew (1915), Dixon's Oceanic Mythology (1916), Elliot Smith (1919), Fox's The Threshold of the Pacific (1924, including its full term index), and Michael W. Scott's Arosi ethnography (The Severed Snake, 2007; Oceania, 2021) surface no other named individuals: the remaining Makiran supernaturals (figona/hi'ona, adaro, aunga ghosts, kakamora, masi, auhenua ancestors) are unnamed classes, and the only named adaro, Ngorieru/Ugoriaru, is already in the corpus. The snake goddess Walutahanga was deliberately excluded because no scholarly source localizes her beyond generic "Solomon Islands/Melanesia"; Rapuanate is an 'Are'are (Malaita) hero, Warohunugamwanehaora and the Masi fools belong to Ulawa, and Atawa/Amwea are Bauro moieties rather than persons. Beyond Oharimae the individuated record for Makira island appears genuinely exhausted.
