REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added six figures that Rarotongan-specific scholarship clearly individuates beyond the recently authored core: the cosmogonic parents Atea and Papa (Paparoa-i-te-itinga), wired as father and mother of the existing Tangaroa and Rongo (Keane, Te Ara; Taonui in Vaka Moana, 2006; Gill 1876; Craig 1989); the lizard-bodied god Tongaiti, who besides his place among the five divine sons has a dedicated Rarotongan narrative (Manuiri, JPS 5, 1896) and sailed with Tangiia's god-company (Te Ariki-tara-are, JPS 28-29); and the three great mortals of the settlement cycle — the navigator-priest Iro-nui-ma-oata (Te Rei, JPS 25-26, 1916-17; Savage, JPS 25, 1916), his son Tai-te-ariki, adopted by Tangiia and installed as first ariki (Te Ariki-tara-are, JPS 28, 1919), and the pursuer Tutapu-aru-roa (Smith, Hawaiki, 1910; Buck, Vikings of the Sunrise, 1938). I deliberately excluded Tane, who appears in the Rarotongan five-son genealogy but is cultically individuated only on Aitutaki and Mangaia, and Motoro, Tangiia's deified son, whose worship belongs to Mangaia's Ngariki (Gill 1876). Gill's other Mangaian figures (Vari-ma-te-takere, Miru, Tinirau, the lunar aspects of Avatea) were likewise judged to belong to a Mangaian rather than Rarotongan register. Note: the JPS website and several archives were blocked by this session's egress policy, so attestation was verified through search-indexed excerpts of the JPS texts, Te Ara, and the secondary literature cited.
