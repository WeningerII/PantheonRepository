RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISIONS
================================================================================

PARENT CLUSTER. The directed parent edge for every child is greek_minos
(registered; type demigod, half-Olympian son of Zeus by Europa, king of Crete and
later judge of the dead). Minos's existing record lists each of these children only
as an external-reference ("father-of … not registered as Pasiphae-line cascade"),
so none of the four exists yet as a registered figure (confirmed by grep: no
id 'greek_ariadne' / 'greek_androgeos' / 'greek_catreus' / 'greek_glaucus_minos',
and the only registered Glaucus is greek_glaucus_corinth, the Corinthian
taraxippus, a wholly distinct figure). parentRoles {greek_minos:"father"}.

SECOND PARENT — PASIPHAE. The mother of all four is Pasiphae (daughter of Helios).
Per the authoring brief I grepped for greek_pasiphae: there is NO registered
figure (app/data.js line ~6301 holds only a section-comment placeholder
"Pasiphae — sun-daughter wife of Minos", with no id). Pasiphae is therefore an
UNREGISTERED second parent. She is not added to parentIds (matching the corpus
convention already used for greek_phaedra, whose parentIds = ['greek_minos'] only,
and for the Theseus×Phaedra children greek_apollod_acamas / greek_apollod_demophon,
whose parentIds list only the registered parent). She is named in each child's
lifecycle and relations (as an externalRef mother) and in notes.

TYPE BY DESCENT (anti-drift rule). Minos = demigod (divinity fraction 0.5). The
second parent Pasiphae is unregistered and is therefore counted as mortal (0) for
the fraction math, with the sum still divided by 2: (0.5 + 0) / 2 = 0.25. 0.25
falls in the quartigod band (0.1875–0.375). All four children are accordingly
authored as type "quartigod". This is exactly the precedent already in the corpus:
greek_apollod_acamas and greek_apollod_demophon (Theseus demigod × Phaedra
unregistered-mortal) are both registered as "quartigod". None of these four is a
historical human, so the "author historical humans as mortal" carve-out does not
apply; quartigod stands. (Note the mythological subtlety, recorded in notes but NOT
used to inflate the tier: Pasiphae is herself a daughter of Helios, so in full myth
these children are divinely descended on both sides; because she is unregistered the
strict descent math counts her as mortal and the LOWER tier is taken, per the rule.)

SIBLINGS. The full sibling set of Minos × Pasiphae in Apollodorus 3.1.2 is
Ariadne, Phaedra, Androgeos, Glaucus, Catreus, and Deucalion (son of Minos).
Phaedra is the already-existing sibling (greek_phaedra): each of the four authored
figures carries a ONE-WAY sibling relation to greek_phaedra (the brief's rule for an
already-registered sibling). The four authored figures are wired as siblings to one
another RECIPROCALLY within this batch. Deucalion-son-of-Minos and the Minotaur are
unregistered and are given as externalRef sibling/half-sibling notes only where
attested, not as resolvable personIds.

PER-FIGURE NOTES.

  • ARIADNE (greek_ariadne) — daughter of Minos and Pasiphae; gave Theseus the
    clew/thread of wool that let him escape the Labyrinth after killing the Minotaur
    (Apollod. Epit. 1.8–9; Plut. Thes. 19; Catullus 64). Abandoned by Theseus on
    Naxos; became the consort/wife of Dionysus, who set her bridal crown (made by
    Hephaestus) in the sky as the constellation Corona Borealis, and by whom (per
    Hesiod Theog. 947–949) she was made immortal and ageless. Wired: father
    greek_minos; mother (externalRef) Pasiphae; siblings (the three other authored
    children + one-way greek_phaedra); lover greek_apollod_theseus (registered, ended
    by abandonment on Naxos); spouse/consort greek_hesiod_dionysus (registered).
    vitalStatus living (deified-immortalized by Dionysus, Theog. 949 "deathless and
    ageless"); a lifecycle apotheosis stage is recorded, era heroic-age.

  • ANDROGEOS (greek_androgeos) — son of Minos and Pasiphae; an outstanding athlete
    who won every contest at the Athenian Panathenaic games and was then killed —
    either by the other competitors out of envy, or sent by Aegeus against the
    Marathonian Bull, which slew him (Apollod. 3.15.7; Diod. 4.60.5; Plut. Thes. 15).
    His death is the casus belli for which Minos made war on Athens and imposed the
    tribute of youths and maidens to the Minotaur. Father of Alcaeus and Sthenelus,
    whom Heracles later took from Paros (Apollod. 2.5.9). Wired: father greek_minos;
    mother (externalRef) Pasiphae; siblings as above. Dies → lifecycle death stage,
    era heroic-age, vitalStatus deceased.

  • CATREUS (greek_catreus) — eldest son of Minos and Pasiphae; king of Crete after
    Minos. An oracle foretold he would die at the hand of one of his own children;
    he sent his daughters Aerope and Clymene abroad (Aerope to Nauplius, becoming
    ancestress of the house of Atreus) and his son Althaemenes fled to Rhodes to
    avoid fulfilling it. In old age Catreus sailed to Rhodes to leave his kingdom to
    Althaemenes; not recognized, he was killed by Althaemenes' spear/thrown stone —
    the prophesied death by his own child (Apollod. 3.2.1–2; Diod. 5.59). Grandfather,
    through Aerope, of Agamemnon and Menelaus; the funeral of Catreus is the pretext
    that draws Menelaus from Sparta in the Helen-abduction tradition. Wired: father
    greek_minos; mother (externalRef) Pasiphae; siblings as above; children given as
    externalRef (Althaemenes, Aerope, Apemosyne, Clymene — none registered). Dies →
    lifecycle death stage, era heroic-age, vitalStatus deceased.

  • GLAUCUS SON OF CRETE (greek_glaucus_minos) — the young son of Minos and Pasiphae
    who, chasing a mouse (or a ball), fell into a great jar (pithos) of honey and
    drowned. Minos, told by the Curetes that whoever could best describe a strange
    three-coloured calf in his herds could revive the boy, was answered by the seer
    Polyidus of Argos (likening it to a ripening blackberry). Polyidus found the body
    by augury; shut in the tomb with the corpse, he saw a serpent revive a dead
    serpent with a herb, used the same herb to raise Glaucus, and (forced to teach the
    boy divination) on departing had Glaucus spit in his mouth, undoing the lesson
    (Apollod. 3.3.1–2; Hyg. Fab. 136; the lost Sophocles "Manteis"/Polyidus and
    Euripides "Polyidus"). A variant names Asclepius as the reviver (Apollod. 3.10.3,
    among those Asclepius raised). Wired: father greek_minos; mother (externalRef)
    Pasiphae; siblings as above; reviver Polyidus given as externalRef (unregistered);
    Asclepius variant noted. He DIES and is RESURRECTED within the heroic-age, so the
    lifecycle carries a death stage and a restored-to-life stage, both era heroic-age;
    final vitalStatus living (raised by Polyidus). Distinct from greek_glaucus_corinth
    (the Corinthian, eaten by his mares) and from Glaucus the sea-god and Glaucus the
    Lycian — the disambiguation is stated in notes.
