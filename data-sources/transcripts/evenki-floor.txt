================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added two genuinely individuated, well-attested figures absent from the existing set: Agdy, the Evenki personification of thunder (the 'old man/lord of thunder' and the iron, fiery-eyed thunder-birds, famously invoked in oral explanations of the 1908 Tunguska event), and Enekan Togo, 'grandmother-fire', the named spirit-mistress of the household hearth. Enekan Togo is wired to the existing pantheon as subordinate to the sky-grandmother Buga (Enekan Buga), whose hearth-level representative she is; Agdy is honestly left solitary because scholarship attaches it to shamans and the lower-world thunder-ancestors rather than to any attested parentage or symmetric bond. I deliberately excluded weaker candidates: the sun (dylacha) and moon (bega) appear as ordinary words rather than clearly personified named deities, and the water-, taiga-, and animal-'masters' are spirit classes (musun/ejen) without a single attested proper name, so including them would be padding. Sources checked include Vasilevich (1969), Anisimov (1959/1963 Michael translation), Shirokogoroff (1935), and Ol'khovatov (2023).
