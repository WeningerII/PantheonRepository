================================================================================
THE GAP — THE REGISTRY HELD MĀRA AND HIS THREE DAUGHTERS AND NOT THE MAN
THEY WERE SENT TO TEMPT
================================================================================

The Buddhist key held **fourteen records**. Here is all of them:

    Māra                        the tempter
    Taṇhā, Aratī, Rāgā          his three daughters
    Śakra                       king of the Trāyastriṃśa heaven
    Sujā                        Śakra's wife
    Vemacitrin                  the asura lord Śakra fights
    Brahmā Sahāmpati            who asks the Buddha to teach
    Pṛthivī                     the earth, called to witness
    Yama                        the judge of the dead
    Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Virūḍhaka,     the Four Heavenly Kings
    Virūpākṣa, Vaiśravaṇa

Every one of those is a **deva or an adversary** — a figure Buddhism inherited
from the Indian pantheon and filed in its cosmology.

**There is no Buddha in it.** No Śākyamuni. No Amitābha, no Vairocana, no
Bhaiṣajyaguru, no Dīpaṃkara. **There is no bodhisattva in it**: no
Avalokiteśvara, no Mañjuśrī, no Maitreya, no Kṣitigarbha, no Tārā. **There is
nobody from the Buddha's household**: no Māyā, no Śuddhodana, no Yaśodharā, no
Rāhula, no Mahāprajāpatī. **There is not one disciple**: no Śāriputra, no
Maudgalyāyana, no Ānanda, no Devadatta.

Four of the fourteen records exist only in order to attack or petition a man the
registry did not hold. **Pṛthivī is in the corpus because she was called to
witness — and the corpus did not hold who called her.**

This is the programme's recurring finding at the largest scale it has yet been
found: not a missing adversary, not a missing household, but **a world religion
filed entirely through the beings on its margins.**

================================================================================
A SCHEMA PROBLEM THIS BATCH CANNOT SOLVE AND WILL NOT HIDE
================================================================================

This registry's `type` vocabulary is `deity | numen | demigod | quartigod |
scion | mortal`. **Buddhism does not regard a Buddha as a god**, and a bodhisattva
is not a god either; the devas the registry already holds are, in this
tradition's own view, unenlightened beings inside saṃsāra, and it is the Buddhas
and bodhisattvas — who are *not* gods — that stand above them.

The schema has no category for that, so:

- **Śākyamuni is `mortal`**, with his parents in `parentIds` and his awakening on
  his lifecycle, where the tradition puts it. His disciples and household are
  `mortal` for the same reason.
- **The Buddhas and bodhisattvas are `deity`**, because it is the nearest
  category the vocabulary has, **and every one of those records says on its face
  that the tradition does not call them gods.**

**This is escalated and not papered over.** It is the same class of problem lane
23 escalated about the Armenian era labels: a vocabulary that fits most of the
corpus and misdescribes one tradition.

================================================================================
AND ONE REGISTERED ERA FOR EVERYTHING
================================================================================

The Buddhist era vocabulary has exactly one entry, `mythic` — so the historical
Buddha's lifetime, the celestial Buddhas of the pure lands, and Maitreya, who has
not come yet, are all filed in the same bucket.

**This batch does not extend the vocabulary.** Lane 22 extended the Continental
Germanic list because a label's stated date range *contradicted* the material
being filed under it; here the single label is merely coarse, not wrong, and
splitting a tradition's era vocabulary is a claim about that tradition's shape
that the owner should make.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BATCH DOES NOT INGEST
================================================================================

The **Eighteen Arhats**, the **Twenty-Eight Buddhas** as a series, the **Thirty-
Five Confession Buddhas** and the **Thousand Buddhas of this aeon** are
**collectives and series**; individuals are ingested where the sources give them
an act. **Kaṇṭhaka**, the horse Siddhārtha rides out of the palace, is an animal
identified by an owner — the rejection this programme has applied to five horses,
two hounds, Balder's foal and the dogs of Artavazd. Kapilavastu, Bodh Gayā,
Sārnāth and Kuśinagara are **places**; Sukhāvatī and Vaiḍūryanirbhāsa are
**places** too, even though they are pure lands. The Bodhi tree is a **tree**.
