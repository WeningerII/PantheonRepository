Sweep 005 — strict-alphabetical family-completion sweep, batch 5.
Batch of 50 ids (bhil_pithora … buryat_erlen_khan) read in order, full prose
(notes, every lifecycle[].notes, sources[].citations) read semantically.

Outcome: the Bicolano (Asuang/Gugurang/Oryol/Magindara/Handyong), Blackfoot
(Sun-Moon-Morning Star), Bororo (twin culture-heroes, the aroe/bope opposition),
Bodo, the Buddhist Four-Heavenly-Kings set, the Buganda Kintu cycle and the
Ssese-house balubaale, the Bunyoro Batembuzi→Bacwezi line, the Burmese nat
sibling-pairs and Thagyamin, and Buryat Erlen Khan are overwhelmingly already
wired within their clusters. Eleven genuinely NAMED-BUT-NO-ENTRY attested kin
were found, verified, and authored below.

AUTHORED (NEW), with the in-corpus prose that names them:

1. buddhist_tanha / buddhist_arati / buddhist_raga — Māra's three daughters
   Taṇhā (Craving), Aratī (Aversion/Discontent) and Rāgā (Passion), named in the
   buddhist_mara entry ("assails the Bodhisattva at the awakening with armies and
   the three daughters"). They appear together in the Māra-saṃyutta (SN 4, the
   Dhītaro Sutta SN 4.25) and the Dhammapada commentary on vv.179–180. Authored as
   daughters of Māra (parentId buddhist_mara) and mutual sisters; reciprocal
   father-of edges needed on the existing buddhist_mara.

2. buddhist_suja — Sujā, consort of Śakra and daughter of the asura-lord
   Vemacitrin, named in BOTH the buddhist_sakra entry ("married to Sujā, daughter
   of the asura lord Vemacitrin") and the buddhist_vemacitrin entry ("father-in-law
   of Sakka through his daughter Sujā"). Per DPPN s.v. Sujā and the Kulāvaka Jātaka
   (No.31). Authored: spouse buddhist_sakra, father buddhist_vemacitrin (parentId);
   reciprocals needed on both existing figures.

3. buganda_kayikuuzi — Kayikuuzi ("he who digs holes"), third child of the
   sky-father Ggulu and brother of Nambi and Walumbe, named in the buganda_ggulu
   entry ("father of Nambi, Walumbe, and Kayikuuzi"). In the Kintu cycle (Roscoe,
   The Baganda, 1911) Ggulu sends him to recapture Walumbe; he digs the Ttanda
   pits chasing Death. Authored: parentId buganda_ggulu, sibling buganda_nambi,
   sibling buganda_walumbe; reciprocals needed on those existing figures.

4. bunyoro_isimbwa — Isimbwa, son of the last Batembuzi king Isaza and of Nyamata
   (Nyamiyonga's daughter), and father of the Bacwezi founder Ndahura. Named in
   the bunyoro_ndahura entry ("Ndahura's father Isimbwa was son of Isaza") and the
   bunyoro_nyamiyonga entry ("Nyamiyonga's daughter Nyamata was mother of Isimbwa").
   Per Roscoe, The Bakitara or Banyoro (1923); Fisher, Twilight Tales (1911).
   Authored: parentIds [bunyoro_isaza, bunyoro_nyamata], father-of bunyoro_ndahura.

5. bunyoro_isaza — Isaza, last king of the Batembuzi, husband of Nyamata and father
   of Isimbwa, lured into the underworld by Nyamiyonga; named in the bunyoro_ndahura
   entry ("his father Isimbwa was son of Isaza, last of the Batembuzi kings") and
   implied throughout the bunyoro_nyamiyonga entry ("drew the Batembuzi king Isaza
   into the land of the dead"). Authored: spouse bunyoro_nyamata, father-of
   bunyoro_isimbwa, enemy/captive-of bunyoro_nyamiyonga; reciprocal needed on
   bunyoro_nyamiyonga.

6. bunyoro_nyamata — Nyamata, daughter of the underworld-king Nyamiyonga, wife of
   Isaza and mother of Isimbwa, named in the bunyoro_nyamiyonga entry ("Nyamiyonga's
   daughter Nyamata was mother of Isimbwa") and bunyoro_ndahura. Authored: parentId
   bunyoro_nyamiyonga, spouse bunyoro_isaza, mother-of bunyoro_isimbwa; reciprocal
   needed on bunyoro_nyamiyonga.

7. burmese_byatta — Byatta, the royal flower-gatherer of King Anawrahta and father
   of the Taungbyon brothers, named in the burmese_shwe_hpyin_naungdaw / _nyidaw
   entries ("sons of Byatta and the ogress Me Wunna"). Executed for his liaison, he
   became a nat. Per R. C. Temple, The Thirty-Seven Nats (1906); Maung Htin Aung,
   Folk Elements in Burmese Buddhism (1962). Authored: spouse burmese_me_wunna,
   father-of both Taungbyon brothers.

8. burmese_me_wunna — Me Wunna (Popa Medaw), the flower-eating ogress of Mount Popa,
   mother of the Taungbyon brothers, named in the same two brother-entries. She died
   of grief and became a nat. Per Temple (1906); Maung Htin Aung (1962). Authored:
   spouse burmese_byatta, mother-of both Taungbyon brothers.

9. blackfoot_apistotoke — Apistotoke (Ihtsipaitapiiyo'pa, "Source of Life"), the
   abstract creator, named in BOTH the blackfoot_napi entry ("the abstract creator
   Apistotoke") and the blackfoot_natosi entry ("Naato'si, who was himself created
   by the abstract Apistotoke"). Blackfoot tradition keeps Apistotoke and Naato'si
   strictly distinct. Per native-languages.org (Apistotoke); Grinnell, Blackfeet
   Lodge Tales. Authored: creator-of / ancestor-of blackfoot_natosi.

WIRE-EDGES on existing corpus figures (no new entry needed; reported for the
maintainer to apply — both endpoints already exist):

  A. ipisowaahs--parent-->blackfoot_natosi  AND  ipisowaahs--parent-->blackfoot_kokomikisomm
     The Morning-Star figure ipisowaahs has empty parentIds, yet blackfoot_natosi
     records father-of-->ipisowaahs and blackfoot_kokomikisomm records
     mother-of-->ipisowaahs. Add [blackfoot_natosi, blackfoot_kokomikisomm] to
     ipisowaahs.parentIds so the Sun-Moon→Morning-Star→Poïa lineage closes from the
     child side. (Both targets exist; poia already lists ipisowaahs as parent.)

  B. bunyoro_ndahura--father-of-->bunyoro_wamara
     bunyoro_wamara records parentIds [bunyoro_ndahura] (Wamara is the last Bacwezi
     king, Ndahura's successor-son), but bunyoro_ndahura carries no reciprocal
     parent→child edge. Add father-of-->bunyoro_wamara to bunyoro_ndahura. (Target
     exists.) Authoring bunyoro_isimbwa (item 4) with father-of-->bunyoro_ndahura
     likewise calls for bunyoro_ndahura.parentIds to gain [bunyoro_isimbwa],
     completing Isaza→Isimbwa→Ndahura→Wamara.
