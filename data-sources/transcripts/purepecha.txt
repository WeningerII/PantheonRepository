================================================================================
RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISION
================================================================================

The chief source for Tarascan (Purépecha) religion is the *Relación de las ceremonias y
ritos, población y gobernación de los indios de la provincia de Mechuacán* (the *Relación
de Michoacán*, c. 1539–41), compiled by the Franciscan friar Jerónimo de Alcalá from the
oral testimony of the priest-petámuti and Uacúsecha nobility. Its second and third parts
preserve the dynastic epic of Tariácuri and the cult of Curicaueri. Standard modern
scholarship: Helen Perlstein Pollard, *Tariácuri's Legacy: The Prehispanic Tarascan State*
(Univ. of Oklahoma Press, 1993); J. Benedict Warren, *The Conquest of Michoacán* (1985);
and studies of Tarascan religion by Hans Roskamp and (on the Tarascan language of the
Relación) the lexical/onomastic tradition behind the colonial Tarascan vocabularies.

EIGHT firmly-attested figures are authored here, in two clusters plus two singletons.

1) THE FOUR FURTHER TIRIPEMENCHA (brother-gods of the directions) — siblings of the
   existing purepecha_tiripeme. The Relación describes the Tiripemencha ("Tiripemecha")
   dividing/separating (at Vayámeo) so that each became the patron god of a Pátzcuaro-basin
   town; together with Curicaueri they are the brother-gods (hermanos) set over the houses
   of the world. The individually named brothers attested are:
     • TIRIPEME QUARENCHA (Cuarencha) — the eastern brother (rising-sun / red in the
       secondary systematization), associated with Corínguaro.
     • TIRIPEME TURUPTEN — patron god taken to Irámuco (Ylamucuo / Iramuco); western
       brother (setting-sun / white).
     • TIRIPEME XUNGÁPETI (Xugápeti) — patron god taken to Pechátaro; northern brother
       (yellow).
     • TIRIPEME CAHERI ("the great Tiripeme") — patron god taken to Pareo; southern
       brother (noon-sun / black). Called "the black squirrel" in some readings and tied
       to the war-squads.
   The cardinal-direction-and-colour scheme (east/red, north/yellow, west/white, south/
   black, with a fifth blue centre) is the modern scholarly systematization (Pollard 1993
   and following) of the directional Tiripemencha; in the Relación itself the secure datum
   is the *brotherhood* and the *town patronage*. Both layers are cited and the colour/
   direction notes are attributed to the secondary literature, not over-claimed for the
   primary source.

   WIRING DECISION (accuracy-first, per HARD RULE 1). The Relación makes the Tiripemencha
   the BROTHERS of Curicaueri (the corpus already records purepecha_tiripeme as a `sibling`
   of purepecha_curicaueri, and curicaueri carries the reciprocal). Curicaueri is therefore
   their BROTHER, not their father; wiring the new brothers as "children of Curicaueri"
   would be factually wrong. They are accordingly wired as:
     - parentIds: ["purepecha_cuerauaperi"] (parentRoles {mother}) — Cuerauáperi is the
       attested "mother of all the gods"; this is the directed parent edge mandated for the
       Tiripemencha, set to the *actual* divine parent (the mother).
     - sibling → purepecha_curicaueri  (RECIPROCAL-NEEDED on the existing figure)
     - sibling → purepecha_tiripeme    (RECIPROCAL-NEEDED on the existing figure)
     - sibling → each of the three other new Tiripemencha (reciprocated within this batch)
   This keeps the corpus internally consistent with its existing Curicaueri↔Tiripeme
   sibling model and is the well-attested reading. type = deity (offspring of a deity /
   brother-god). The reason is recorded in each figure's notes.

2) THE FOUNDING UACÚSECHA LORDS — UÁPEANI and PAUACUME, with TARIÁCURI.
     • PAUACUME (Pawácume) and UÁPEANI (Vapeani) are, in the Relación, the founding lords
       of the Uacúsecha ("the eagles"), sons of Sicuirancha; they settled the Pátzcuaro
       basin and recognized the Chichimec gods. PAUACUME is the father, and UÁPEANI the
       uncle, of Tariácuri. (Two later lords repeat the same names a generation on; the
       figures authored here are the elder founding pair, sons of Sicuirancha.) Authored as
       MORTAL legendary ancestral lords (not children of deities), wired as brothers
       (sibling, reciprocated in-batch).
     • TARIÁCURI (Purépecha *Tarhiakurhi*, glossed "strong wind" in the modern reading) —
       the great founder-king and culture-hero of the Tarascan state; born into the
       Uacúsecha clan (14th c.), raised by priests as a *curití* in the service of
       Curicaueri, and sent forth by a prophetic vision of Curicaueri to forge a unified
       state. He established the three royal seats and, at his death, divided the realm
       among his son Hiquíngare (Pátzcuaro) and his nephews Hiripan (Ihuatzio) and
       Tangáxoan (Tzintzuntzan). He carried and distributed the obsidian blades of
       Curicaueri and is the deified dynastic founder. Authored as a DEMIGOD (a deified
       historical/legendary king), child (parentId) of purepecha_pawacume so he is not
       kinless. era mythic.

3) AUICANIME (Avicanime) — the goddess of hunger/famine, named in the Relación as one of
   "the aunts of the gods of heaven," who in the myth deceives a woman into cooking her own
   son while the gods starve; she personifies the divine hunger that the Tarascan
   sacrificial system was meant to feed. Authored as a DEITY. No individually named divine
   kin is firmly attested for her (the "aunt of the gods" formula is a generic kinship
   class, not a specific named parent or sibling), so she is flagged SOLITARY with the
   cited reason rather than wired to a guessed relative.

FIGURES CONSIDERED BUT NOT AUTHORED (with reasons):
  • CHUPITHIRIPEME / Chupi Tiripeme (the "blue" fifth/centre Tiripeme, lake-and-sacrifice)
    — the centre-of-the-world fifth member appears mainly in the modern fivefold
    direction-and-colour systematization of the Tiripemencha (Pollard and following) rather
    than as a cleanly separable named brother in the Relación's town list; to avoid padding
    the directional set beyond the four securely individuated, town-patron brothers (and
    the existing purepecha_tiripeme), Chupithiripeme is NOT authored here. It is noted in
    the Tiripemencha figures' notes as the centre/blue member of the scheme.
  • CURITA CAHERI / Curita-caheri ("the great burning"), Hurendequavécara, Pungarancha,
    Querenda-angápeti, Uazoríquare, etc. — further named Tarascan deities and local patrons
    of the Relación are real but are deferred to a later pass; this pass covers exactly the
    figures requested and securely individuated above, without padding.
