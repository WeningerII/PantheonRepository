================================================================================
RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISION
================================================================================

The three eponymous ancestors of the Tutsi, Hutu and Twa — GATUTSI, GAHUTU and GATWA —
are firmly attested in the Rwandan dynastic-and-origin tradition but were NOT yet authored
as separate corpus entries (they appear only inside the `notes`/`lifecycle` prose of
banyaruanda_kanyarwanda and banyaruanda_kazikamuntu). They are authored here as three new
figures, wired as sons of Kanyarwanda and siblings of one another.

VERIFICATION (genuine absence). GREP of app/data.js and data-sources/transcripts/ for the
bare names (Gatutsi/Gahutu/Gatwa, plus Katutsi/Kahutu/Katwa) and for the prefixed ids
banyaruanda_gatutsi / banyaruanda_gahutu / banyaruanda_gatwa returns ONLY mentions inside
existing figures' prose (Kanyarwanda's and Kazikamuntu's notes/lifecycle). No standalone
entry exists for any of the three. They are therefore genuinely absent and authored in full.

SOURCES. The chief modern source is Jacques J. Maquet, *The Premise of Inequality in Ruanda:
A Study of Political Relations in a Central African Kingdom* (Oxford Univ. Press, for the
International African Institute, 1961), which records the origin tale of the three sons and
the destiny-allotment by which their inequality was sacralised. The dynastic frame
(Gihanga → Kanyarwanda Gahima) follows Alexis Kagame, *Inganji Karinga*, and Jan Vansina,
*Antecedents to Modern Rwanda: The Nyiginya Kingdom* (Univ. of Wisconsin Press, 2004; his
English version of *Le Rwanda ancien: Le royaume nyiginya*, 2001). Earlier Bantu-myth
attestation: A. C. Werner, *Myths and Legends of the Bantu* (London: Harrap, 1933), ch. VIII.
The widely-cited synthesis of the variant fatherhoods (Gihanga / Kanyarwanda / Kazikamuntu /
Kigwa) and the milk-pot test is collected at "Origins of Hutu, Tutsi and Twa" (Wikipedia),
which cites Maquet and the Rwandan oral tradition.

THE MYTH (as authored). A primordial father — named variously as Gihanga, his son
Kanyarwanda Gahima, the first man Kazikamuntu, or the celestial Kigwa — set his three sons
a test of vigilance (in the best-known version each is given a pot of milk to guard
overnight). Gatwa drank his milk; Gahutu fell asleep and let his pot spill; only Gatutsi
kept watch and preserved his. The father therefore made Gatutsi his successor and lord of
cattle, set Gahutu to serve his brother (acquiring cattle only through that service), and
consigned Gatwa to the margins of society. In the parallel Kazikamuntu telling already in
the corpus, Gahutu likewise fails by falling asleep over an assigned task, Gatutsi is chosen
as the sober and clever chief, and Gatwa is CURSED for killing one of his brothers — i.e.
Gatwa does not merely lose but is cursed/expelled. This allotment of destinies is the
myth's etiological core: it sacralises the precolonial Tutsi–Hutu–Twa hierarchy.

WIRING DECISION (per instructions; founder-ancestors).
  - parentIds: ["banyaruanda_kanyarwanda"] (parentRoles {father}) for each of the three —
    Kanyarwanda Gahima is the cluster parent; the directed son-of edge is set to him. No
    second parent (mother) is individuated/registered in the tradition, so none is wired.
  - sibling → each of the other two new sons (reciprocated WITHIN this batch).
  - VARIANT NOTE recorded in each figure: a variant tradition makes the three the sons of
    the first man Kazikamuntu (banyaruanda_kazikamuntu) instead of Kanyarwanda (and still
    other variants name Gihanga or Kigwa). This is stated in `notes` but, to avoid asserting
    two competing directed parent edges, only the cluster parent Kanyarwanda is wired in
    parentIds; the Kazikamuntu variant is documented, not double-wired.

TYPE BY DESCENT (anti-drift / anti-inversion). Kanyarwanda is a quartigod (divinity
fraction 0.25); the unnamed, unregistered second parent counts as mortal (0); the child
fraction is therefore (0.25 + 0)/2 = 0.125, which by the bands alone would fall in the
scion range (0.046–0.1875). HOWEVER these three are the eponymous TRIBAL FOUNDERS of the
Tutsi, Hutu and Twa, which the authoring rule directs be authored as "mortal" regardless;
the parallel canonical strand likewise makes them sons of the MORTAL first man Kazikamuntu;
and the tie-break instruction is to pick the LOWER tier when unsure (mortal < scion).
All three are accordingly authored as type = "mortal". era = "heroic-age" (the cluster era),
which keeps each child no earlier than the quartigod parent. The reasoning is recorded in
each figure's notes.

LIFECYCLE. Each son undergoes the destiny-allotment transformation (made chief / made
servant / cursed-and-cast-out), and all three are deceased founding ancestors, so a
lifecycle stage is recorded for each, with era EXACTLY "heroic-age". Gatwa's stage records
the curse/expulsion (the "transform/fall" event) explicitly.
