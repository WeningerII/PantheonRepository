Tahitian (Society Islands) pantheon — figure-authoring transcript
================================================================

Tradition: Tahitian (Society Islands — Tahiti, Ra'iātea, Borabora, Huahine, Mo'orea).
Registered era keys (ERA_ORDER['Tahitian']): primordial, mythic, mythic-prehistoric.
New id prefix: tahitian_. The Pantheon Registry previously held ZERO Tahitian figures; this
batch authors the well-attested core of the Society Islands pantheon from scratch.

Sources (the standard corpus for the Society Islands)
-----------------------------------------------------
  - Teuira Henry, Ancient Tahiti (Bernice P. Bishop Museum Bulletin 48, Honolulu, 1928) —
    the foundational compilation, built from the manuscripts (the "Orsmond MSS") of the
    missionary John Muggridge Orsmond (Henry's grandfather), collected from named Tahitian
    priests and orators before 1848. The Ta'aroa cosmogony, the chant of the great gods,
    the 'Oro / Vairaumati cycle, the Ruahatu deluge, the Hina-and-Ru lunar myth and the
    Māui sun-snaring all descend from this work (and its French original, Tahiti aux temps
    anciens, trans. Bertrand Jaunez, Société des Océanistes).
  - William Ellis, Polynesian Researches (London, 1829; 2nd ed. 1831), vols. I–II — the
    earliest systematic missionary ethnography of the Society Islands; the four great gods,
    the 'arioi society, the to'o images and the Maui sun-myth.
  - J. A. Moerenhout, Voyages aux îles du Grand Océan (Paris, 1837) — the Ta'aroa creation
    chant ("Il était Taaroa…") and the great-gods cosmogony, a parallel early recension.

The Tahitian "manifestation" principle (load-bearing for the genealogy below): in the
Society Islands a deity is regularly given in several named forms, and "sisters and daughters
are often to be understood as manifestations of the same deity in several forms, each with
its distinct place in myth and ceremonial." Hina-tu-a-uta ("Hina-standing-inland"), the named
mother of 'Oro, and Hina-nui-te-araara, the woman in the moon, are accordingly authored here
as one Hina with multiple attested aspect-names, not as separate figures (Henry, Ancient
Tahiti, 1928).

AUTHORED (nine firmly-attested core figures)
  * TA'AROA (Tangaroa) — tahitian_taaroa [deity, primordial]. The supreme creator who alone
    existed in the dark, revolving in his shell Rumia (an egg-shell in the void), his own
    parent with neither father nor mother; he cracked the shell, made the rock-foundation and
    the sky-domes from its pieces, and formed the world out of his own body — backbone the
    mountain-ranges, ribs the ridges and cliffs, flesh the soil, blood the rainbow and the red
    of creatures, tears the seas and lakes and rivers, finger- and toe-nails the shell and
    scales of fishes and turtles, feathers (when he shook them off) the trees and verdure. He
    built the first fare-atua ("god's house") of his own body — backbone the ridgepole, ribs
    the rafters — the model of every marae. He then summoned the artisans who, with their
    adzes (to'i), sculpted the great gods. SOLITARY (self-existent, parentless).
  * TĀNE — tahitian_tane [deity, mythic]. "The first god," sculpted at Ta'aroa's command; god
    of beauty, of the forests and of the craftsmen, and the god of peace. He adorned the dome
    of the sky, fixing the stars, hanging the sun for the day and the moon for the night; in
    the octopus-cosmogony it is Tāne who severs the clinging arms of Tumu-ra'i-fenua, the great
    octopus whose body had enclosed the sky, and fills the world with beauty. Shaped by
    Ta'aroa → parentIds [tahitian_taaroa]; brother of Tū, Ro'o and 'Oro within the batch.
  * TŪ — tahitian_tu [deity, mythic]. Tū-the-craftsman (Tū-nui, Tū-of-the-face-toward-the-sky
    in the wider Polynesian record), the ancient god of war and of artisans, Ta'aroa's fellow-
    worker in the making of the world; the older war-god of the Society Islands, later
    overshadowed on Tahiti by 'Oro. Shaped by Ta'aroa → parentIds [tahitian_taaroa]; brother of
    Tāne, Ro'o, 'Oro.
  * RO'O — tahitian_roo [deity, mythic]. The Tahitian Rongo/Lono: god of agricultural produce,
    of the breadfruit harvest and of the weather, the orator-god and messenger of the gods (and
    especially of Tāne). One of the four great gods of the Society Islands. Shaped by Ta'aroa →
    parentIds [tahitian_taaroa]; brother of Tāne, Tū, 'Oro. (Distinct from the mortal fisherman
    Ro'o of the Ruahatu deluge — a homonym, not authored.)
  * 'ORO — tahitian_oro [deity, mythic]. The great war-god of Ra'iātea, son of Ta'aroa and
    Hina(-tu-a-uta), born and enshrined at the marae Taputapuatea in 'Opoa on Ra'iātea — its
    mythic origin. In peace the patron of the fine arts; founder and patron of the 'arioi
    society of player-priests. He dwelt on Mount Paia (Borabora) with his sisters Te-ouri and
    'Oa'oa, descended to earth on the rainbow as a warrior, and took the mortal maiden
    Vairaumati of Vaitape to wife; his images were the to'o, a sennit-bound wooden core dressed
    in red and yellow feathers. parentIds [tahitian_taaroa, tahitian_hina]; spouse Vairaumati
    is mortal and below the registry's threshold, recorded by externalRef; brother of Tāne, Tū,
    Ro'o.
  * HINA — tahitian_hina [deity, mythic]. The great goddess of many forms: as Hina-nui-te-
    araara ("great Hina the watcher") the woman in the moon, who stepped from Ru's canoe into
    the full moon (Hina-i-a'a-i-te-marama) and beats there the cloth of the gods beneath her
    banyan; as Hina-fa'auru-va'a ("Hina the canoe-steerer") the pilot of her brother Ru in his
    voyages; and as Hina-tu-a-uta ("Hina-standing-inland") the consort of Ta'aroa and mother of
    'Oro. Mother of 'Oro → reciprocated; authored as a single deity with multiple aspect-names.
  * MĀUI — tahitian_maui [demigod, mythic-prehistoric]. The demigod trickster and culture-hero;
    in the Tahitian telling a priest (tahu'a) at the marae who, his work unfinished as the sun
    sank, seized the sun's rays (the hihi) and held back its course to lengthen the day, and who
    fished up islands (notably of the Tuamotu) from the sea-floor with his sacred hook. Later
    deified. SOLITARY at the registry threshold (his attested kin in the Tahitian record are
    not separately registered) — flagged.
  * HIRO — tahitian_hiro [demigod, mythic-prehistoric]. The half-divine hero of the navigators
    (and, proverbially, of thieves) of Ra'iātea: master canoe-wright who built the great pahi
    voyaging-ships, slayer of sea-monsters, protector of seafarers. Husband of Vai-tu-marie and
    father of Marama ("Moonlight"). SOLITARY at the registry threshold (his named human kin are
    not registered) — flagged.
  * RUAHATU — tahitian_ruahatu [deity, mythic]. The ocean-god of the deep, a being with a man's
    body and the tail/snout of a swordfish (a'u); in the Ra'iātea deluge two fishermen, Te-aho-
    aroa and Ro'o, snagged their hooks in his hair as he slept and woke him, whereupon he vowed
    to drown the land beneath the sea, sparing only those who fled to the islet Toa-marama; the
    flood rose and covered Ra'iātea. SOLITARY (no parent, consort or sibling attested) — flagged.

VERIFIED-AND-CONSIDERED, NOT AUTHORED (no padding)
  * ATEA / TE TUMU / Te Papa — the primal-pair cosmogony (Atea "space" with Papa, or Te Tumu
    "foundation" with Te Papa "stratum-rock") belongs primarily to the Tuamotu and other groups;
    in the dominant Society-Islands Orsmond/Henry recension the world is made by Ta'aroa from his
    own body, not by an Atea–Papa pair. Not authored, to avoid importing a non-Tahitian primary
    cosmogony.
  * TE FATU / Tefatou — appears chiefly as a Marquesan/wider-Polynesian earth-/underworld figure;
    not securely individuated in the core Society-Islands record. Not authored.
  * The 'arioi twin patrons Orotetefa and Uretetefa, the sisters Te-ouri and 'Oa'oa, Vairaumati,
    Vai-tu-marie and Marama — recorded only by externalRef where they touch an authored figure;
    they sit below the registry's individuated-deity threshold.
