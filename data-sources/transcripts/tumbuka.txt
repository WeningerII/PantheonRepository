PANTHEON REGISTRY — FIGURE-AUTHORING TRANSCRIPT
Tradition: Tumbuka (traditional religion of the Tumbuka / Tumbuka-Kamanga peoples of
northern Malawi and eastern Zambia — the Chiuta high-god cult, the Chikang'ombe
serpent-divinity, and the vimbuza spirit world)
Eras (registered, exact): "primordial", "mythic"
  - "primordial" = the self-existent creator / sky / rain god (cosmic, timeless)
  - "mythic"     = the serpent-divinity and other narrative beings
ID prefix: tumbuka_

================================================================================
RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISION
================================================================================

Tumbuka traditional religion centres on a self-existent high god, CHIUTA (also called
Leza and Mulungu), approached above all as the sender of rain; and on the great
serpent/python sky-divinity CHIKANG'OMBE, the numen of the highland hill-shrines through
whom the high god was reached and rain solicited. The healing world of the vimbuza
spirits — the basis of the UNESCO-inscribed (2008) vimbuza healing dance — is a CULT (a
class of afflicting/possessing spirits and the ng'oma drumming that treats them), not an
individuated divine figure, and is recorded as such in the figures' notes rather than
authored as a person.

Primary / anchor ethnographic literature consulted (via WebSearch; the egress policy in
this session blocked direct page fetches of Wikipedia, Google Books, Internet Archive,
Project Gutenberg, university repositories and the encyclopedia mirrors, returning HTTP
403 at the gateway — so citations are given at WORK level, without page numbers, and only
where the datum was independently corroborated across the reference literature):
  • T. Cullen Young, "Notes on the History of the Tumbuka-Kamanga Peoples of the Northern
    Province of Nyasaland" (1932); and "Notes on the Customs and Folk-lore of the
    Tumbuka-Kamanga Peoples" — the foundational records of the Chiuta high god and the
    Chikang'ombe snake-cult and its Mkandawire priesthood.
  • Alice Werner, "The Natives of British Central Africa" (1906) and "Myths and Legends of
    the Bantu" (1933) — early ethnographic notice of the Henga/Tumbuka snake-divinity and
    the central-Bantu high-god names (Chiuta, Leza, Mulungu).
  • Steven M. Friedson, "Dancing Prophets: Musical Experience in Tumbuka Healing"
    (Univ. of Chicago Press, 1996) — the standard modern ethnography of Tumbuka religion,
    the vimbuza spirits, the nchimi healer-prophets and the ng'oma drum.
  • Molefi Kete Asante & Ama Mazama (eds.), "Encyclopedia of African Religion" (Sage,
    2009), s.v. Chiuta — the consolidated modern statement of Chiuta's epithets and role.
  • "Chiuta," Oxford Reference (A Dictionary of African Mythology / world-mythology
    reference) — the "great bow"/rainbow gloss and the rain-god core.
  • Harry W. Langworthy and the territorial-cult literature on northern Malawi
    (and the Leiden study of chieftainship/territory in northern Malawi) for the
    religious-political primacy of the Chikang'ombe shrine over incoming chiefly lineages.
Where a datum rests on a particular layer of this literature, the layer is cited on the
field rather than asserted for all sources at once.

TWO firmly-attested, individuated figures are authored.

1) CHIUTA — deity, era "primordial". The self-existent (self-created), omniscient creator
   and sky/rain god; an "absentee" high god of the heavens. His name is glossed "the great
   bow" / "the great bow in the heavens" and he is symbolised by the rainbow (Tumbuka uta =
   "bow"; the rainbow is the bow of Chiuta in the sky). Above all a RAIN god — the succour
   of a people often afflicted by drought — controlling rain, thunder/lightning, fertility
   and the harvest. Epithets attested in the reference literature: Mulengi ("creator"),
   Mwenco ("owner of all things"), Wamtatakuya ("eternal"); also named Leza and Mulungu in
   the wider central-Bantu high-god vocabulary. Authored SOLITARY: Tumbuka theology gives
   Chiuta no parents, consort or divine offspring (he is self-existent), so no kin edge is
   asserted. Placed in the "primordial" era as the cosmic, timeless creator/sky/rain god,
   per the registered era keys.

2) CHIKANG'OMBE (Chikangombe) — numen, era "mythic". The great snake/python sky-divinity
   of the Tumbuka highlands (the Nyika–Henga–Nkhamanga country), associated with rain (and,
   in the ethnographic record, lightning) and served at hill-shrines — Chikang'ombe hill in
   Nkhamanga and Mwanda hill on the Malawi–Zambia border among his "homes" — by priests of
   the MKANDAWIRE clan. He is the medium/intermediary through whom the high god (Chiuta /
   Leza) was approached and rain solicited; he manifests as a (male) snake/python and could
   take other living forms. Women/girls were dedicated to him as his spirit-wives. His
   shrine held a religious-political primacy that the incoming Chikulamayembe chiefly
   lineage sought to subordinate. type = numen (a divine serpent-power / numinous being),
   per the corpus's use of that type for such beings. Authored SOLITARY: the sources give
   Chikang'ombe no named divine parent, sibling or offspring; his tie to Chiuta is the cult
   relationship of medium-to-high-god (recorded in domains/faculties/notes), NOT an attested
   kinship, so — per the accuracy-first rule — no kin/relation edge is asserted to Chiuta or
   anyone else. The spirit-wives are a cult institution (human women dedicated to the god),
   not a named divine consort, and so are recorded in faculties/materialCulture, not as a
   relation.

FIGURES CONSIDERED BUT NOT AUTHORED (with reasons):
  • MWEZI ("the moon") — NOT AUTHORED. "Mwezi" is simply the ordinary Tumbuka (and Chewa)
    common noun for "moon / month" (Tumbuka–English and Chewa dictionaries; the Chewa
    proverb "Mwezi satungira mkanda"). The claim that Mwezi is "the moon goddess of Tumbuka
    mythology" appears only in tertiary, uncited content-mill mythology pages and mirrors;
    it is not attested as an individuated Tumbuka divinity in any of the ethnographic
    literature (Young, Werner, Friedson, the Encyclopedia of African Religion). Per the
    brief's instruction to verify and not assume, Mwezi is excluded rather than invented.
  • The VIMBUZA — NOT AUTHORED as a figure. The vimbuza are a CLASS of afflicting/healing
    (possessing) spirits and the name of the ng'oma healing dance/cult that treats the
    illness they cause; the related vyanusi/Nguni-warrior spirits are likewise a class.
    No single, individually named vimbuza divinity is attested, so the vimbuza are recorded
    as a cult in Chikang'ombe's and Chiuta's notes rather than authored as a person.
  • CHEWA / MARAVI rain-cult figures (Makewana, the Msinja/Chisumphi shrine, Mbona of the
    Lower Shire) — DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED. These belong to the neighbouring Chewa/Mang'anja
    territorial rain-cults, not to Tumbuka religion, and no source firmly attests them as
    Tumbuka; importing them would be a tradition error.

Both authored figures are kinless within Tumbuka theology → flagged SOLITARY. No
within-batch symmetric relations exist, so there are no RECIPROCAL-NEEDED edges and no
cross-corpus reciprocals to add.
