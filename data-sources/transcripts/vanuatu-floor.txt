================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added six individuated, doubly-cited figures: from Codrington's Banks Islands Qat cycle the ogre Qasavara, Qat's spirit-wife Iro Lei, and the money-spirit Ro Som (all verified verbatim in Codrington 1891, corroborated by Dixon 1916 and the Codrington-Palmer Mota dictionary 1896); and from Malekula the devouring guardian Le-hev-hev (Layard 1942; Campbell 1959; Deacon's JRAI 1934 article; Encyclopedia of Religion 2005) plus the culture hero Ambat and the ogress Nevinbimbaau (Deacon 1934; Poignant 1967), linked as reciprocal enemies. The eleven Tangaro brothers were deliberately not authored separately: only Tangaro the Wise and Tangaro the Fool are individuated, and those aspects are already covered by the existing Tagaro and Mwerambuto entries. Thinner named figures - Iro Vilgale (the first woman, a single Codrington passage), Temes Savsap (the Seniang counterpart of Le-hev-hev), Temes Malau, Mansip, Lindanda, and the mortal Ganviviris - are documented within the notes of the six new records rather than as standalone entries.
