PANTHEON REGISTRY — FIGURE-AUTHORING TRANSCRIPT
Tradition: Samoan (Sāmoa, Western Polynesia)
Registered era vocabulary: primordial, mythic, mythic-prehistoric
Author batch: weningerii@gmail.com — 2026-06-23

PURPOSE
-------
The Samoan pantheon was entirely absent from the Pantheon Registry. This transcript
supplies its well-attested CORE — the supreme creator, the lord of the spirit-world
and his war-goddess daughter, the earthquake-god, the coconut-origin woman, the
heaven-raiding fisherman, the conjoined tattoo-twins, and the firmly-attested war-
god octopus, the high earth-god Moso, and the ʻava-origin figure Pava. Every figure
is drawn from the standard nineteenth/early-twentieth-century ethnographic corpus of
Samoan religion: chiefly George Turner, "Samoa, a Hundred Years Ago and Long Before"
(London: Macmillan, 1884); John B. Stair, "Old Samoa, or Flotsam and Jetsam from the
Pacific Ocean" (London: Religious Tract Society, 1897) and his "Jottings on the
Mythology and Spirit-lore of Old Samoa" (Journal of the Polynesian Society, 1896) and
his account of the temple of the Feʻe (JPS, 1894); and Augustin Krämer, "Die
Samoa-Inseln" (Stuttgart, 1902/03; Eng. "The Samoa Islands," trans. T. Verhaaren),
the great genealogical-traditional monograph. Fraser's translations of the Samoan
creation chant (in the Journal of the Polynesian Society) underlie the cosmogony.
No figure or citation here is invented. Samoan-language terms are given only where
they are real, sourced words (pō "night/void," lagi "sky/heaven," Pulotu the
spirit-world, tatau "tattoo," ʻava "kava," tagata "people," toa "ironwood,"
fafine/tane "woman/man," etc.). Where a detail is variant or single-witness it is
flagged in the notes.

FIGURES AUTHORED (11)
---------------------
1.  samoan_tagaloa      — Tagaloa / Tagaloalagi / Tagaloa-faʻatutupu-nuʻu, the
                          self-existent supreme creator who stood alone in the
                          primordial Expanse and made the heavens, the islands, the
                          sea, and humankind. [deity, primordial]
2.  samoan_saveasiuleo  — Saveasiʻuleo, the half-man/half-eel Aliʻi (lord) of Pulotu,
                          the spirit-world; brother of Ulufanuaseseʻe; father of
                          Nafanua, who devoured his offspring until Nafanua was
                          hidden from him. [deity, mythic]
3.  samoan_nafanua      — Nafanua, the great war-goddess of Pulotu; daughter of
                          Saveasiʻuleo and Tilafaiga; wielder of the four toa-wood
                          war-clubs; her prophecy to Malietoa Faʻiga "Faʻatali i lagi
                          se ao o lou mālō." [deity, mythic] parentIds:
                          [samoan_saveasiuleo, samoan_tilafaiga].
4.  samoan_mafuie       — Mafuiʻe, the earthquake-god of the land of perpetual fire
                          beneath the earth, from whom Tiʻitiʻi (the Samoan Māui)
                          wrests fire and breaks off one arm. [deity, mythic]
5.  samoan_sina         — Sina, the woman of "Sina ma le Tuna" (Sina and the Eel),
                          the coconut-origin myth: the slain eel's buried head grows
                          into the first coconut tree. [mortal, mythic]
6.  samoan_losi         — Losi, the fisherman/demigod who raids Tagaloa's heaven and
                          carries the taro (and other food-plants) down to earth.
                          [demigod, mythic-prehistoric]
7.  samoan_tilafaiga    — Tilafaiga, one of the conjoined twin goddesses who brought
                          the tatau (tattooing) from Fiji; also the mother of
                          Nafanua. [deity, mythic] (twin of Taema; mother-of Nafanua)
8.  samoan_taema        — Taema, the conjoined twin of Tilafaiga; co-bringer of the
                          tatau from Fiji. [deity, mythic] (twin of Tilafaiga)
9.  samoan_lefee        — Le Feʻe, the octopus war-god of Aʻana and Vaimauga, whose
                          voice is thunder; honoured at the stone "Fale o le Feʻe."
                          [deity, mythic]
10. samoan_moso         — Moso, a high god of the land/earth (set against Tagaloa of
                          the heavens), a widely-invoked village aitu. [deity, mythic]
11. samoan_pava         — Pava, the man at whose house the first ʻava (kava) was
                          drunk; his son, slain for disrespect, was restored to life
                          by the libation — the charter of the ʻava ceremony.
                          [mortal, mythic-prehistoric]

RELATION WIRING NOTES (all within this fresh batch — no cross-tradition edges)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Parent→child edges use the directed kinds "father-of" / "mother-of"; child→parent
  edges use "father" / "mother" (non-symmetric).
- Saveasiʻuleo is father-of Nafanua; Tilafaiga is mother-of Nafanua. Nafanua carries
  the reciprocal "father"/"mother" edges and parentIds [samoan_saveasiuleo,
  samoan_tilafaiga].
- TYPE NOTE: Nafanua is the child of two deities (Saveasiʻuleo and Tilafaiga), so by
  the registry's tier-computation a child of two deities computes to `deity`; she is
  therefore typed "deity" even though the popular literature loosely calls her a
  "demigod."
- Tilafaiga and Taema are conjoined twin sisters: a reciprocal "twin sibling" edge is
  wired BOTH directions within the batch.
- Saveasiʻuleo's brother Ulufanuaseseʻe is NOT authored as a separate figure (he is a
  minor narrative foil with no independent cult); the brother-bond is recorded in
  Saveasiʻuleo's notes, not as a relation edge to an unauthored id.
- Pava's mythic counterpart in the ʻava charter is Tagaloa-ʻUi (the first high chief,
  a distinct figure from the supreme creator Tagaloa-lagi authored here as
  samoan_tagaloa); to avoid conflation, no genealogical edge is drawn from Pava to
  samoan_tagaloa. Pava and his (unnamed) son therefore have no in-batch kin — see
  SOLITARY flag.

SOLITARY:
- samoan_tagaloa — The supreme creator is self-existent, alone in the primordial Expanse (Pō / Ilu) before there was sky, sea, or land; he has no parents or progenitors in the cosmogony (J. Fraser, 'The Samoan Story of Creation,' JPS 1, 1892; G. Turner, Samoa, 1884). His descendants (the lesser Tagaloa, Tagaloa-ʻUi, etc.) are not authored in this core batch, so no child-edges are drawn from him.
- samoan_mafuie — The earthquake-and-fire god is attested only as the solitary keeper of the subterranean fire, with no parents, consort, or offspring in the corpus used here (G. Turner, Samoa, 1884; J. B. Stair, Old Samoa, 1897).
- samoan_sina — The heroine of the coconut-origin myth Sina ma le Tuna is attested in that tale without divine kin; her relation is to the eel Tuna (a transformed Fijian king), not to any figure in this batch (G. Turner, Samoa, 1884; A. Krämer, Die Samoa-Inseln, 1902/03).
- samoan_losi — The fisherman-demigod is given no fixed parentage in the corpus used here; variant traditions loosely make him a descendant of Tagaloa, but no firm genealogical edge is attested for the core batch (G. Turner, Samoa, 1884; J. B. Stair, Old Samoa, 1897).
- samoan_lefee — The octopus war-god is attested as a solitary district war-power with no parents, consort, or offspring in the sources (G. Turner, Samoa, 1884; J. B. Stair, 'The temple of the Feʻe,' JPS, 1894).
- samoan_moso — The high earth-god / village aitu Moso is attested as a widely-invoked tutelary power without a genealogy in the corpus used here (G. Turner, Samoa, 1884; J. B. Stair, Old Samoa, 1897).
- samoan_pava — The host of the first ʻava has no attested divine kin; his mythic counterpart is Tagaloa-ʻUi (the first high chief, distinct from the supreme creator Tagaloa authored here) and his son is unnamed, so no in-batch kin-edges are drawn (J. B. Stair, Old Samoa, 1897; G. Turner, Samoa, 1884).
