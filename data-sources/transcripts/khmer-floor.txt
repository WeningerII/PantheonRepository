================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added five well-attested, individuated Khmer figures absent from the existing founder-myth set: the paired dance-drama divinities Moni Mekhala (goddess of sea and lightning) and Ream Eyso (the thunder storm-giant), wired to each other as cosmic antagonists whose danced pursuit explains the monsoon; Preah Pisnukar, the Khmer form of the divine architect Vishvakarman and legendary builder of Angkor Wat in the 17th-century Poem of Angkor Wat; Preah Thorani (Neang Konghing), the earth goddess who wrings the flood from her hair to defeat Mara; and Yeay Mao, the named warrior-heroine venerated as the great tutelary neak ta of the southern coast. Each rests on at least two real scholarly sources (Cravath 2007; Shapiro-Phim 1994; Jacob 1996; Pou; Guthrie 2004; Ang Choulean 1986/1995; Forest 1992). Thorani and Pisnukar are Indic receptions but are strongly individuated in the Khmer record with distinct native names, iconography, and cults, so they qualify; the three unpartnered figures are documented as standing alone and are placed in solitary with cited reasons. I stopped at five rather than padding with legendary kings (e.g. Ketumala) or unnamed spirit-classes, which lack the individuation or cannot be cleanly related under the allowed relation kinds.
