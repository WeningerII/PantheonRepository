================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added three genuinely individuated, named Kunwinjku ancestral beings that scholarship documents beyond the existing five: Nadulmi, the kangaroo (black wallaroo) ancestor and leader of the Wubarr ceremony (Taylor 1996; Holmes 1992); Namanjwarre, the estuarine crocodile ancestor and guardian of the sacred objects of the Mardayin ceremony (Holmes 1992; National Gallery of Australia); and Nawura, the male creator-ancestor of the sandstone 'stone country' (Nganjmirra 1997; NGA collection records). Nadulmi and Namanjwarre are wired to each other as companions because both are principal totemic ancestors of the interlinked Wubarr/Mardayin ceremonial cycle depicted together in Yirawala's work, while Nawura stands alone as no kin or personal relation to other figures is attested. I deliberately excluded spirit-classes that are not individuated named beings — the Yawkyawk/Ngalkunburriyaymi water spirits, Mimih, and Namande — and did not conflate the antilopine-kangaroo term kandakidj with the proper-named Nadulmi, keeping the set to well-attested individuals and treating the record as effectively exhausted for further individuated figures.
