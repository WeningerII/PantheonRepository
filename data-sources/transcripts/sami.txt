Pantheon Registry — Sámi (Saami) pantheon, mythic core: the Áhkká household goddesses, the wind-man, the creator's son, and the death-rider
=====================================================================================================================================

Tradition: Sámi (the Saami of Sápmi — northern Fennoscandia and the Kola Peninsula).
Era key used: mythic (the single registered Sámi era).

Scope of this batch. The corpus already carries six Sámi figures — the sun-mother
(sami_beaivi), the thunder-god Horagalles (sami_horagalles), the death-goddess
Jábmeáhkká (sami_jabmeahkka), the alder-man/hunt-god Leib-olmai (sami_leib_olmai),
the moon-god Mano (sami_mano) and the high creator Radien-attje (sami_radien_attje).
The conspicuous gap is the great female complex of the Áhkká ("old women / goddesses")
who govern conception, gestation and birth — the mother Máttaráhkká and her three
daughters Sáráhkká, Juoksáhkká and Uksáhkká — together with the wind-man Bieggolmai,
the creator's active son Radien-pardne, and the death- and disease-god Ruohtta (Ruto).
This batch adds seven firmly-attested figures:

  • sami_mattarahkka — Máttaráhkká (Maderakka), the ancestral mother-goddess who dwells
                       beneath the floor of the goahti; she receives the soul sent down
                       from the upper world and clothes it with a body, then passes the
                       child to her daughters. Mother of the three Áhkká.
  • sami_sarahkka    — Sáráhkká, the most beloved of the goddesses, who dwells under the
                       hearth; she of birth, menstruation, sexuality and the growing flesh
                       of the child in the womb. Wood was cloven and porridge eaten in her
                       honour after a successful birth.
  • sami_juoksahkka  — Juoksáhkká, the "bow-woman," guardian of children, who if duly
                       petitioned could turn a girl-child in the womb into a boy.
  • sami_uksahkka    — Uksáhkká, the "door-woman," who dwells by the door of the goahti,
                       guards those who pass in and out, receives the newborn into the
                       world and protects the child learning to walk.
  • sami_bieggolmai  — Bieggolmai / Biegga-olmai, the "wind-man," who shovels the winds
                       into and out of their cave with his two shovels; lord of wind,
                       weather and sea, one of the deities of the noaidi drum.
  • sami_radien_pardne — Radien-pardne, the active son of the passive high creator
                       Radien-attje (and of Radien-akka), who carries out his father's
                       will in the world.
  • sami_ruohtta     — Ruohtta (Ruto), the disease- and death-god who rides on horseback
                       — alone among Sámi powers — spreading sickness; ruler of the
                       underworld Rotaimo, appeased by the sacrifice (transfer) of a horse.

Sources and attestation. These figures are documented in the early ethnographic and
missionary record and in the standard handbooks: Lars Levi Læstadius, Fragments of
Lappish Mythology (manuscript 1838–1845; ed. Pentikäinen, trans. Vähämäki, 2002),
who emphasised the "more Lappish" character of the birth-giving Áhkká; Uno Holmberg
(Harva), Finno-Ugric [and Siberian] Mythology / the Lapp sections (1927); Håkan Rydving,
The End of Drum-Time: Religious Change among the Lule Saami, 1670s–1740s (1993); the
earlier sources Læstadius, Holmberg and Rydving themselves draw on (Tornæus, Skanke,
Jessen, Leem, Friis); and the noaidi drum-iconography (Manker, Die lappische
Zaubertrommel, 1938–50), on which a group of three Áhkká with their mother is one of
the most common female motifs. The three named daughters are a Western-Sápmi
elaboration: in the eastern (Kola / Finnish) area the daughters are not distinguished,
and Máttaráhkká alone (or merged with Sáráhkká) is found. Everything below is
attested in these works; nothing is invented. All entries are classed as secondary
attestation, since the Sámi tradition is known only through outside observers and the
later scholarly record.

A note on relations. The conception-process the sources describe gives a natural,
cited chain: the soul is sent from the upper world by Radien (Veralden-radien /
Radien-attje), Máttaráhkká gives it a body, Sáráhkká places it in the mother and grows
its flesh, Juoksáhkká and Uksáhkká attend the birth and the newborn. The three
daughters are wired with parentIds ["sami_mattarahkka"] and reciprocal mother/sibling
edges; Radien-pardne with parentIds ["sami_radien_attje"]. Symmetric edges toward the
existing figure sami_radien_attje are flagged RECIPROCAL-NEEDED below.
