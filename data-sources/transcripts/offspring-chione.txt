================================================================================
RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISION
================================================================================

Per Ovid (Metamorphoses 11.301–317), Chione — the mortal daughter of Daedalion of
Phocis — was loved on the same day by both Hermes and Apollo, and bore heteropaternal
twins on the same night: AUTOLYCUS (by Hermes, "skilled in theft, a crafty wight … to
make of white things black, and black things white") and PHILAMMON (by Apollo, "famous
for the lyre and song"). The corpus already records this in the notes of greek_chione,
which carries external-ref placeholders for both twins and a "// ─── Autolycus ───"
comment-stub in the demigod block of app/data.js; NEITHER twin is registered as a
standalone figure (confirmed by grep: no id greek_autolycus / greek_philammon, and no
transcript authoring them). Both are therefore authored here.

TYPE BY DESCENT (per the anti-inversion rule). Each twin = one Olympian father (deity,
fraction 1.0) + Chione (mortal, fraction 0). Divinity fraction = (1.0 + 0)/2 = 0.5,
which falls in the demigod band (0.375–0.75). Both twins = DEMIGOD. era = heroic-age
(the cluster era). Both die in the tradition, so both carry a lifecycle.

AUTOLYCUS — the master-thief of Parnassus. Homer (Odyssey 19.394–398) makes him the
father of Odysseus's mother Anticlea and says he "excelled all men in thievery and the
oath," gifts won from Hermes himself; at Od. 19.399–412 he comes to Ithaca, dandles the
newborn on his knees and names him Odysseus ("child of wrath"); later the boy is gashed
by the boar on Parnassus while hunting with Autolycus's sons (Od. 19.413–466). Ovid
(Met. 11.313–315) gives the shape-shifting/colour-changing theft-craft. Apollodorus
(2.4.9) names Autolycus as the man who taught Heracles to wrestle. He is the stock
ancestor-of-cunning: grandfather of Odysseus through Anticlea, and (in some lines)
of Jason through Polymede. Authored as DEMIGOD; wired to greek_chione + greek_hesiod_hermes
as parents; one-way relations to the already-registered Anticlea (father-of) and Odysseus
(grandfather/named-by); reciprocal twin-sibling to greek_philammon (in-batch).

PHILAMMON — the master-singer. Son of Apollo and Chione (Pherecydes FGrH 3 F 120;
Apollodorus 1.3.3; the same-night birth in Ovid Met. 11.316–317 and Hyginus Fab. 161,
200). Pausanias (10.7.2) ranks him the second victor (after Chrysothemis of Crete, before
his own son Thamyris) in the most ancient Pythian song-contest, the hymn-to-Apollo nome;
he founded the choruses of maidens who sang at Delphi the births of Leto, Artemis and
Apollo (Pherecydes; Pollux 4.7), and some made him the inventor of choral music. Pausanias
(2.37.2) reports — "though I do not believe it" — the tradition that he founded the
Lernaean Mysteries; and (9.36.2) that he fell defending the sanctuary of Apollo at Delphi
against the raiding Phlegyans, killed in the battle. By the Parnassian nymph Argiope he
fathered Thamyris (Pausanias 4.33.3, 10.7.2; Apollodorus 1.3.3). Authored as DEMIGOD;
wired to greek_chione + greek_hesiod_apollo as parents; reciprocal twin-sibling to
greek_autolycus (in-batch). Thamyris and Argiope are not registered, so no edge is wired
to them (the Thamyris paternity is recorded in notes only).
