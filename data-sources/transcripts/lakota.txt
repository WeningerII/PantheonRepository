Figure-authoring batch — the Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka theology of the Sioux/Lakota. The
existing corpus held only the "Fallen Star / Star Husband" lineage (star_man,
tapun_sa_win, fallen_star); the structured pantheon recorded by James R. Walker
was entirely absent. This batch adds the well-attested core: the four Superior
Gods (Íŋyaŋ the Rock, Maká the Earth, Škáŋ the Sky, Wí the Sun), the four
Associate Gods (Haŋwí the Moon, Tȟaté the Wind, Wakíŋyaŋ the Winged/Thunder,
Wóȟpe the Falling Star/Mediator — identified by the sources with White Buffalo
Calf Woman), plus Íŋyaŋ's first-born son Iktómi (the Spider, formerly Ksá the
god of wisdom) and his water-monster antagonist Uŋktéhi. Genealogy is wired per
Walker: Íŋyaŋ→Iktómi and Škáŋ→Wóȟpe, Wí⇄Haŋwí (estranged spouses), and
Wakíŋyaŋ⇄Uŋktéhi (eternal enemies). Sources: J. R. Walker, "The Sun Dance and
Other Ceremonies of the Oglala Division of the Teton Dakota" (AMNH Anthropological
Papers 16, 1917) and "Lakota Belief and Ritual" (ed. R. J. DeMallie & E. A.
Jahner, 1980) and "Lakota Myth" (ed. Jahner, 1983); corroborated by W. K. Powers,
"Oglala Religion" (1977). Only the widely published cosmology is used.

Ten figures completing the well-attested core of the Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka theology — the
four Superior Gods (Íŋyaŋ, Maká, Škáŋ, Wí), the four Associate Gods (Haŋwí,
Tȟaté, Wakíŋyaŋ, Wóȟpe), Iktómi, and Uŋktéhi — wired by the Walker genealogy
(Íŋyaŋ→Iktómi; Škáŋ→Wóȟpe; Wí⇄Haŋwí; Wakíŋyaŋ⇄Uŋktéhi) and sourced to Walker's
1917 Sun Dance, Lakota Belief and Ritual (1980), Lakota Myth (1983), and Powers'
Oglala Religion (1977). Wóȟpe is represented, per the sources, as identical with
White Buffalo Calf Woman (Ptesáŋ Wíŋ).
