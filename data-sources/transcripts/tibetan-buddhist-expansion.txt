================================================================================
THE GAP — NINE RECORDS, AND EVERY ONE OF THEM A GUARD AT THE DOOR
================================================================================

The Tibetan Buddhist key held **nine records**. Here is all of them:

    Pehar Gyalpo        oath-bound protector of Samye and Nechung
    Palden Lhamo        protectress of Tibet and of the Dalai Lamas
    Begtse              armoured war god, bound at Altan Khan's court
    Dorje Legpa         oath-bound protector of the termas
    Za Rahula           planetary protector of the mind-revelations
    Dorje Shugden       sectarian protector
    Tsiu Marpo          monastery guardian
    Tashi Tseringma     chief of the Five Long-Life Sisters
    Shinje              Yama Dharmarāja, lord of death

**Every single one is a wrathful protector.** Nine records, one category.

There is no Padmasambhava — **the figure Tibet calls the second Buddha, and the
man who bound four of those nine under oath in the first place.** There is no
yidam: no Yamāntaka, no Cakrasaṃvara, no Hevajra, no Guhyasamāja, no Kālacakra,
no Vajrayoginī, no Vajrakīlaya. There is no Vajradhara, the buddha every tantric
lineage of the new schools traces itself back to. There is no Tārā in any of her
Tibetan forms, no Yeshe Tsogyal, no Machig Labdrön. There is no Gesar. There is
no king — not Nyatri Tsenpo who came down on the cord, not Drigum Tsenpo whose
cord was cut, not Songtsen Gampo who pinned the demoness. **There is no monkey
and no ogress**, and the Tibetans' account of where the Tibetans came from is
not in the corpus at all.

Lane 26 found a world religion filed entirely through the beings on its margins.
**This is the same fault rotated ninety degrees: a tradition filed entirely
through its doorkeepers.** The corpus held the ones who guard the shrine and
nobody who is in it.

================================================================================
THE SHARPEST SINGLE CASE: TWO OF THREE, AND THREE OF FIVE
================================================================================

The Nyingma school names **three root protectors**: Ekajaṭī, who guards the
mantras; Za Rāhula, who guards the mind-revelations; and Dorje Legpa, who guards
the termas.

**The corpus held Za Rahula. The corpus held Dorje Legpa. The corpus did not
hold Ekajaṭī.** Two of three — and the missing one is the first-named.

The **Five Long-Life Sisters** are a fixed set of five, bound by Padmasambhava
and made protectors of the Kagyü teachings by Milarepa. The corpus held
**Tashi Tseringma**, their chief, and **none of the other four** — including
**Miyolangsangma, who lives on the summit of Everest.**

This programme has now found "holds one of a set and not the set" in Welsh,
Anglo-Saxon, Hurrian and Buddhist material. Here it found it twice in one key of
nine records.

================================================================================
THE LINE THIS LANE DRAWS, STATED SO IT NEED NOT BE DERIVED A FOURTH TIME
================================================================================

Three lanes have now hit the same question: what to do with a figure who was a
historical person and whose surviving transmission is legend. Lane 23 left it
open with Trdat III, lane 24 with Jumong's successors, lane 26 with Nāgārjuna —
while lane 26 nevertheless ingested **Śākyamuni**, who is that same kind of
figure.

The line the corpus has in fact been drawing, stated here plainly:

> **A figure is ingested when the tradition's own transmission of them is mythic
> narrative** — a birth without parents, a descent on a cord, a binding of
> spirits, an emanation identified — **and blocked when the retrievable material
> is a dated biography with legend attached.**

By that line **Padmasambhava** (born from a lotus, departs without dying),
**Yeshe Tsogyal**, **Mandarava**, **Nyatri Tsenpo**, **Drigum Tsenpo**,
**Songtsen Gampo** and **Trisong Detsen** (all transmitted as emanations, in
narratives about binding land-spirits) are in. **Milarepa, Marpa, Naropa,
Tsongkhapa, Atiśa, Machig Labdrön, Longchenpa and the Karmapas are blocked** —
each has a dated life and a literature about it, and each belongs to the class
the owner should rule on rather than a lane.

**This is a stance, not a licence.** It is recorded here and in the census so
that the owner can overturn it in one place.

================================================================================
THE THREE TIBETAN KEYS
================================================================================

The corpus files Tibetan religion under **three** tradition keys:

    Tibetan Buddhist (9)   the nine protectors above
    Bön (6)                Shenlha Ökar, Satrig Ersang, Sangpo Bumtri,
                           Sipé Gyalmo, Gekhö, and Tönpa Shenrab
    Tibetan Bön (2)        Nyenchen Tanglha and Yul-lha

and the id prefixes do not follow the keys: `tibetan_tonpa_shenrab` sits under
`Bön`, `tibetan_nyenchen_tanglha` under `Tibetan Bön`, `tibetan_palden_lhamo`
and `tibetan_pehar` under `Tibetan Buddhist` beside six `tibetan_buddhist_*`
ids. **Nyenchen Tanglha, filed under Tibetan Bön, is a mountain god bound under
oath by Padmasambhava** — which is to say the boundary between the keys does not
track anything in the material.

**This batch does not merge or rename anything.** Every record it adds goes under
`Tibetan Buddhist`, edges point at the existing records wherever the sources
support them regardless of which key they sit in, and the key question is
escalated. It is the fifth lane to raise tradition-key duplication, after
Finnish/Karelian, Korean/Jeju, Hittite/Hattic and Buddhist/Chinese.

================================================================================
THE SCHEMA PROBLEM LANE 26 ESCALATED, UNCHANGED
================================================================================

`deity | numen | demigod | quartigod | scion | mortal` still has no category for
a buddha, a bodhisattva or a yidam. A yidam is not a god the practitioner
petitions; it is a form the practitioner takes. The batch assigns `deity` because
it is the nearest category the vocabulary has, and **every affected record says
so on its face**. Nothing here is decided that lane 26 did not already escalate.

The single registered era, `mythic`, is likewise unchanged and likewise coarse:
Vajradhara, who is outside time, and Trisong Detsen, who reigned in the eighth
century, are filed in the same bucket. Lane 22's test still applies — a label is
extended when it *contradicts* its material, not when it is merely broad.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BATCH DOES NOT INGEST
================================================================================

The **Eight Herukas** (kagyé), the **Twenty-One Tārās**, the **Thirty-Five
Confession Buddhas**, the **Eighty-Four Mahāsiddhas** and the **Twelve Tenma
Goddesses** are **collectives and series**; individuals are ingested where the
sources give them an act or a named station. The **Thirteen Geomantic Temples**,
**Samye**, **Nechung**, the **Jokhang**, **Mount Kailash** and the
**Copper-Coloured Mountain** are **places** — Chomolungma is a place too, and
Miyolangsangma, who lives on it, is not. **Terma** are texts. The **nāgas**,
**tsen**, **gyalpo** and **mamo** are **classes of spirit**, not figures.
