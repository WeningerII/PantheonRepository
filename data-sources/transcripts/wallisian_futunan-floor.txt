================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added four individuated figures documented for the Polynesian outliers of Futuna and 'Uvea (Wallis) and genealogically tied to the existing Fakavelikele: Finelasi (tutelary goddess of Futuna, dwelling on Mount Puke and known on Wallis as Kakahu); Malafulafu (protector-god of nature, animal-shifting, holder of a 'stone of life and death'); and the founding ancestral couple Mago and Tafaleata, said to have come from Samoa in a coconut and to have engendered Fakavelikele, Finelasi and Malafulafu. These are attested in Burrows' Ethnology of Futuna (1936), Guiot's 2000 study of Futunan sacred forests (Journal de la Société des Océanistes), Mayer's 1973 survey of Wallisian-Futunan oral tradition, and Abbal's 'Les mythes de Wallis et Futuna', and are distinct from the separately-corpus'd Samoan, Tongan and general Polynesian material. I stopped at four: repeated searches of Burrows' Ethnology of Uvea and the ethnographic literature surfaced no further individuated, named 'Uvea-specific deity beyond Kakahu/Finelasi — the remaining Wallisian religious record consists of unnamed atua tanutanu (deified chiefs) and the imported Tui Tokelau cult, neither of which yields new distinctly-Wallisian named individuals I could cite responsibly.
