================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added four genuinely individuated, well-attested figures beyond the existing five. Lageiki (Laga-iki) is the fifth of the five tupua whom the corpus was missing; he is wired into the pantheon as one of the migrant gods led by Huanaki. Tilalofonua (god of the rat/kuma) and Halevao (god of the flying fox/peka) form the paired protagonists of Loeb's wing-lending tale and are reciprocally related to each other as new figures. Mataginifale, the celebrated whale-borne culture-heroine and patroness of women's crafts, stands genealogically apart from the tupua and is recorded as solitary with a cited reason. All figures rest on at least two of Loeb (1926), Smith (1902–03) and Craig (1989); I checked for further named individuals (e.g. a distinct Niuean Maui, or the reported "Levmatangi") but found no scholarly attestation individuating them, so I stopped at these four.
