================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added four genuinely individuated, well-attested Luo figures missing from the corpus: Podho, the eponymous progenitor whom Ogot calls 'the Adam of the Luo' and who is wired to the existing Ramogi via a father-of relation; Owiny Sigoma, the legendary warrior-leader of the Jok-Owiny and founder of the Karuoth royal clan; Gor Mahia, the celebrated magician-chief and seer of Kanyamwa; and Nyamgondho wuod Ombare, the canonical fisher-hero of the lakeshore moral legend. Each is documented in mainstream scholarship (Ogot's History of the Southern Luo, Ochieng's Outline History of Nyanza, Cohen and Atieno Odhiambo's Siaya, and the Luo oral-literature anthologies of Onyango-Ogutu and Roscoe and of Miruka). I deliberately excluded the Shilluk figure Nyikang and the Anuak/Acholi Jok material, since those Northern and Central Luo religious systems are catalogued under their own traditions and folding them into this Kenyan-Luo (Joluo) set would conflate distinct corpora; I also excluded place-legends and unnamed spirits (for example the anonymous 'lady of the lake') that lack an individuated named being.
