REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added four genuinely individuated figures missing from the corpus: the first man Andriambahomanana and first woman Andriamahilala, whose contrasting death-choices (the banana that sprouts anew, and the moon that wanes and returns) constitute the canonical Malagasy origin-of-death myth (Ruud 1960; Scheub 2000; Sibree 1896); the incarnate son of Zanahary, Andrianerinerina, who descended at Anerinerina, was stranded by a food taboo, and became the divine ancestor of the Merina royal line (Delivré 1974; Ottino 1982, 1983); and the landscape-shaping giant Darafify/Rapeto of the eastern tradition (Renel 1910; Sibree 1896). The first couple are wired to one another as spouses and both subordinated to the existing Zanahary, while Andrianerinerina is placed as Zanahary's child, integrating the new figures into the standing pantheon. I stopped at four because the remaining named beings I checked are either non-individuated (generic Vazimba, unnamed ancestors) or are mere place-eponyms (the giant's wives Rasoabe and Rasoamasay, personifications of lakes) that lack the sustained scholarly attestation these four carry.
