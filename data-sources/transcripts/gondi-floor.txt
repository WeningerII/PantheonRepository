================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added four genuinely individuated figures beyond the existing five. Raitad Jango (the first priestess and chief goddess of the sagas, sister of Lingo) and Hira Suka Patalir (the kingri-playing bard who freed the imprisoned gods and was crushed by the boulder) are the two named actors of the Kachargarh deliverance epic distinct from Lingo and Kali Kankali, documented in Kangali's Gondi Punem Darshan (1986), Patankar's Seminar essay (2021) and Sahapedia. Narayan Deo, the Gond sun-god of the Laru Kaj pig sacrifice, is attested in Russell & Hira Lal (1916) and Fuchs (1960); Nagoba, the serpent Persa Pen of the Mesram Raj Gonds at Keslapur, is documented at length by Fürer-Haimendorf (1948, 1979). New figures are wired to the existing pantheon (Patalir subordinate to Lingo; Narayan Deo and Nagoba to the Persa Pen clan-god node) and to each other (Jango and Patalir as allies). I deliberately excluded generic spirit-classes (the 33 Saga Deva, village-mothers) and the syncretic Shiva-figure Shembhu, as none is individuated Gond material.

The rites, documented in detail by the anthropologist Christoph von Fürer-Haimendorf, open with the Mesram priests fetching water and pots from the Godavari; the god's worship is bound to the clan's identity and its assembly, and Fürer-Haimendorf himself helped institutionalise the accompanying durbar in the 1940s. As a specifically named clan-deity Nagoba individuates the generic Persa Pen cult already represented in the corpus."
