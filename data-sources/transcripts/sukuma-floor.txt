REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added the two named protagonists of the central Sukuma myth — the all-swallowing ogre Shing'weng'we and the clever hero Masala Kulangwa (Healey & Sybertz 1996: 64-65; Tertrais 2000; Sybertz & the Ndoleleji Research Committee, Tears of Joy, 2006) — and the four individuated founders of the Sukuma dynastic tradition: the hunter-pioneer Ilembo, the leader Nkanda whose utterance 'nsukumala aha' named the Sukuma, the ancestress Minza, and Sanga, the first ntemi of Sukumalaha (Itandala 1975 and 1979; Sukuma Museum, 'Sukuma Chiefs and Royal History'; Cory 1951). I deliberately did not author Lyangombe, the spirit whose disciples the Sukuma Baswezi society are (Cory, 'The Buswezi', American Anthropologist 57, 1955), because he already stands in the corpus as banyaruanda_ryangombe and re-authoring him under Sukuma would create a cross-tradition duplicate. Beyond these, the ethnographic record (Millroth, Lyuba: Traditional Religion of the Sukuma, 1965; Tanner 1959; Cory 1953) presents Sukuma religion as the creator Liwelelo plus unindividuated classes of ancestor spirits (masamva) and society spirits, so no further named, individuated beings qualified; the Nyamwezi corpus figures (Likube, Limatunda, Limi, Liwelelo) were not cross-duplicated.
