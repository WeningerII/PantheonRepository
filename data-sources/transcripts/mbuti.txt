PANTHEON REGISTRY — FIGURE-AUTHORING TRANSCRIPT
Tradition: Mbuti (Bambuti / Efe) — the forest religion of the Mbuti and Efe
hunter-gatherers of the Ituri rainforest, Democratic Republic of the Congo.
ID prefix: mbuti_
Eras (registered, exact): "primordial", "mythic"
  - "primordial" = the creator / sky gods (cosmic, timeless)
  - "mythic"     = the origin-of-death narratives and forest-being tales

================================================================================
RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISION
================================================================================

The principal ethnographic sources for Mbuti/Efe forest religion are Paul Schebesta,
*Die Bambuti-Pygmäen vom Ituri* (3 vols., 1938–48) and *Revisiting My Pygmy Hosts*
(Eng. tr. 1936), and Colin M. Turnbull, *The Forest People* (1961) and *Wayward
Servants: The Two Worlds of the African Pygmies* (1965). Jean-Pierre Hallet's *Pygmy
Kitabu* (1973) is a popularising account and is treated cautiously here; where it is
the only support for a claim that is noted, and Schebesta/Turnbull are preferred for
cited data. Patrick Putnam's field notes and the encyclopaedic syntheses (Mircea
Eliade, ed., *Encyclopedia of Religion*, "Pygmy Religions"; *Encyclopedia.com*) are
used as secondary corroboration. The figures' folklore-handbook profiles (e.g. the
"Khonvoum"/"Tore"/"Arebati" entries summarising Schebesta) are cited as tertiary.

A METHODOLOGICAL CAUTION is recorded throughout. Schebesta, a Catholic priest working
under Wilhelm Schmidt's *Urmonotheismus* ("primitive monotheism") programme, tended to
systematise the scattered Mbuti/Efe god-names into a single high creator. Turnbull
(1965) explicitly disagreed: among the net-hunting Mbuti he found no anthropomorphic
creator-god but a benevolent divinity simply IDENTIFIED WITH THE FOREST itself (ndura),
addressed as mother, father, sibling and lover. A more recent critique (Ondřej Havelka /
others, "How to make up the creator-god of the Efe…", arguing that the unified supreme
being is partly an artefact of monotheistic projection) sharpens the point. Accordingly:
the divine NAMES and their core mythemes (the rainbow-bow, the remaking of the sun, the
fire-theft and decree of death, the clay-and-skin creation of the first human) are
firmly attested in the record and are authored; but the corpus does NOT over-assert a
tidy single pantheon or invented genealogical links between these names. Where bands
equate two names (Arebati ≈ Tore) or keep them distinct, both readings are cited and
neither is forced.

FOUR firmly-attested, individuated figures are authored.

1) KHONVOUM (Khonuum / Kmvoum / Khvum / Chorum) — the sky creator and "great hunter."
   Carries a bow made of two snakes that appears to people as the rainbow; at sunset he
   gathers fragments of the stars and throws them at the sun so it rises renewed; created
   humankind from clay of three colours (black and white people from black and white clay,
   the Pygmies from red clay); communicates with people through the elephant Gor (the
   thunderer) and through a chameleon. Authored as a DEITY, era "primordial". (Schebesta,
   with the standard handbook summaries.)

2) TORE (Tooro) — the forest god and lord of the animals/game; a thunder/storm god who
   appears in the storm, hides in the rainbow, and shows himself as a leopard in the boys'
   initiation. In the fire-theft narrative the first Pygmies steal fire from Tore; he gives
   chase but cannot catch them, and on returning home finds his (old) mother dead. In grief
   and anger he decrees that humankind too shall die — and so becomes the god of the dead,
   to whom souls return. Authored as a DEITY, era "mythic", with a LIFECYCLE phase recording
   his transition to death-god at the fire-theft. (Schebesta; Eliade/Encyclopedia of
   Religion; with Turnbull's dissent — the forest itself as god — recorded in notes.)

3) AREBATI — among the Efe, the moon/creator/sky god who fashioned the first human, Baatsi,
   from clay, covered him with skin and filled the skin with blood; the Moon is his witness,
   reporting the eating of the forbidden Tahu-tree fruit that brings death into the world. He
   is authored as DISTINCT because the Efe creation cycle (Baatsi, the Tahu tree, the moon-
   witness) is a recognisably separate strand from Tore's forest/fire material and from
   Khonvoum's sky-hunter material — while noting explicitly that some bands equate Arebati
   with Tore. Authored as a DEITY, era "primordial". (Schebesta; Oxford/Encyclopedia
   summaries of the Efe creation.)

4) NEGOOGUNOGUMBAR — the child-eating forest giant/ogre of Mbuti tale, a cautionary monster
   said to devour children who stray; popularly linked to the giant forest hog. Authored as a
   NUMEN (a being/spirit, not a high god). Attestation is THIN and largely folkloric-
   compendium rather than primary-ethnographic; this is stated plainly in the figure's notes.

CULT, NOT A FIGURE. The molimo — the sacred wooden (sometimes bamboo) trumpet and the
forest festival of the same name, sung to "wake the forest" at a death or crisis (Turnbull,
*The Forest People*) — is a CULT practice and instrument, not a personified figure. It is
recorded under materialCulture on Tore (the death-god of the forest cult to which the molimo
death-festival belongs), not authored as its own entry.

WIRING DECISION (accuracy-first). The Mbuti/Efe material does NOT supply secure, individually
named divine genealogies among these four names; the relationships actually attested are
EQUATION/IDENTIFICATION between band-variants (Arebati ≈ Tore; the forest ≈ "god"), not
parent/child or sibling descent. No blood-kin parentIds are therefore asserted. The one
firmly-attested cross-figure relation authored is the symmetric "identified with"/syncretic
link between AREBATI and TORE (the band-level equation), reciprocated within this batch as an
`identified with` relation on both figures. Gor the elephant and the chameleon are Khonvoum's
animal MESSENGERS and are recorded as faculties/materialCulture (not authored as figures, as
they are vehicles rather than individuated persons). All four figures are otherwise flagged
SOLITARY with the cited reason rather than wired to a guessed relative. inheritability is set
to "none" on every faculty: none of these powers is an attested heritable blood/descent power.

FIGURES CONSIDERED BUT NOT AUTHORED (with reasons):
  • THE FOREST / ndura as "God" (Turnbull's net-hunter divinity) — this is the impersonal,
    unindividuated divinity-as-forest of Turnbull's Mbuti, deliberately NOT a named anthropo-
    morphic figure; it is the very thing Turnbull contrasts with Schebesta's named gods. It is
    recorded in the notes (and in Tore's domain/notes) rather than reified into a "figure,"
    because authoring "the forest" as a person would misrepresent the attested theology.
  • BAATSI — the first human/first man of the Efe creation (made of clay by Arebati). Baatsi is
    the primordial MORTAL/ancestor, not a god, and the sources treat him as the created man
    rather than an individuated culture-hero with his own cult; he is recorded inside Arebati's
    creation account (faculty/lifecycle notes) and is deferred rather than authored as a thin
    standalone mortal. (He could be added in a later pass if a mortal-ancestor entry is wanted.)
  • GOR (the elephant, "the thunderer") and the CHAMELEON — Khonvoum's messengers; vehicles of
    divine communication, not individuated deities. Recorded under Khonvoum.
  • MUUNGU / Mungu — a "high deity / greatest of forest gods" name reported for the Bambuti in
    some compendia. This is the wide Bantu *Mulungu/Mungu* supreme-being word almost certainly
    borrowed from neighbouring Bantu farmers, not a securely Mbuti-internal individuated figure;
    to avoid importing a loan-name as a distinct Mbuti god it is NOT authored, only noted here.
  • EPILIPILI — given in some summaries as a third equated creator-name (Tore/Arebati/Epilipili).
    It is attested only as an alternate NAME in the equation, with no separable myth of its own,
    so it is recorded as an alt-name consideration, not authored as a distinct figure.
