Tuamotuan pantheon — the well-attested CORE, authored from scratch (the tradition
was entirely absent from the Pantheon Registry).
====================================================================================

Tradition string: Tuamotuan. New id prefix: tuamotuan_.
Registered era keys (ERA_ORDER['Tuamotuan'] in app/data.js): primordial, mythic,
mythic-prehistoric — and ONLY these three are used below.

The Tuamotu archipelago (the Paumotu/Pa'umotu atolls of eastern French Polynesia,
between the Society Islands and the Marquesas) preserved one of the richest and most
deeply esoteric of the Polynesian theological systems, recorded chiefly on the atolls
of Anaa, Fa'ga'tau (Fagatau), Vahitahi and Fakahina/Hao in the 1920s–30s. This batch
supplies the firmly-attested foundation in three strata: (1) the cosmogonic high-god
Kiho-tumu, "Kiho the Source," the self-existent creator in the void beneath the
foundation of the world (Stimson's esoteric recension); (2) the standard East-Polynesian
creation pair and great gods recorded across the group by Emory — Atea (sky/space) with
his consort Fakahotu (earth/fruitfulness), their son Tane the demiurge, the sea-creator
Takaroa (Tangaroa), and the gods Rongo and Tu; and (3) the demigod heroes of the
firmly-attested cycles — Tahaki the radiant red-skinned hero and Maui-tikitiki the
sun-snarer and fisher of islands — together with Hina and the eel-woman Faumea.

ANCHORING SOURCES (the standard corpus for the Tuamotus)
--------------------------------------------------------
  - J. Frank Stimson, Tuamotuan Religion (Bernice P. Bishop Museum Bulletin 103,
    Honolulu, 1933) — the foundational record of the esoteric theology, the chants,
    and the cult of the supreme god Kiho-tumu.
  - J. Frank Stimson, The Cult of Kiho-tumu (Bernice P. Bishop Museum Bulletin 111,
    Honolulu, 1933) — the dedicated study of the supreme self-existent creator, the
    void beneath the world-foundation, and his creative word.
  - J. Frank Stimson, The Legends of Maui and Tahaki (Bernice P. Bishop Museum
    Bulletin 127, Honolulu, 1934) — the Maui and Tahaki cycles in Tuamotuan and
    English (the Fagatau recension of Fariua-a-Makitua; comparative notes by K. P.
    Emory, chant-music by E. G. Burrows).
  - J. Frank Stimson, Tuamotuan Legends (Island of Anaa), Part I: The Demigods
    (Bernice P. Bishop Museum Bulletin 148, Honolulu, 1937) — the Anaa recensions of
    the Maui, Tahaki, Rata and related demigod cycles.
  - Kenneth P. Emory, "Tuamotuan Concepts of Creation," Journal of the Polynesian
    Society 49 (1940): 69–136 — the decisive comparative account of the Tuamotuan
    cosmogony, the Atea–Fakahotu pair, the Atea–Tane conflict, and the great gods.
  - Kenneth P. Emory, "The Tuamotuan Creation Charts by Paiore," Journal of the
    Polynesian Society 48 (1939): 1–29 — the indigenous genealogical creation-charts
    of Paiore of Anaa.
  - Kenneth P. Emory, "The Tuamotu Legend of Rongo, Son of Vaio," Journal of the
    Polynesian Society 56 (1947): 52–54 — Rongo's dedicated Tuamotuan legend.
  - Martha Warren Beckwith, Hawaiian Mythology (Yale University Press, 1940) — used
    only as a careful secondary digest that quotes and cross-references Stimson's
    Tuamotuan texts (the Maui-tikitiki-a-Ataraga cycle, the Tahaki cycle, the Faumea
    and Te Tuna episodes); never as a primary attestation in place of Stimson/Emory.

VERIFICATION NOTES THAT SHAPED THE DATA (accuracy-first)
--------------------------------------------------------

- TWO RECENSIONS, ONE PANTHEON. The Tuamotuan record holds two partly-distinct
  cosmogonic strata, and I represent the difference rather than fusing them. (a) The
  ESOTERIC recension of Stimson (Bulletins 103, 111) places above and behind everything
  the self-existent KIHO-TUMU, "Kiho the Source," who alone existed in Te Kore, the void
  beneath the foundation (te tumu) of the world, and who by his creative word brought
  forth the cosmos and the gods. (b) The WIDER recension recorded across the group by
  Emory (JPS 1940; Paiore charts, JPS 1939) is the standard East-Polynesian pairing
  cosmogony, leading up to ATEA (the sky-expanse) and his consort FAKAHOTU (earth/
  fruitfulness) as apex-ancestors of mankind, with the great gods Tane, Takaroa, Rongo
  and Tu. Emory himself came to doubt how widespread and ancient the esoteric Kiho cult
  was — the well-known Stimson/Emory disagreement — and I record that scholarly caveat
  in Kiho's notes rather than suppressing either tradition. I do NOT genealogically wire
  Kiho to Atea (the two recensions are not harmonized in the sources), so Kiho is entered
  self-existent and kinless (SOLITARY).

- KIHO-TUMU. "Kiho the Source" (tumu = "source, foundation, origin/trunk"). The supreme
  self-existent creator who dwelt alone in the void beneath the world-foundation and
  created by the spoken/chanted word; his sacred ocean is the Milky Way and the dark rift
  within it his sacred ship, "the Long Shark" (Te Mago-roa) — both well-attested Stimson
  details that populate the material culture. Entered deity / immortal / primordial.
  SOLITARY: self-existent, with neither father nor mother by definition.

- ATEA and FAKAHOTU. Across the Tuamotus Atea (also Vatea; "light, clear space, the
  expanse") is the sky-expanse made the apex-ancestor of mankind, and his consort is
  Fakahotu (Fa'ahotu, "the fructifier; that which causes fruit/produce to form"), the
  earth/fruitfulness. Emory (JPS 1940) records the striking Tuamotuan detail that as
  their first children kept dying, Atea and Fakahotu EXCHANGED SEXES so that the sky
  became the father-mother who suckled the young — represented here in Atea's lifecycle
  and faculties, not as a separate figure. They are wired as spouses, and as the parents
  of Tane (parentIds). Both entered deity / primordial.

- TANE the demiurge. Tane is the active shaper of the visible world and (in the Tuamotu
  telling recorded by Emory) the rebellious young god whom Atea tried to seize, sending a
  host of lesser deities after him; Tane fled across the clouds, and his eating of an
  ancestor in his hunger is given as the origin of cannibalism. He is also one of the
  man-making triad — Tane–Tu–Rongo — who form the first man (the figure made "in the
  image of Tane," of earth fetched by Rongo and shaped by Tu with his spittle). Child of
  two deities (Atea × Fakahotu) → TYPE deity (per the rule: child of two deities → deity).
  Entered deity / mythic; parentIds [atea, fakahotu].

- TAKAROA (Tangaroa). Takaroa is the standard Tuamotuan dialect form of the pan-Polynesian
  Tangaroa, the sea-god and a creator. The firmly-attested Tuamotuan episode is the
  voyage to the island of the eel-woman FAUMEA, who has man-eating eels within her body
  and teaches Takaroa to lure them out; she bears him two sons, Tu-nui-ka-rere (also
  Rata-nui) and Turi-a-Faumea (Beckwith 1940, quoting the Tuamotuan text; the sons are
  recorded in parentRoles/notes, not authored as padding). Takaroa ↔ Faumea wired as
  spouses. Entered deity / primordial.

- RONGO and TU. Both are firmly attested in the Tuamotus. Rongo has his own dedicated
  Tuamotuan legend (Emory, "The Tuamotu Legend of Rongo, Son of Vaio," JPS 56, 1947),
  and both appear among the great gods of the group; together with Tane they form the
  man-making triad "equal in essence" (Tane–Tu–Rongo). I wire Rongo and Tu as SIBLINGS
  of Tane on the strength of this firmly-attested fraternal triad, but I do NOT assert
  Atea/Fakahotu parentage for them, because the indigenous creation-charts (Paiore, JPS
  1939) place the great gods at differing genealogical points and the sources do not
  uniformly make Rongo and Tu sons of Atea; the variant placements are noted. Both
  entered deity / mythic.

- TAHAKI (Tafaki). The radiant red-skinned hero of the firmly-attested Tuamotuan cycle
  (Stimson, Bulletin 127, the Fagatau recension; Bulletin 148, the Anaa recension). He
  is the son of HEMA and the twin of KARIHI (only Tahaki has the magical power); his
  reddish skin and golden hair are his recognition-mark; his great deed is the quest to
  recover his father Hema, seized by the goblins (the Kona/ponaturi-type beings) at
  Matuauru, whom he ensnares in the great net Tukutuhuraho-nui and beats to death. In the
  fish-episode the fish strip his beautiful red skin when he dives and it is restored on
  his return. His father Hema, twin Karihi, and grandmother Kuhi/Kui are firmly named in
  the cycle but are thinly-developed name-level figures, so I record them in parentRoles/
  notes and wire no in-batch kin edge to them (none are authored); Tahaki is therefore
  entered kinless (SOLITARY). DEMIGOD (a hero of the heroic-genealogy stratum, not a god),
  mythic-prehistoric.

- MAUI-tikitiki. The pan-Polynesian culture-hero in the Tuamotuan (Anaa/Fagatau)
  recension, the fifth and last-born son of ATARAGA and the chiefess HUAHEGA (daughter
  of the fire-magician Mahuike). His firmly-attested deeds: snaring the SUN with a rope
  made of the hairs of his mother's head; fishing up the land of HAVAIKI from the
  ocean-floor with his brothers; bringing FIRE by killing the fire-holder Mahuike in a
  tossing-contest; and slaying the eel TE TUNA (from whose severed head sprang the first
  coconut palm, Niu-roa-i-Havaiki) — the deed by which he wins Hina. His parents Ataraga
  and Huahega, and his four elder brothers, are named but thin, recorded in parentRoles/
  notes (not authored). He is wired to HINA as lover (the Te Tuna cycle). DEMIGOD,
  mythic-prehistoric.

- HINA. Firmly attested in the Tuamotus as the moon-woman and as the wife of the eel
  TE TUNA whom Maui wins by slaying Tuna (the coconut springing from Tuna's head), and
  as Hina-fa'auru-va'a ("Hina who steers the canoe"), the divine canoe-pilot and sister
  of the voyager-god Ru. She is the multi-aspect Polynesian Hina; her several attested
  aspect-names are treated as one Hina, not separate figures. Wired to Maui as lover.
  Her own parentage is not securely given in the Tuamotuan sources, so no parentId is
  asserted (she carries a real in-batch edge to Maui, so she is not flagged SOLITARY).
  Entered deity / mythic.

- FAUMEA. The eel-woman of the Tuamotus (the folk vagina-dentata motif), consort of
  Takaroa, mother of Tu-nui-ka-rere and Turi-a-Faumea; in the octopus-demon episode
  (Rogo-tumu-here's abduction of Turi-a-Faumea's wife Hina-a-rau-riki) she holds the
  wind in "the sweat of her armpit" and releases it at Takaroa's command to power the
  rescue-canoes. Wired to Takaroa as spouse. Entered deity / mythic.

- PUNA. The firmly-attested Tuamotuan king of HITI-MARAMA (or of Vava'u in some
  tellings), a realm of the deep "north of Pitcairn and Elizabeth/Henderson but long
  since swallowed in the sea," and the antagonist-sovereign of the Rata / Vahi-vero
  cycle: it is Puna who abducts the water-nymph Tahiti-tokerau (wife of Vahi-vero), whose
  demon-bird army (chief Matutu-ta'ota'o) seizes Rata's parents, and who comes in
  SHARK-form for vengeance. NOTE ON THE PROMPT'S "eel-lord" GLOSS: the brief described
  Puna as "the eel-lord of the deep," but the Tuamotuan sources firmly make Puna the
  SHARK-king/sea-sovereign of the lost land Hiti-marama (the distinct eel-figure of the
  Tuamotu cycles is Te Tuna, slain by Maui — authored implicitly via Maui/Hina). I have
  therefore authored Puna accurately as the shark-form king of the deep, not as an eel,
  and flagged the discrepancy here. His kin (Kui, Vahi-vero, Tahiti-tokerau, Rata) are
  not authored figures, so Puna is entered kinless (SOLITARY). Entered deity /
  mythic-prehistoric.

- GENEALOGY WIRED WITHIN THIS BATCH (no other-tradition ids): Atea <-> Fakahotu
  (spouses) and both -> Tane (parentIds); Tane <-> Tu and Tane <-> Rongo and Tu <-> Rongo
  (siblings, the man-making triad); Takaroa <-> Faumea (spouses); Maui <-> Hina (lovers).
  Kiho, Tahaki and Puna are entered kinless with cited reasons (see SOLITARY flags).

SOLITARY: tuamotuan_kiho — entered self-existent and parentless. Kiho-tumu ("Kiho the Source") is by definition the supreme self-existent creator who alone existed in the void beneath the foundation of the world, with neither father nor mother (Stimson, Bulletins 103 and 111, 1933). The esoteric Kiho recension is not genealogically harmonized in the sources with the Atea–Fakahotu cosmogony, so no kin edge to any other figure is asserted; he is the apex of his own recension.

SOLITARY: tuamotuan_tahaki — entered kinless. Tahaki (Tafaki) is firmly attested as the radiant red-skinned hero of the Tuamotuan cycle (Stimson, Bulletins 127 (1934) and 148 (1937)), with his father Hema, twin Karihi and blind grandmother Kuhi/Kui all named — but these are thinly-developed name-level figures not authored in this batch, so per the accuracy rule no in-batch kin edge is asserted rather than authoring padding-figures or importing a parent from another Polynesian recension.

SOLITARY: tuamotuan_puna — entered kinless. Puna is firmly attested as the king of Hiti-marama and the shark-form antagonist of the Rata/Vahi-vero cycle (Stimson, Bulletin 148 (1937); Beckwith 1940), but the heroes and kin of that cycle (Kui, Vahi-vero, Tahiti-tokerau, Rata) are not authored figures in this batch, so no in-batch kin edge is asserted.
