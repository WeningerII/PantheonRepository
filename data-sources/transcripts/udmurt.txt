================================================================================
RESEARCH NOTES & METHOD
================================================================================
The Udmurt (Votyak) sacred world, as documented in the classic ethnography, is
built on three registers:
  (1) an aerial/celestial tier of male sky-gods and a parallel set of female
      "-mumy" (mother) deities;
  (2) chthonic / place "-murt" ('man/person') and "-kuzё" ('master') spirit-
      owners of nature and the homestead;
  (3) the adversary / sacred-grove power opposed to the supreme sky-god Inmar.

The five figures already in the registry — udmurt_inmar (supreme sky-god),
udmurt_inmumy (sky-mother), udmurt_kyldysin (earth/fertility creator-god, whose
alts already include Mu-Kylchin / Mu-Kyldysin), udmurt_shundy_mumy (sun-mother)
and udmurt_vumurt (the water-man) — cover the apex of (1) plus one each of the
"-mumy" and "-murt" types. They leave the following well-attested CORE figures
unrepresented, authored here (6):

  • udmurt_nyulesmurt  — Nyulesmurt, the forest-man / master of the forest and
                         game (the Udmurt "leshy").  [the -murt forest-owner]
  • udmurt_korka_murt  — Korka-murt / Korka-kuzё, the house-spirit / master of
                         the homestead.            [the -murt house-owner]
  • udmurt_kuaz        — Kuaz' / Kvaz', god of the weather and atmosphere; with
                         Inmar and Kyldysin one of the three supreme gods.
  • udmurt_gudyri_mumy — Gudyri-mumy, the Thunder-Mother.   [a -mumy goddess]
  • udmurt_invu_mumy   — Invu-mumy / Invozho-mumy, Mother of the Heavenly Water
                         (rain/heaven-water).               [a -mumy goddess]
  • udmurt_keremet     — Keremet / Lud (also Šaitan), the malevolent counterpart
                         of Inmar and lord of the sacred grove (lud / keremet').

NOT re-authored (already covered / not separately individuated):
  • "Mu-Kylchin / Mu-mumy" (earth-mother): the earth-creator is udmurt_kyldysin,
    whose name set already carries Mu-Kylchin / Mu-Kyldysin (the mu- 'earth'
    compound). The "Mumy / Earth Goddess" listed loosely in some popular surveys
    is not consistently individuated apart from Kyldysin in the primary
    ethnography, so a separate figure would duplicate, not enrich. Noted, not
    padded.

ENRICHMENT: every figure carries domains, faculties (powers) and materialCulture
(items) to the extent the sources attest — the family shrine (kua / kuala), the
great prayer-house (byd'zym kuala), the clan-protector vorshud, the sacred grove
(lud / keremet'), the taban' pancake-offering, black-animal sacrifice, and the
krez' zither played on the heaven-water ritual round. Object/term detail is
included ONLY where attested; the Udmurt `term` is given only where a real word
is verified.

SOURCE BASE (work-level citations):
  • Uno Holmberg (Harva), Permalaisten uskonto ("The Religion of the Permian
    Peoples"), Helsinki, 1914 — the foundational Permic/Votyak survey.
  • Uno Holmberg (Harva), Finno-Ugric, Siberian Mythology = The Mythology of All
    Races, vol. IV, Boston, 1927 (Votyak sections).
  • Bernát Munkácsi (& D. R. Fuchs, ed.), Volksbräuche und Volksdichtung der
    Wotjaken, Mémoires de la Société Finno-Ougrienne 102, Helsinki, 1952

    POWs at Esztergom); cf. Munkácsi, Votják népköltészeti hagyományok,
    Budapest, 1887.
  • V. E. Vladykin, Religiozno-mifologicheskaya kartina mira udmurtov, Izhevsk,
    1994 — standard modern Udmurt ethnography.

  • Tertiary corroboration: "Udmurt Vos" and "Dorvyzhy", Wikipedia;
    "Udmurt", Encyclopedia.com (Encyclopedia of World Cultures);
    everyculture.com, "Udmurt — Religion and Expressive Culture".
