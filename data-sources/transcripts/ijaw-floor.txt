================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added six individuated figures drawn entirely from the Ijaw (Ijo) oral epic The Ozidi Saga, recorded in 1963 from the bard Okabou Ojobolo and published/translated by J. P. Clark-Bekederemo (1977) with an extended critical study by Isidore Okpewho (Blood on the Tides, 2014; Oral Tradition, 2004): the avenger-hero Ozidi the Younger, his murdered father Ozidi the Elder, his mother Orea, his sorceress grandmother Oreame, his idiot-king uncle Temugedege, and the forest wizard Bouakarakarabiri. They form a tightly attested kin-and-combat network, wired to each other by parentage, marriage, sibling and companion ties, and connected to the existing corpus through Orea's invocation of Tamara (Tamuno) against the Smallpox King. I deliberately excluded the epic's many monster-antagonists (Tebesonoma, Odogu, Engarando, etc.) and the generic water-spirit class (Owuamapu) as either non-principled to enumerate or not individuated ancestors/heroes, keeping the addition to the securely documented core cast.
