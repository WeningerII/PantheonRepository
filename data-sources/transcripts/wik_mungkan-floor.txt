================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added six individuated named pulwaiya (totemic ancestors) documented in Ursula McConnel's Wik-Mungkan corpus beyond the existing four: Kapa (the Moon), Min Monti (the jabiru), Min Wuka (the flying-fox), Kongkong (the white fish-hawk, tied to the origin of the fishing-net), and the paired serpent ancestors Mun U.ka (White Snake Man) and Min Tuktaiyan (Black Snake Woman), whom I wired to each other as the two protagonists of a single recorded myth. Each name and native form is taken directly from McConnel's field-text titles catalogued in the South Australian Museum AA 191 collection ('Texts, mainly in Wik-Mungkan') and published in Myths of the Munkan (1957), corroborated by her Oceania articles (1930, 1935) and McKnight (1981); I excluded generic food-totems and figures I could not confirm as specifically Wik-Mungkan (e.g. the coastal hero-brothers Sivirri and Nyunggu, whose cult McConnel attributes to Wik-Mungkan 'and neighbouring tribes'). Further named pulwaiya exist in the same archive (e.g. the pelican Min Mo.ta, the frilled-lizard Min Pontyangoiya, the willy-wagtail Tityarokan and peewit Iyebanya), so the record is not exhausted, but I limited the set to the best-attested, clearly individuated ancestors to prioritise correctness over quantity.
