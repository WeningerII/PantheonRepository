================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added three individuated Desana supernaturals that Reichel-Dolmatoff's ethnographies consistently name as distinct "divine personifications" created by the Sun-Father, alongside the already-registered Emëkóri-mahsë and Vaí-mahsë: Vihó-mahsë (the snuff-being who dwells in the Milky Way and mediates all shamanic contact), Ñamíri-mahsë (the Night People, agents of witchcraft and the punitive/evil principle, explicit counterpart of the existing Day People), and Diróa-mahsë (the Blood People, who with the Day People uphold the principle of good). Each is wired to the existing pantheon via a subordinate-to relation to desana_sun_father, since the sources present all of them as ordained by the Sun. I stopped at three because the remaining figures in the corpus (Sun-Father, Master of Animals, Maker of Humanity, Daughter of the Sun) are already present, and other Desana narrative beings (e.g. the Moon or an unnamed "Son of the Sun") lack a securely attested individuated name in the accessible scholarship; padding with them would be speculative. Sources consulted: Reichel-Dolmatoff, Amazonian Cosmos (1971), The Shaman and the Jaguar (1975), Desana: Simbolismo de los indios tukano del Vaupés (1968), and Buchillet's "Nobody is There to Hear" (1992).
