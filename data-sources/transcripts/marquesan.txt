MARQUESAN PANTHEON — CORE COSMOGONY AND CULTURE-HEROES
=======================================================

Tradition: Marquesan (Te Henua ʻEnana / Te Fenua ʻEnata — "The Land of Men," the
indigenous name of the Marquesas Islands archipelago, eastern Polynesia).

This transcript authors the firmly-attested CORE of the Marquesan pantheon, which
was entirely absent from the registry. The authoritative ethnographic source is
E. S. Craighill Handy, "The Native Culture in the Marquesas" (Bernice P. Bishop
Museum Bulletin 9, Honolulu, 1923) — the major reconstruction of pre-contact
Marquesan religion and cosmology — supplemented by Handy, "Marquesan Legends"
(Bishop Museum Bulletin 69, 1930), the companion volume of recorded narratives.
Cross-corroboration from the comparative-mythology literature (Roland B. Dixon,
"Oceanic Mythology," Mythology of All Races vol. 9, 1916; E. Tregear,
"Maori-Polynesian Comparative Dictionary," 1891) is cited where it confirms the
Handy material.

COSMOGONIC SUMMARY (Handy 1923, ch. on religion; Dixon 1916, ch. I):
In the primeval void (Po) reigned TANAOA (darkness, = pan-Polynesian Tangaroa)
and MUTU-HEI (silence). In the course of time ATEA ("Light"/"Space") evolved or
separated himself out of Tanaoa and drove him away, confining the powers of night
within bounds; then ONO ("Sound") evolved himself out of Atea and destroyed
Mutu-hei. From these two struggles arose ATANUA ("Dawn"), whom Atea took to wife,
begetting a host of deities and the lineage of mankind. In the pu'e (sacred
creation chant — taboo to recite before women), 'Atea impregnates ONE-U'I, the
"sand-woman," and the first man TIKI is born. Marquesan genealogies of the 19th
century traced the people of Hiva up some ninety generations to the progenitors
Atea and Atanua, with Tiki named as the first man. In a Hiva Oa tradition Atea and
TANE are brothers, sons of TOHO / TOHETIKA. The great culture-hero MAHUI (the
Marquesan Māui) fished up islands, won fire from his grandfather MAHUIKE in the
underworld, and snared the sun. Other firmly-named figures in Handy's narrative
register (Native Culture p. 247) include TU, ONO, TOHETIKA, MAHUI, MAHUIKE, POHU,
and AKAUI.
