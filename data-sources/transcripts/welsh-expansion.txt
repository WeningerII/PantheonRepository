================================================================================
THE GAP — SIX REGISTERED ERAS IN A ROW HELD NOTHING
================================================================================

An 83-name check across the WHOLE registry returned 33 hits and 50 absences.

Lane 18 found the Irish vocabulary registering `fenian-cycle` and filing nobody
in it, and called an empty registered era the clearest statement a corpus can
make about what it is missing. **The Welsh vocabulary has NINE eras and holds
records in three of them.**

    mythic-prehistoric          9 records
    mabinogion-mythological    30 records
    mythological-cycle          1 record
    roman-british               0
    post-roman                  0
    norman-and-edwardian        0
    late-medieval               0
    early-modern                0
    modern                      0

Everything from the Roman period onward is empty. That is not an accident of
sampling: it is precisely the span in which the Welsh Arthurian material lives —
*Culhwch ac Olwen*, the Triads, the Three Romances, the Dream of Macsen, the
Myrddin poems — and the corpus held the Four Branches of the Mabinogi almost
completely and none of it.

**THE REGISTRY DID NOT HOLD ARTHUR.**

Nor Cai, nor Bedwyr, nor Gwenhwyfar, nor Medrawd. Nor Culhwch or Olwen, whose
tale is the oldest Arthurian story in any language. Nor Myrddin, in either of
the two forms the Welsh tradition itself distinguishes.

And within the layer the corpus DID hold, the same fault as everywhere else:

- **Arawn without Hafgan.** The corpus holds Arawn, king of Annwn, and Pwyll,
  who changes places with him for a year and a day — and did not hold the rival
  Pwyll is sent to kill, which is the only thing that happens in the exchange.
- **Ceridwen without Morfran.** The corpus holds Ceridwen and Taliesin, and the
  cauldron is brewed for **Morfran**, her hideous son, whom she is trying to
  compensate with wisdom. The three drops land on the servant instead. The
  corpus held the woman, the accident and the beneficiary, and not the person
  the whole thing was for — nor **Creirwy**, the beautiful daughter Morfran is
  ugly *compared with*.
- **Gwyn ap Nudd without Creiddylad or Gwythyr.** The corpus holds a god whose
  one story is that he and another man fight for a woman every May Day until
  Judgement Day, and held neither the other man nor the woman.
- **Mabon ap Modron** — the greatest huntsman of all time, imprisoned since the
  beginning of time, and one of the oldest divine names in Britain — absent.

================================================================================
A FINDING THE SOURCES THEMSELVES MAKE
================================================================================

**There are two Merlins and the Welsh tradition knew it.** As early as the end
of the twelfth century Giraldus Cambrensis sharply distinguishes *Merlinus
Ambrosius* — Myrddin Emrys, found at Carmarthen, who prophesies before
Vortigern — from *Merlinus Silvester* or *Celidonius*, who comes from the North,
is a contemporary of Arthur, sees a horror in the sky during a battle and spends
the rest of his life mad in the woods. Geoffrey of Monmouth combined them.

This batch ingests **two records**, because the tradition distinguishes two
figures and a registry that indexes traditions should be able to say that the
famous single character is a twelfth-century merger. Neither record asserts
they are the same person and neither asserts they are not.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BATCH DOES NOT INGEST
================================================================================

Annwn, Dinas Emrys, Caernarfon and Camlann are **places**. The Three Harmful
Blows and the Triads are **texts**. Adar Rhiannon, the birds of Rhiannon, are a
**collective** of unnamed birds. Ysbaddaden's forty tasks are a **list of
tasks**, and only the few the tale narrates produce records here.
