PANTHEON REGISTRY — FIGURE-AUTHORING TRANSCRIPT
Tradition: Punic (Carthage and the western Phoenician / Punic world — Tunisia, Sardinia,
Ibiza, western Sicily, Malta, Punic Iberia; c. 814–146 BCE and the Roman-African afterlife)
ID prefix: punic_
Eras (registered, exact): "primordial", "mythic"
  - "primordial" = the great gods as timeless cosmic powers, attested epigraphically across the
    Punic world from the foundation of Carthage (trad. 814 BCE) to its fall (146 BCE).
  - "mythic" = narrative/heroic figures: Melqart's death-and-awakening cycle; Sid the eponym
    of Sardinia. (No theogony or birth-narrative of the great gods survives in Punic sources, so
    Baal Hammon, Tanit, Eshmun, Astarte, Baal Shamem and Reshef are placed in "primordial.")

NOTE ON SCOPE — PUNIC vs. inherited Phoenician. This transcript authors the CARTHAGINIAN /
western-Punic pantheon under the punic_ prefix, distinct from the eastern Phoenician homeland
gods already in the corpus under the phoenician_ prefix (phoenician_baal_hammon, _tanit,
_melqart, _eshmun, _astarte, _resheph, _baalshamin). The two sets share theonyms and origin;
here each figure is authored in its PUNIC form and cult role, and the shared origin with the
eastern homeland is recorded in each figure's notes. Relations are wired ONLY within this
punic_ batch (per the hard rule that relation.personId resolve within the batch); the eastern
phoenician_ figures are referenced in prose notes, not as relation edges.

RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISIONS
================================================================================

ANCHOR SOURCES (verified titles/years).
  • Serge Lancel, *Carthage: A History*, trans. Antonia Nevill (Oxford: Blackwell, 1995) —
    Lancel directed the Byrsa excavations; standard narrative-and-religion history.
  • Edward Lipiński, *Dieux et déesses de l'univers phénicien et punique* (Studia Phoenicia
    XIV = Orientalia Lovaniensia Analecta 64; Leuven: Peeters, 1995).
  • Gilbert Charles-Picard & Colette Picard, *The Life and Death of Carthage* (London:
    Sidgwick & Jackson, 1968; Eng. trans. of *La vie et la mort de Carthage*). [First English
    edition 1968 — not 1969/70.]
  • Paolo Xella, *Baal Hammon. Recherches sur l'identité et l'histoire d'un dieu phénico-
    punique* (Contributi alla Storia della Religione Fenicio-Punica I = Collezione di Studi
    Fenici 32; Roma: CNR, 1991) — the standard Baal Hammon monograph.
  • Corinne Bonnet, *Melqart. Cultes et mythes de l'Héraclès tyrien en Méditerranée* (Studia
    Phoenicia VIII; Leuven: Peeters / Namur, 1988) — the standard Melqart monograph.
  • F. O. Hvidberg-Hansen, *La déesse TNT: Une étude sur la religion canaanéo-punique*, 2 vols.
    (Copenhagen: Gad, 1979) — the standard Tanit monograph.
  • Josephine Crawley Quinn, *In Search of the Phoenicians* (Princeton Univ. Press, 2018) — esp.
    the chapter "The Circle of the Tophet" (the tophet and Punic identity).
  • Reshef: William J. Fulco, *The Canaanite God Rēšep* (American Oriental Society, 1976) — the
    first monograph; Edward Lipiński, *Resheph: A Syro-Canaanite Deity* (Studia Phoenicia XIX =
    OLA 181; Peeters, 2009) — the dedicated Lipiński study; Paolo Xella, "Resheph", in *Dictionary
    of Deities and Demons in the Bible* (DDD, 2nd ed.; Brill, 1999), 700–703; M. M. Münnich, *The
    God Resheph in the Ancient Near East* (Mohr Siebeck, 2013).
  • Epigraphic corpora: KAI = H. Donner & W. Röllig, *Kanaanäische und aramäische Inschriften*
    (3 vols., 1st ed. 1960–64; 5th ed. of vol. 1, 2002); CIS I = *Corpus Inscriptionum
    Semiticarum*, pars prima (the great mass of Carthaginian tophet stelae); M. G. Amadasi
    Guzzo, *Le iscrizioni fenicie e puniche delle colonie in Occidente* / *…in Italia* (ICO)
    for Sardinia (Antas) and Italy.

DISTINCTIVELY PUNIC vs. INHERITED (foregrounded per the brief).
  • Distinctively Punic / western: BAAL HAMMON (supreme god of Carthage, lord of the tophet),
    TANIT / Tinnit Pene Baal (the great goddess of Carthage, the "sign of Tanit"), and SID Addir
    / Sardus Pater (eponymous hero-god of Sardinia, son of Melqart, of the temple of Antas).
  • Inherited from the Phoenician homeland but authored here in their Punic role: MELQART (the
    Tyrian dynastic god and ancestral patron honoured at Carthage; egersis), ESHMUN (the healer,
    whose great temple crowned the Byrsa), ASTARTE (present in Punic cult, e.g. the Pyrgi
    dedication; assimilated to Tanit at Carthage), BAAL SHAMEM ("lord of the heavens"), and
    RESHEF (West Semitic god of plague/war — see the caveat below).

THE GREEK/ROMAN INTERPRETATIO (recorded as notes/epithets unless the counterpart is IN this
batch). Baal Hammon = Kronos (Greek) / Saturn (Roman; the immensely popular Saturnus Africanus).
Tanit = Juno Caelestis / Dea Caelestis (Roman), occasionally Artemis/Hera. Melqart = Herakles /
Hercules ("the Tyrian Herakles"). Eshmun = Asklepios / Aesculapius. Sid = Sardus Pater (Roman).
Astarte = Aphrodite (Greek) / Venus·Juno (Roman). Reshef = Apollo (Greek). NONE of these
Greco-Roman counterparts is a figure in this batch, so each is recorded in notes/epithets, NOT
as a dangling "equated-with" relation (per the hard rule).

RELATIONAL WIRING (reciprocal within batch).
  • BAAL HAMMON ⇄ TANIT — the consort pair of Carthage, jointly invoked on every tophet stele.
    Wired as reciprocal `spouse`. CAVEAT carried in notes: the pairing is cultic/epigraphic
    (joint invocation, the epithet "Face of Baal"); NO Punic text gives a marriage narrative,
    parentage, or offspring for either deity. No kin edge to children is asserted.
  • MELQART (father-of) ⇄ SID (son-of) — Sid of Sardinia is the son of Melqart-Herakles. This
    rests on the cult assemblage at Antas (Melqart + Sid/Sardus Pater + Astarte) plus the
    classical genealogy of Sardus son of Makeris (= the "Libyan" Herakles = Melqart) in
    Pausanias 10.17 and Sallust. Flagged as partly reconstructed; wired as the directed
    father-of/son-of pair (Sid's parentId = punic_melqart).
  • No other kin edges are attested. Eshmun, Astarte, Baal Shamem and Reshef are authored
    WITHOUT kin relations (Astarte's pairing with Melqart at Tyre and her assimilation to Tanit
    are noted in prose, not over-claimed as Carthaginian kinship). They are flagged SOLITARY.

ATTESTED vs. RECONSTRUCTED (the cardinal rule — flagged per figure).
  • Firmly attested (epigraphy/primary): the theonyms (bʿl ḥmn, tnt, mlqrt, ʾšmn, ʿštrt, ršp,
    bʿl šmm) and the standard tophet formula l-rbt l-tnt pn bʿl w-l-ʾdn l-bʿl ḥmn ("To the Lady,
    to Tanit Face of Baal, and to the Lord, to Baal Hammon"), with Tanit named FIRST from the
    5th c. BCE; the vow-formula ʾš ndr … k šmʿ qlʾ ("which he vowed … because he heard his
    voice"); the mlk/molk offering term; KAI 47 (Cippi of Melqart), KAI 277 (Pyrgi/Astarte),
    KAI 14 & 15–16 (Eshmunazar / Bodashtart, Sidon); the egersis and the priest-title mqm ʾlm;
    the Byrsa temple of Eshmun as the last redoubt in 146 BCE (Appian; Strabo 17.3.14); the
    temple of Antas to Sid Addir, rebuilt as Sardus Pater Babai.
  • Modern reconstruction (flagged, not asserted as fact): the meaning of "Ḥammon" (brazier /
    Mt Amanus / toponym); the etymology of "Tinnit" (Lipiński's "weeper"; Cross's Asherah-
    epithet); the precise domain-portfolios (sun/vegetation; moon/heaven) read from iconography
    and interpretatio; the identification of the "sign of Tanit" with the goddess; whether
    systematic child sacrifice occurred at the tophet; Melqart as a full "dying-and-rising god"
    (the egersis is attested; the death-by-fire is drawn from Greek Herakles material); Eshmun's
    youth-castration-revival myth (a single late source, Damascius via Photius, cod. 242).

FIGURES CONSIDERED BUT EXCLUDED / NOTE.
  • RESHEF is authored, but with a prominent caveat: per Xella, there is no SECURE evidence for
    a Reshef cult in the Punic cities themselves; older claims of a "Punic Reshef" rest on
    misreadings (of ʿrš "Eresh" and of rš "Baal of the cape"). He is therefore authored as the
    inherited West Semitic god attested across the wider Phoenician-Punic sphere (esp. Cyprus —
    Kition/Idalion, where ršp mkl = Apollo Amyklos), NOT as a securely-attested Carthaginian
    god; this is stated in his notes. He is included because the brief lists him and he is a
    real, citable figure of the Phoenician-Punic religious world.
  • "Moloch" as a god is NOT authored: following Eissfeldt (1935), mlk/molk on the stelae is a
    sacrificial term (mlk ʾmr "offering of a lamb" = molchomor; mlk ʾdm "offering of a man"),
    not the name of a deity. Recorded in Baal Hammon's materialCulture/notes, not as a figure.
  • The Greco-Roman interpretatio counterparts (Kronos/Saturn, Heracles, Asklepios, Juno
    Caelestis, Aphrodite/Venus, Apollo, Sardus Pater) are NOT authored as separate figures and
    NOT wired as relations (they are not in this batch); they appear in notes/epithets only.
