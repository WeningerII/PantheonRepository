================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added three individuated beings that scholarship documents beyond the existing five: Jinopotabá (the crab-shaped healer and prototype jaibaná, son of Karagabí, who crosses the three cosmic worlds), Ba (the personified Thunder of the upper world), and Jino Poto Warra (a hero created by Karagabí to free the Emberá from dangers in Tutruicá's underworld), wiring the two Jino-Poto heroes to Karagabí by parentage and to each other as companions within one mythic complex. Sources are Isacsson's Transformations of Eternity (1993), Pardo's El convite de los espíritus (1987), Vasco's Jaibanás: los verdaderos hombres (1985), and the IIAP Bioetnia synthesis (2011). I deliberately excluded the sun and moon (their names are unstable across sources — Umadau/Humantahú, Jedeko/Hedeko/Gedeco — and Karagabí is himself identified with the moon in the Katío branch, risking duplication) and Nusí (attested as a class of voracious deep-pool fish-monsters rather than a single named individual), since padding with those would be less defensible than the tight, cleanly attested set given here.
