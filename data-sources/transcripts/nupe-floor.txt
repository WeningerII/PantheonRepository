================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added two well-attested figures: Sogba (Sokogba), the thunder cult-power understood as 'the axe of Soko', documented by Nadel (1942; 1954) and analysed by Capo Chichi (Yoruba Studies Review, 2025), wired as subordinate to the existing sky god Soko; and Ndaduma, 'Father Niger', the personified River Niger worshipped as a flood divinity at Gbara and Jima (Sidi, ADVED 2015; Salihu, Wukari International Studies Journal, 2022). Beyond these the individuated record is genuinely exhausted: Nadel's standard ethnographies present Nupe religion as centred on Soko plus impersonal cult-powers (kuti, gunnu, ndako gboya — all already in the corpus) rather than a personified pantheon. Candidates examined and rejected were elo (a purely secular entertainment mask, per Britannica), gugu (the borrowed Yoruba egungun, a spirit-class), gani (an eighteenth-century Bornu import, per Nadel 1949), the Jebba/Tada 'Gara' bronzes (cult objects of the existing Tsoede, not beings), and Ketsa Rock and the 'Mamma' leaf-masquerade, each attested only in a single source (Sidi 2015), below the two-citation bar; the actors of the Tsoede legend (his Igala father, his mother of Nku) remain unnamed in the sources.
