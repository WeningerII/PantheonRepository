REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added the two figures that ethnohistorical scholarship genuinely individuates beyond the recently authored divine core: the hero-ancestor Lempira and his overlord Entepica, both attested in Herrera's Historia general (Década VI, 1601–1615) and in modern scholarship (Chapman 1978; Newson 1986; Martínez Castillo 1987, who published Rodrigo Ruiz's 1558 probanza naming "Elempira"). I verified the remainder of the record is exhausted: García de Palacio's 1576 two-faced, many-eyed stone idol at Cezori/Sesori is the already-authored Icelaca; Comizahual's three sons/brothers are unnamed in Herrera and all retellings; and Anne Chapman's ethnography (Los hijos del copal y la candela, 1985–86) records only syncretic Catholic figures and unnamed spirit classes (ángeles, señores de los cerros, dueños), which do not qualify as individuated native figures. The creator "Managuara/Maraguana" circulating online was examined and excluded because it carries no scholarly citation for the Lenca and appears to conflate Cacaopera (Kakawira, Misumalpan-speaking) oral tradition collected by Miguel Ángel Amaya Amaya with Lenca material.
