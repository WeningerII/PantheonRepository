================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added four further individuated beings drawn from the two central Murinbata myth-cycles as recorded by W. E. H. Stanner (On Aboriginal Religion, 1963) and corroborated by Eliade (1973), Falkenberg (1962) and Coles (2000): Pilirin the Kestrel, who preserves fire when the wounded Kunmanggur carries the world's fire into the sea; the Green Parrot Girls, Kunmanggur's paired co-wives whose pursuit by Tjinimin precipitates the father-killing; and Left Hand and Right Hand, the two ancestral men of the Mutjingga (Karwadi/Punj) myth who slay the swallowing Old Woman and cut the children from her belly, founding the initiation rite. Each is a specifically named actor in the myths rather than a generic spirit-class, and the new figures are wired to the existing pantheon via consort-of and slayer-of links to Kunmanggur and Mutjingga. I deliberately excluded Nogamain: although tertiary encyclopaedias label him "Murrinh-Patha," his spirit-child/sky-being role belongs to western Arnhem Land ethnography and I could not confirm the attribution in the primary Port Keats sources, so including him would have been unsupported padding.
