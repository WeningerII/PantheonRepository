Tongan pantheon — the well-attested CORE, authored from scratch (the tradition
was entirely absent from the registry).

The Tongan tradition is registered with three eras: primordial, mythic, and
mythic-prehistoric. This batch supplies the firmly-attested foundation: the
Tangaloa sky-family and the descent of the Tu'i Tonga, the Maui demigods who
fished up and fitted out the islands, and Hikule'o the keeper of Pulotu (the
spirit-land/underworld). Every figure is verified against the decisive
scholarship and the standard ethnographic record.

The anchoring sources are: E. W. Gifford, Tongan Myths and Tales (Bernice P.
Bishop Museum Bulletin 8, 1924) — the foundational compilation of the cosmogony,
the god-genealogy, the Maui cycle, and the Tu'i Tonga origin; E. E. V.
Collocott, Tales and Poems of Tonga (BPB Bulletin 46, 1928) and his earlier
"Notes on Tongan Religion" (Journal of the Polynesian Society 30, 1921), which
set out the Tongan theogony of the gods of Sky, Earth, Sea and Underworld and
name the four sons of Tangaloa in the sky (Tangaloa Tamapo'uli'alamafoa,
Tangaloa 'Eitumatupu'a, Tangaloa 'Atulongolongo, Tangaloa Tufunga); and William
Mariner / John Martin, An Account of the Natives of the Tonga Islands (1817) —
the earliest first-hand record (1806-1810) of the living religion, Pulotu
("Bolotoo"), and the gods.

Verification notes that shaped the data (accuracy-first):

- THE COSMOGONY (frame, recorded in notes, not as figures). Gifford and Collocott
  record that between the sea and Pulotu stood the rock Touia-'o-Futuna, which
  trembled and split, bringing forth four brother-sister twin pairs — Piki & Kele,
  'Atungaki & Ma'imoa'olongona, Fonu'uta & Fonuvai, Hemoana & Lupe. (Collocott also
  has a primal seaweed/limu and mud/kele from which the rock appears — note the name
  Kele recurs both as the primal mud and as Piki's twin sister.) The pair Piki & Kele
  are the PARENTS of Taufulifonua and Havealolofonua, who in turn are the parents of
  the cosmos-dividers Hikule'o, Tangaloa, and Maui (a widely-reproduced version makes
  only Hikule'o the child of Taufulifonua by Havealolofonua, with Tangaloa and Maui
  borne by the cousins Velelahi and Velesi'i — so the three are siblings/half-siblings;
  this variant is noted, not relied on). I do NOT author the twin-pairs or Touiafutuna
  as separate figures (they fall outside the firmly-developed CORE and would be padding),
  but I record the descent in notes and wire the firmly-attested kin edges among the
  figures I do author.

- TWO LEVELS OF "TANGALOA". The sources distinguish (a) the senior Tangaloa who, with
  his siblings Hikule'o and Maui, divided the cosmos and took the sky, from (b) the
  four named sons of Tangaloa IN the sky: Tamapo'uli'alamafoa (the highest), 'Eitumatupu'a,
  'Atulongolongo, and Tufunga (the carpenter) — Collocott, "Notes on Tongan Religion"
  (JPS 30, 1921). I author the two sky-Tangaloa that carry developed, firmly-attested
  myth — 'Eitumatupu'a (descent to 'Ilaheva; father of 'Aho'eitu) and 'Atulongolongo
  (the plover who makes the first men) — and wire them as brothers. Tamapo'uli'alamafoa
  and Tufunga are real but thinly-developed name-level figures, so I record them in
  notes/parentRoles rather than authoring them as padding.

- HIKULE'O'S GENDER is genuinely variable across the record and I represent it as such
  rather than forcing one value: Mariner (1817) and several tellings treat the ruler of
  Pulotu as male ("his cousins Maui and Tangaloa"), while Gifford/Collocott and the
  common modern reading give "the goddess of Pulotu." The ambiguity is real and rooted
  in the gender-neutral Tongan pronoun (ia). I set sex "unknown" and document both
  readings; the long TAIL (held/tied so that the land would not follow the dead to
  Pulotu — one end held by Tangaloa in the sky, the other by Maui below) is the constant,
  well-attested attribute and populates the faculties and material culture.

- THE THREE MAUI are demigods, not gods: the Tongan record gives Maui Motu'a (the old
  Maui, below, who upholds the earth), his son Maui 'Atalanga, and the trickster grandson
  Maui Kisikisi (Maui-fusi-fonua, "land-puller"). The island-fishing and the fire-bringing
  are firmly attested; which Maui "brought fire" varies (Kisikisi in most tellings,
  'Atalanga in some), so I assign the deed to Kisikisi with the variant noted. The magic
  fish-hook came from the old fisherman Tonga-fusi-fonua of Manuka, whose wife Tavatava
  betrayed which hook to choose — a constant of the Tongan recension (Gifford 1924).

- 'AHO'EITU is a DEMIGOD: son of a deity (Tangaloa 'Eitumatupu'a) and a mortal ('Ilaheva).
  The murder-and-resurrection by his jealous sky-brothers (his portions vomited into a
  kumete bowl by Tangaloa's command, restored with water and nonufiafia leaves), and his
  installation as the first Tu'i Tonga, are the fixed elements. He had FIVE elder
  sky-brothers — Talafale, Matakehe, Maliepo, Tu'i Loloko and Tu'i Folaha; the eldest,
  Talafale, became the Tu'i Faleua (the substitute royal line), and the OTHER FOUR
  (Matakehe, Maliepo, Tu'i Loloko, Tu'i Folaha) became the Falefa, the "four houses" /
  attendant matāpule of the Tu'i Tonga (Gifford 1924; Collocott 1928).

- 'ILAHEVA VA'EPOPUA is a MORTAL of the older Tongan population; all accounts make her the
  wife of Tangaloa 'Eitumatupu'a and mother of 'Aho'eitu. Her own PARENTAGE is left empty:
  one tradition makes her a daughter of Seketo'a, but this is weakly and inconsistently
  attested and geographically awkward (Seketo'a being firmly the Niuatoputapu god while
  'Ilaheva is of Tongatapu), so it is recorded in parentRoles only, not asserted as a
  parentId. I likewise do NOT genealogize her directly to the worm-born first men, which
  the sources do not do.

- SEKETO'A is firmly attested (Gifford 1924; Collocott; and the continuous Niuatoputapu
  cult): a man of the Ma'atu line of Niuatoputapu — grandson of its founder Puakatefisi —
  deified after his jealous elder brother Moimoi turned against him, who appears as a
  shark/fish and is summoned by the Ma'atu chiefs by casting kava on the sea, his two
  remora as his matāpule. He belongs to the Ma'atu/Niuatoputapu line, NOT to the Tu'i
  Tonga line and NOT to Tele'a. He is included for his own developed cult; because the
  'father of 'Ilaheva' link is not firmly attested he is entered kinless (SOLITARY).

- HINA in the Tongan recension is firmly attested as the moon-woman who sits in the moon
  beating tapa and as the great protecting goddess of the fire-walkers (the Tongan-specific
  detail), and in the Maui genealogy as the wife of Maui 'Atalanga and mother of Maui
  Kisikisi. She is therefore entered as a deity with a real in-batch kin edge.
