================================================================================
THE GAP — THE OLDEST WRITTEN LIST OF FINNISH GODS IS FROM 1551
AND THE CORPUS HELD A THIRD OF IT
================================================================================

A 91-name check across the WHOLE registry returned 39 hits and 52 absences.

In 1551 Mikael Agricola prefaced his Finnish translation of the Psalter with a
verse catalogue of the gods of Häme and of Karelia — twelve to a region, each
with its function stated in a line or two. **It is the oldest written record of
Finnish religion there is**, and it is why Agricola is called the father of the
study of it.

The registry held, of those twenty-four: Tapio, Ahti, Väinämöinen, Ilmarinen,
Ukko, Pellonpekko (as `finnish_pekko`), Nyrckes (as `finnish_nyyrikki`) and
Hiisi (under the *Karelian* key). Eight. **It did not hold:**

    Rahko        who split the Moon black
    Liekkiö      lord of grass, roots and trees
    Rongoteus    who gave the rye
    Virankannos  who guarded the oats
    Äkräs        who made peas, beans, turnips, cabbage, flax and hemp
    Köndös       who made the burn-clearings and the ploughlands
    Rauni        Ukko's consort
    Kekri        who increased the growth of the cattle
    Veden emo    who drove the fish into the net

Nine named gods with stated functions, from the single document that makes
pre-Christian Finland legible at all, and a registry indexing 560 traditions
held none of them. **The corpus had the heroes of a nineteenth-century epic and
almost none of the gods of the sixteenth-century list.**

================================================================================
AND THE KALEVALA ITSELF ENDS WITH SOMEONE THE CORPUS DID NOT HOLD
================================================================================

The Kalevala has fifty cantos. The corpus held the cast of the first
forty-nine and **not the person the fiftieth is about.**

**MARJATTA** — the maiden so chaste she will not drink milk from a cow kept
with a bull — eats a lingonberry, conceives, and bears a son in a stable. The
child is lost in a swamp and found. Väinämöinen judges that a fatherless boy
should be taken out and killed, and **the two-week-old infant answers him back**
and reproaches him for the judgement. The boy is baptised King of Karelia, and
Väinämöinen sings himself a boat of copper and leaves the world.

That is how the epic ends. The corpus held Väinämöinen with ten relations on him
and **did not hold the woman or the child who end his age.**

================================================================================
THE SAMPO IS FORGED FOR A WOMAN THE CORPUS DID NOT HOLD
================================================================================

Louhi's price for the Sampo is her daughter. The corpus held **Louhi**, held
**Ilmarinen** who forges it, held **Väinämöinen** who is refused, held the
wedding's host and the wedding's guests — and did not hold **the Maiden of
Pohjola**, who is what the forging is *for*.

She sits on the rainbow weaving cloth of gold; she sets Väinämöinen the task of
tying an egg in invisible knots; she marries Ilmarinen; she bakes a stone into
Kullervo's bread and he sends the bears and wolves that kill her; and her death
is why Ilmarinen forges the golden bride. **The corpus held Kullervo and his
revenge and not the woman he took it on.**

She is unnamed in the Kalevala — *Pohjan neiti*, the Maiden of the North, is a
title used throughout as a name. A registry that declines to hold her because
her mother never named her would be recording the epic's silence as an absence
of the person.

================================================================================
AND TUONI HAD FIVE DAUGHTERS AND THE CORPUS HELD ONE
================================================================================

`finnish_loviatar` was in the registry with `parentIds` naming Tuoni and
Tuonetar. Her four sisters — **Kalma**, whose name is the smell of a corpse;
**Kivutar**, who boils pain in a pot; **Kiputyttö**, who sits on the pain-stone
on Kipumäki where three rivers meet; and **Vammatar** — were not.

This is lane 18's Ernmas exactly, one tradition later: **the corpus held the
mother, the father and the famous daughter, and none of the rest.**

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BATCH DOES NOT INGEST
================================================================================

**Kratti**, **Tonttu**, **Piru**, **Kapeet**, **Kalevanpojat** and **Liekkiö in
its later sense** are in Agricola's list or beside it and are **classes of
spirit or collectives**, not figures: a treasure-guard, a house-spirit, a devil,
the beings who eat the moon, the sons of Kaleva. Liekkiö is ingested on
Agricola's own line for it — *lord of grass, roots and trees* — and the record
says in as many words that the later folklore uses the word for a different
thing.

**Hittavainen**, twelfth in Agricola's Karelian list, is **rejected and the
reason is itself the finding**: the name is generally read as Huittavainen, that
is **Saint Hubert**, patron of hunters. There is a Catholic saint inside the
oldest list of Finnish gods.

Tuonela, Manala, Pohjola, Sariola, Väinölä and Kipumäki are **places**. The
Sampo and the kantele are **objects**. Vetehinen, Näkki, Maahinen, Menninkäinen
and Para are **classes of spirit**.

================================================================================
A TRADITION-KEY FINDING, RAISED AGAIN AND SHARPER THAN BEFORE
================================================================================

The corpus holds a **Karelian** key with seven records, of which **four are the
same beings as Finnish records**: Väinämöinen and Ilmarinen are held **twice
each**, under both keys, and Ukko three times counting `estonian_uku`.

This batch adds **nothing** under Karelian and creates **no new duplicates**.
**Antero Vipunen** and **Iku-Turso** — the dead giant Väinämöinen climbs inside
for the three words, and the sea-monster Louhi raises — are absent from the
Finnish key and present under Karelian, and are therefore census rows and not
records. The Kalevala is one poem and the registry has it under two keys, and
which figure lands under which is not decided by anything.
