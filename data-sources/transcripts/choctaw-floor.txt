================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added four genuinely individuated, named beings that the Choctaw ethnographic record documents beyond the existing set: Nalusa Chito / Impa Shilup (the great black soul-eater), Bohpoli / Kowi Anukasha (the invisible forest 'Thrower' who trains medicine men), Hoklonote'she (the thought-reading shape-shifter), and Ishkitini (the horned owl of death omen), each carried by Bushnell (BAE Bulletin 48, 1909), Swanton (BAE Bulletin 103, 1931), Lankford (1987), or Mould (2004). I deliberately excluded Okwa Nahollo ('white people of the water') and the Kowi Anukasha as a whole, since scholarship treats these as spirit-races rather than named individuals, and I excluded Heloha and Melatha (thunder/lightning birds) because their individuated attestation rests essentially on a single modern children's retelling (Harrell 1995) rather than two independent scholarly sources; the shilup/shilombish dual soul was likewise excluded as a concept rather than a being. None of the four added figures has attested genealogy or symmetric kinship in the sources, so all are recorded as standing alone with cited justification.
