REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added six individuated, epigraphically or textually attested Berber deities absent from the existing set: Bonchor (central god of the Dii Mauri relief at Vaga/Beja), Aulisua (the widely-attested Mauretanian protective god called deus sanctus), Ieru (a Numidian moon-god whose name preserves Berber ayyur), Bacax (the cave-god of Thibilis with a dated Roman dedication series), and Mastiman and Sinifere (the death-god and war-god named alongside Gurzil in Corippus' Iohannis). Mastiman and Sinifere are wired to each other as companions because Corippus catalogues them together among the eastern Libyan (Laguatan) gods, and their prose notes tie them to the existing figure Gurzil; the four other newcomers are attested only as isolated cult-names and are therefore recorded as solitary. I deliberately excluded the remaining Dii Mauri names (Macurtam, Iunam, Vihinam, Varsissima, Matilam) that survive merely as labels in a group list without individuating attributes, and the poorly-secured Ierna, who is a mortal chieftain-priest rather than a god; each new figure carries at least two real scholarly or primary citations (Camps 1954, Benabou 1976, Gsell 1913-1928, the Encyclopedie berbere, CIL VIII, Corippus ed. Diggle-Goodyear 1970, Moderan 2003, Merrills 2023).
