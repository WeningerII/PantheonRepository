================================================================================
THE GAP — THE REGISTRY HELD ONE SIDE OF EVERY CONFLICT
================================================================================

A 138-name check across the WHOLE registry returned 17 hits and 121 absences,
and six of the seventeen hits are FALSE: "Uttara" matched mon_uttara, "Kurma"
matched fur_kuruma, "Sati" matched egyptian_satis, "Mangala" matched
mandinka_maa_ngala, "Ila" matched koryak_illa, and "Bali" matched hindu_vali —
which is the vanara king of the Ramayana and NOT the asura king of the Vamana
story. Five more were aliases already carried on existing records.

The 133 records were not thinly spread. They are dense in places: the corpus
holds five sons of Karna. What it does not hold is the other side of anything.

**THE REGISTRY HELD THE PANDAVAS AND NOT THE KAURAVAS.**

All five Pandavas are there. Draupadi, Kunti, Madri, Pandu, Dhritarashtra,
Bhishma, Vyasa, Satyavati, Drupada, Dhrishtadyumna, Abhimanyu, Karna and five
of Karna's sons are there.

There is not one Kaurava. No **Duryodhana**. No **Gandhari**, who tied a cloth
over her own eyes for life because her husband was born blind, and who bore the
hundred. No **Dushasana**, no **Shakuni**, no **Vikarna**. And none of the men
the war is actually fought by on that side: no **Drona**, who taught both
houses; no **Kripa**; no **Ashwatthama**; no **Kritavarma**; no **Jayadratha**.

The Mahabharata is a war between two families and the registry held one of them.

It is the same fault everywhere else in the tradition:

- **Indra without Vritra.** The corpus holds Indra. Indra's defining act, the
  one the Rigveda returns to more than any other, is killing the serpent who
  has seized and imprisoned the waters of the world. The serpent was not there.
- **Durga without Mahishasura.** Durga is in the corpus. Her name in worship is
  Mahishasuramardini, the slayer of the buffalo demon, and the largest festival
  in eastern India commemorates that killing. The buffalo demon was not there.
- **Narasimha without Hiranyakashipu, and without Prahlada.** The corpus holds
  the man-lion avatar, whose entire reason for existing is to kill one
  particular asura at one particular moment — neither man nor beast, neither
  day nor night, neither inside nor out — in order to save one particular boy.
  Neither the asura nor the boy was in the corpus.
- **Varaha without Hiranyaksha**, whose abduction of the earth is the only
  reason the boar avatar occurs.
- **Dasharatha's three queens: two held, one absent.** Kausalya and Sumitra
  were in the corpus. **Kaikeyi** was not — the queen whose two boons send Rama
  into the forest for fourteen years, which is the event the Ramayana consists
  of. This is the same shape as lane 14's five-of-six idols of Kiev, and worse,
  because the missing one is the one that makes the story happen.
- **Ravana without Mandodari, Shurpanakha or Maricha** — his queen, who tells
  him to give Sita back; his sister, whose mutilated face is why he takes her;
  and the demon who becomes the golden deer.
- **No rishis at all.** Not Vasishtha, not Vishvamitra, not Agastya, not Atri,
  not Narada — and not **Valmiki**, who composed the Ramayana the corpus holds
  twenty-odd characters from.
- **No lunar dynasty above the Kurus.** No Yayati, no Nahusha, no Puru, no
  Yadu — so the Kauravas and the Yadavas, the two houses the whole epic is
  about, had no common ancestor in the corpus and no reason to be related.
- **No Navagraha.** No Brihaspati, no Shukra, no Shani, no Rahu, no Ketu.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BATCH DOES AND DOES NOT DO
================================================================================

This lane takes the conflicts. Forty-four records: the Kauravas and the Kuru
court, the adversaries the gods are defined against, the lunar-dynasty
ancestors who make the two houses kin, Krishna's antagonists, and the missing
half of the Ramayana.

It does NOT take the Navagraha, the remaining avatars (Kurma, Parashurama,
Kalki), the Vedic gods still absent (Mitra, Rudra as a separate record, Pushan,
Tvashtr, Bhaga, Aryaman, the Maruts), the Devi Mahatmya's other asuras (Shumbha,
Nishumbha, Raktabija), or the Ikshvaku dynasty. Those are census rows here and
the obvious shape of the next Hindu lane.

**The hundred Kauravas are not ingested as a hundred.** The sources name a
handful — Duryodhana, Dushasana, Vikarna and Chitrasena are the ones the
tradition itself singles out — and the rest are a number, not a list. Four
brothers and their sister's absence are recorded; ninety-six name-slots are not
manufactured.

================================================================================
A NOTE ON ERAS, WHICH IS A FINDING RATHER THAN A CHOICE
================================================================================

The corpus files `hindu_dhritarashtra` at era `epic-period` and his nephews the
Pandavas at `dvapara-yuga`, which in the registered Hindu era order is EARLIER.
The Kauravas added here are therefore filed at `epic-period`, matching their
father, because a parent may not be later than a child and the test suite
enforces it. The result is that first cousins sit in two different eras.

That is not a defect introduced by this batch — it is a pre-existing
inconsistency in how the Hindu era vocabulary has been applied, and this batch
declines to fix it by mutating records it did not author. It is raised in the
census and the audit for the owner.

AUTHORED FIGURES — III. THE ADVERSARIES THE GODS ARE DEFINED AGAINST
================================================================================

Four gods in this corpus are named, worshipped and depicted in terms of one
killing each, and the corpus held none of the four killed. Indra without Vritra,
Durga without Mahishasura, Narasimha without Hiranyakashipu, Varaha without
Hiranyaksha.
