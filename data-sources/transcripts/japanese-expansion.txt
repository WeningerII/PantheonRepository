================================================================================
THE GAP
================================================================================

A 61-name check across the WHOLE registry returned 10 hits and 51 absences,
and six of the ten were deliberate controls. Two of the remaining four are
false: "Takemikazuchi" matched `japanese_kojiki_takemikazuchi_miwa`, who is a
human scion of the Miwa line and not the thunder kami of Kashima; and
"Bishamonten" and "Emma" matched `buddhist_vaishravana` and `buddhist_yama`,
which are the Indian ends of two transmissions rather than the Japanese
figures.

The registry's 48 Japanese records are not what the number suggests. Roughly
twenty of them are a single late-Heian Minamoto genealogy — Yoshiie, Yoshitomo,
Yoritomo, Yoshitsune and their kin — and another seven are a Kojiki
mythohistorical sub-branch around the Miwa line. **The kami pantheon proper
was about twenty records**, and what it was missing was the structure:

- **The Kotoamatsukami.** The first five kami, who come into being alone in
  Takamagahara without procreation, were absent in their entirety — including
  Ame-no-Minakanushi, who is the first being in the Kojiki.
- **The rock cave.** Amaterasu and Ame-no-Uzume were here; Omoikane, who
  devised the plan, Ame-no-Tajikarao, who hauled the door open, Ishikoridome,
  who made the mirror, and Ame-no-Koyane and Futodama, who did the divination,
  were not. The single most important episode in Japanese myth had two of its
  participants in the registry.
- **The Izumo cycle.** Susanoo was here; Yamata-no-Orochi, whom he killed,
  Kushinada-hime, whom he married, and her parents were not.
- **The kuni-yuzuri.** Ōkuninushi was here; the envoys who came to take the
  land from him and the two sons who answered them were not — so the
  transaction on which the legitimacy of the Japanese imperial house rests had
  one party.
- **The Hyūga cycle.** Ninigi and Konohanasakuya-hime were here; their sons
  Hoori and Hoderi, the sea-god's daughters Toyotama-hime and Tamayori-hime,
  and Ugayafukiaezu were not — which means the descent from Amaterasu to the
  first emperor broke off two generations short.
- **Jimmu.** The first emperor was absent, as were Yamato Takeru and Empress
  Jingū.
- **Five of the Seven Lucky Gods.** Ebisu and Benzaiten were here.
- **Tenjin**, who is Sugawara no Michizane, the most widely worshipped deified
  human in Japan.

================================================================================
A NOTE ON WHAT THIS BATCH DOES NOT DO
================================================================================

It does not ingest **yōkai as classes**. Tengu, kappa, kitsune, oni and tanuki
are kinds of being, and this programme does not record kinds. Three *named*
yōkai are here — Shuten-dōji, Tamamo-no-Mae and Yamata-no-Orochi — because
they are individuals with names, acts and opponents.

It does not resolve the **Buddhist/Japanese tradition-key split**. Bishamonten
matched `buddhist_vaishravana` on the sweep and is nonetheless recorded here,
because the Japanese member of the Seven Lucky Gods is a different figure with
a different office in a different group; the census records the transmission
as a CROSS-TRADITION row rather than merging or ignoring it. Kannon, Jizō,
Amida and Fudō Myōō are blocked for the same reason and left for a deliberate
pass: they are major Japanese cult figures and also Buddhist ones, and which
key they belong under is the owner's call, now raised for the fifth lane
running.

================================================================================
AUTHORED FIGURES — II. THE ROCK CAVE
================================================================================

Susanoo's behaviour drives Amaterasu into the cave of heaven and the world goes
dark. The eight million gods hold a council outside, and the plan they execute
is the founding act of Japanese ritual: a mirror is forged, a jewel-string
strung, a divination performed, a dance danced, and when the sun looks out to
see what the laughing is about a strong kami hauls the door aside and another
ties a rope across it so she cannot go back.

The registry held the sun and the dancer. It did not hold the strategist, the
strongman, the smith, or the two priests — who are also the ancestors of the
two clans that ran Japanese court ritual for a thousand years.

================================================================================
AUTHORED FIGURES — III. IZUMO: THE SERPENT, AND THE TRANSFER OF THE LAND
================================================================================

Two episodes, both set in Izumo and both absent from the registry except for
their principals.

The first: Susanoo, expelled from heaven, comes down beside the Hi river and
finds an old couple and their last daughter weeping, because a serpent from
Koshi has eaten seven of the eight. He turns the girl into a comb, gets the
serpent drunk, cuts it up, and finds a sword in its tail.

The second: the kuni-yuzuri, in which Amaterasu sends envoys to demand that
Ōkuninushi hand over the land he has built. The Japanese imperial house's
claim to legitimacy is a title deed and this is the conveyance. The registry
held the party giving up the land and no one on the other side of the table.

================================================================================

The Shichifukujin were assembled in the Muromachi period out of four religions
— Shinto, Buddhism, Hinduism and Daoism — and formalised, the tradition says,
by the priest Tenkai, adviser to Tokugawa Ieyasu, who matched each to a virtue
of the ideal statesman. Only one of the seven, Ebisu, is of purely Japanese
origin, and the registry held him and Benzaiten and none of the other five.

They are filed under `mythic`, as the registry's existing two are, rather than
split across the dynasties their individual origins would imply; the Muromachi
assembly and the Edo popularity are on each record.

AUTHORED FIGURES — VIII. HEIAN: A GOD MADE OUT OF A GRIEVANCE, AND THE
                         DEMON-SLAYERS
================================================================================

Nine figures from the tenth to twelfth centuries, all absent. Sugawara no
Michizane, who was exiled by a rival and whose angry ghost was promoted to
kami of the highest rank to stop it killing people. Two of the three creatures
Japanese tradition names as the greatest yōkai — Shuten-dōji, king of the oni,
and Tamamo-no-Mae, the nine-tailed fox. And the men who dealt with them:
Minamoto no Yorimitsu, his four retainers, and the onmyōji Abe no Seimei.

The registry already holds about twenty late-Heian Minamoto — the warrior
genealogy from Yoshiie down to Yoshitsune — and not Yorimitsu, who is the
member of that clan the folklore is about.
