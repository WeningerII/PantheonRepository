Tradition: Etruscan
Eras (registered, exact): villanovan | orientalizing | archaic | classical | hellenistic | roman-period
Author pass: Lane 1 expansion — the Etruscan tradition held only 17 records for
             one of the best-documented pre-Roman pantheons in Italy. This pass
             adds 33 further attested individuated figures.

================================================================================
SOURCING NOTE
================================================================================

This session has no direct outbound web access; the environment's egress policy
refuses en.wikipedia.org, britishmuseum.org, perseus.tufts.edu, britannica.com
and the rest. Research was done through server-side web search, which returns
the substance of those pages without fetching them. Citations therefore name
the reference work or the attesting object, and no printed monograph is cited
as though it had been read cover to cover.

Principal evidence classes used:
  - The Liver of Piacenza (bronze haruspical model, 2nd-1st c. BCE), whose
    outer rim carries sixteen abbreviated theonyms. The abbreviations are
    disputed for most regions; only names with independent corroboration are
    ingested from it, and the disputed-reading problem is stated on each record
    that rests on it.
  - Etruscan engraved bronze mirrors, which carry roughly 300 inscriptions
    naming the figures they depict — the main source for Alpanu, Achvizr,
    Thanr, Thalna, Mean, Ethausva and the Lasa-type attendants.
  - Tomb painting, above all the François Tomb at Vulci (Cacu, the Vibenna
    brothers, Macstrna) and the Tomb of Orcus (Tuchulcha).
  - Graeco-Roman report: Varro on Voltumna as deus Etruriae princeps, Livy on
    the Fanum Voltumnae and the nail-rite of Nortia, Tertullian Apol. 24.8 on
    Nortia of Volsinii, the land-surveyors' corpus for the prophecy of Vegoia.

Identity discipline follows the precedent already in this corpus: Etruscan
counterparts of Greek and Roman gods are kept as their own records (Tinia is
not merged into Zeus or Jupiter; `oscan_hercle` is already separate from
`greek_apollod_heracles`). Where a scholarly position would collapse two of
these records into one — Aplu as an epithet of Śuri — both are kept and the
position is recorded on each, with an explicit equated-with edge rather than a
silent merge.
