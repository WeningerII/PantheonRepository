Yámana (Yaghan) spirit-world — figure-authoring transcript
==========================================================

Tradition: Yámana (Yaghan), the canoe-people of the Beagle Channel and the
southern Fuegian archipelago, Tierra del Fuego. Registered era key (the only
one valid for this tradition): mythic-primordial.

Research scope: The Pantheon Registry already holds the supreme being
Watauinéwa (yamana_watauinewa) and the dualistic culture-hero Yoálox brothers
(yamana_yoalox_brothers). This pass authors the well-attested CORE of the
remaining named spirit-world: the fearsome initiation-spirit Yetaita, the Sun
(Lëm), the Moon-woman (Hánuxa), the sky/rainbow figure Akáinix, and the
water/sea spirit Lakúma.

The principal documentation is Martin Gusinde's monograph Die Feuerland-Indianer
II: Die Yamana (1937; fieldwork 1918–1924; Eng. trans. The Yamana, HRAF 1961)
and the narrative collection assembled from his materials by Johannes Wilbert,
Folk Literature of the Yamana Indians: Martin Gusinde's Collection of Yamana
Narratives (UCLA Latin American Studies 40, 1977). E. Lucas Bridges, Uttermost
Part of the Earth (1948), supplies the family's first-hand testimony and the
flood material associated with the Moon-woman.

The Sun, the Moon-woman, and the rainbow-man belong to the great kína myth: in
primeval time the women ruled the men by impersonating spirits in the secret
lodge (the kína); when the men discovered the deception they rose and killed
the women, and the chief actors of that age ascended into the sky — the woman
Hánuxa becoming the Moon, the man Lëm the Sun, and Akáinix the rainbow. The
kína ceremony (last performed 1922 on Navarino, attended by Gusinde and
Koppers) re-enacts that myth; the malevolent spirit Yetaita, impersonated by
masked, body-painted initiates of the men's lodge, emerges to terrify the
novices (ushwáala).

Anchored decisions and notes:
  * YETAITA — the dreaded malevolent spirit of the men's kína. He is OPPOSED to
    the supreme being Watauinéwa but is expressly NOT his rival or equal
    ("there cannot be rivalry between them"; Yetaita "does not have the same
    importance as the creator god"). He is therefore NOT wired as an enemy of
    Watauinéwa, and is flagged SOLITARY (kinless, with cited reason).
  * LËM ↔ AKÁINIX sibling — the stable, unambiguous relation: the Spanish
    Ser-Indígena / El Rompehielos tradition names Akáinix expressly "hermano de
    Lem el sol" (brother of Lëm the Sun). Wired reciprocally within the batch.
  * HÁNUXA ↔ LËM spouse — wired per the English Ser-Indígena passage (Hánuxa
    wife of Lëm the Sun). SOURCES CONFLICT: the Spanish Ser-Indígena /
    El Rompehielos tradition instead makes Hánuxa (Janusha) the wife of Akáinix
    the rainbow. This variant is NOTED in Hánuxa's notes. Wired reciprocally
    within the batch as Hánuxa↔Lëm.
  * LAKÚMA — the dangerous sea/water spirit; a kinless monster-spirit with no
    attested individual kin, flagged SOLITARY.
  * HÁNNUSH — a separate GIANT in Gusinde/Wilbert, NOT the Moon-woman Hánuxa;
    NOT conflated and OMITTED here (a spirit-class/giant without the
    individuated mythic biography the core requires).
  * OMITTED as verified-unattested per the prior pass: Cilawáia, Kalampáso,
    Tánuwa.
