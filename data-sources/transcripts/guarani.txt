Authoring the Guaraní pantheon CORE from scratch — two coherent, firmly-attested layers.

VERIFICATION NOTES (all figures cross-checked against the named scholarly corpus + reference works):

LAYER A — the Mbyá-Guaraní cosmogonic high gods (León Cadogan, *Ayvu Rapyta: Textos míticos de los Mbyá-Guaraní del Guairá*, Universidade de São Paulo, Boletim 227, Antropologia no. 5, 1959; Curt Nimuendajú, *Die Sagen von der Erschaffung und Vernichtung der Welt als Grundlagen der Religion der Apapocúva-Guaraní*, Zeitschrift für Ethnologie 46, 1914). Cadogan's Chapter I ("Maino'i reko ypykue" — the genesis) is the locus classicus: Ñande Ru Papa Tenondé / Ñamandú Ru Ete unfolds out of the primordial darkness; from the wisdom contained in his own divinity (kuaa-rara) and "as the sun rises" he unfolds the foundation of human speech (ayvu rapyta) and the sacred word-soul (ñe'ẽ), the flower-crown adorned with feathers (jeguaka) and the divine flames and life-giving mist that form his heart. By virtue of that same creative wisdom he then "caused to know of his divinity" the future-true-fathers of Karai, of Jakaira, and of Tupã — and each of the four, together with his own consort, caused the future-true-MOTHER (Ñamandú Chy Ete, Karai Chy Ete, Jakaira Chy Ete, Tupã Chy Ete) to know divinity, to stand facing his heart. So Cadogan attests BOTH the four divine fathers AND their four paired mothers — I author all eight. The twins Kuarahy (the Sun) and Jasy (the Moon) are the demiurgic brothers of the Guaraní twin-myth; in the Mbyá recension the elder is Pa'i Rete Kuaray (Kuaray, the Sun), child of the god and the maiden Ñande Jary, and Jasy (the Moon) is his younger brother — I wire them as twin brothers, sons of Ñande Ru (Ñamandú). Jasy is the trickster/erring younger twin (the premature look/laugh that spoils the resurrection of their mother).

LAYER B — the seven monstrous children of Tau and Keraná (Paraguayan-Guaraní folk mythology; systematized by Narciso R. Colmán [Rosicrán], *Ñande Ypy Kuéra / Nuestros antepasados*, 1929; documented by Dionisio M. González Torres, *Folklore del Paraguay*, Editorial Comuneros / Editora Litocolor, Asunción, 1980s, repr. 1995; Moisés S. Bertoni, *La civilización guaraní*, 1922). Keraná, daughter of the pious Marangatú, is seized/seduced by the evil spirit Tau; the goddess Arasy curses their union, and Keraná bears seven children born as monsters — in birth order: Teju Jagua, Mbói Tu'ĩ, Moñái, Jasy Jateré, Kurupi, Ao Ao, Luisón. The seven are full siblings; collectively associated with the Pleiades (Eichu). I wire the seven as reciprocal siblings, children of Tau + Keraná, and Keraná as daughter of Marangatú (noted, not authored — Marangatú is a minor pious-ancestor figure, kept as an external note rather than a stub).

Note on the four mothers: Ñamandú/Karai/Jakaira/Tupã Chy Ete are each wired only as spouse of their respective father (no parents of their own are attested — they are caused-to-know-divinity, not begotten). This is the canonical Cadogan structure, not an omission.
Note on Marangatú (Keraná's father) and Arasy (the cursing goddess): both are attested in the folk sources but kept as in-text references rather than authored stubs — Marangatú as a minor pious ancestor, Arasy as a figure whose principal role here is the curse; neither has the developed profile warranting a separate figure in this CORE batch.
