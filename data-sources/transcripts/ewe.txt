EWE (Eʋe) PANTHEON — vodu (deities) of the Ewe-speaking peoples of Ghana, Togo and Benin.
Figure-authoring transcript for the Pantheon Registry. tradition = "Ewe"; era = "primordial" only; id prefix "ewe_".

SCOPE & METHOD
--------------
This batch authors the firmly-attested great vodu of the Ewe (Gbe) pantheon that were still
missing from the corpus. The existing figures are NOT re-authored and are referenced by id:
  ewe_mawu_lisa (twofold creator pair), ewe_mawu (sky-mother), ewe_lisa (sun-lord),
  ewe_nana_buluku (primordial grandmother), ewe_so (the thunder-god So/Sogbo/Heviesso).

The Ewe and the neighbouring Fon share a single Gbe religious complex; the classic
ethnographies treat them together. The seven great vodu born of the creator pair Mawu-Lisa
are, in the canonical Dahomean/Ewe account: Sakpata (eldest, the earth and smallpox), Sogbo
(thunder — the existing ewe_so), Agbe (the sea), Age (the hunt and bush), Gu (iron and war),
Djo (air), and Legba (the youngest, the messenger). This batch adds Sakpata, Age, Gu and
Legba as children of the existing ewe_mawu_lisa, making them siblings of one another and of
the existing ewe_so (Sogbo) — the latter requiring reciprocal edges on the existing figure
(see RECIPROCAL-NEEDED). It further adds Da/Dã (Aido-Hwedo / the Ewe Anyi-eʋo), the
rainbow-serpent who girdles the world; Afa/Fa, the vodu of divination and destiny; and
Avlekete, the sea vodu. Those three are recorded with SOLITARY flags: in the attested
tradition the world-serpent is a pre-existent cosmic power (not begotten by Mawu-Lisa), the
oracle-vodu Fa is not a consistently-begotten son of the pair, and Avlekete's parentage is
given variously (child of the sea-pair Agbe/Naete, or of Sogbo) with neither parent authored
in this batch.

Sources are the standard ethnographies named for the task — Jakob Spieth, Die Ewe-Stämme
(1906) and Die Religion der Eweer in Süd-Togo (1911); A. B. Ellis, The Ewe-Speaking Peoples
of the Slave Coast of West Africa (1890); E. G. Parrinder, West African Religion (1949/1961);
M. J. Herskovits, Dahomey: An Ancient West African Kingdom (1938, for the cognate Fon
vodun) — with corroborating specialist sources where they fix an Ewe-specific term or object.

RELATION NOTES
--------------
- ewe_sakpata, ewe_age, ewe_gu, ewe_legba are children of the existing ewe_mawu_lisa
  (directed parent/child; no reciprocal required toward the parent).
- Those four are siblings of one another (reciprocal "sibling" WITHIN this batch) and siblings
  of the existing thunder-god ewe_so (Sogbo). The four sibling-edges toward ewe_so are emitted
  as RECIPROCAL-NEEDED so the reciprocal can be added to the existing figure.
- ewe_legba is the interpreter/messenger of Fa's oracle: a NON-symmetric "associate of" edge
  ewe_legba -> ewe_afa (no reciprocal required); Afa carries the converse note.
- ewe_da, ewe_afa, ewe_avlekete carry SOLITARY flags with cited reasons (below).

SOLITARY:
- ewe_da — The world-serpent Da/Dã (Aido-Hwedo; the Ewe rainbow-serpent Anyi-eʋo) is a PRE-EXISTENT cosmic power, present at and serving the making of the world rather than begotten within it: he carries the creator Mawu-Lisa during creation and then coils to uphold the finished earth. The tradition expressly does not number him among the begotten children of Mawu-Lisa ("nobody knows where the serpent came from; it is not one of Mawu's deities"). No parent, consort or offspring is authored for him in this batch (M. J. Herskovits, Dahomey, 1938, vol. 2, the cosmic serpent; A. B. Ellis, The Ewe-Speaking Peoples of the Slave Coast, 1890, the rainbow-serpent Anyi-eʋo). The Fon couple the serpent-power with a female counterpart, but that figure is not authored here.
- ewe_afa — Afa/Fa, the vodu and oracle of divination and destiny, is not a consistently-begotten son of Mawu-Lisa; it is the personified oracle of fate. In Fon theology the source of Fa's power is the female sky-spirit Gbadu, set by Mawu atop the palm-tree, and the oracle's messages are interpreted by Legba — a relation captured by the non-symmetric edge ewe_legba "associate of" ewe_afa rather than by parentage. Neither Gbadu nor any begetting parent is authored in this batch (E. G. Parrinder, West African Religion, 1961; M. J. Herskovits, Dahomey, 1938, vol. 2, the cult of Fa).
- ewe_avlekete — The sea-and-lagoon vodu Avlekete is attested as a power of the water-pantheon, but the sources give her descent variously — junior/consort of the sea-deity Agbe and his sister-wife Naete in one tradition, a junior of the thunderer Sogbo in another — and none of those potential parents is authored in this batch, so no firm parent-edge can be drawn (M. J. Herskovits, Dahomey, 1938, vol. 2, the sea-pantheon; "Fon and Ewe Religion," Encyclopedia of Religion, 2005).
