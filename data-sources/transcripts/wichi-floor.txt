================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added two genuinely individuated, named Wichí divine beings absent from the existing set: Nilataj, the remote celestial creator documented by Braunstein (1974) who framed the earth and inverted sky and earth, and katés lhukwetáj, the Star Woman/Venus analysed by Gómez (2008) and Barúa (2001) as the personified female sky-being who descends to marry a hunter. Both are attested in multiple scholarly sources and are distinct from the corpus's Sun, Moon, Ahát, Lawo' and Tokwaj. I deliberately excluded the Pleiades (Potseslhay/Pätselhay) and the forest and animal 'owners' (dueños), which the ethnography treats as spirit-classes rather than individuated named persons, and I left both new figures unwired because their sources attest no genealogical link to existing corpus members (the creation-myth helper 'Ahötaj' overlaps lexically with the already-registered Ahát but the identification is uncertain, so I note it in prose rather than asserting a relation).
