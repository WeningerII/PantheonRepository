================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added three genuinely individuated Me'phaa (Tlapanec) deities absent from the existing set: Akuun mbaa, the god of the earth who owns the animals and gives seeds and edible plants; Akuun júba, the lord of the mountain and owner of wild game to whom hunters pray; and Bego (Totonásha), the lightning-and-rain god, whom myth makes a son of the existing fire god Akuun mbatsuun' (wired via parentIds and a subordinate-to relation). All three are consistently documented in Danièle Dehouve's ethnographies of Tlapanec sacrificial ritual and in corroborating Me'phaa cosmology scholarship. I deliberately did not author the localized 'lord of water' figure a second time, since the corpus already carries Akuniya of La Ciénaga and the generic 'Akuun iya' title overlaps with it; the remaining Tlapanec record (a male/female high-god pair and unnamed 'impersonal' gods noted by Schultze Jena) is not individuated enough to yield further named figures.
