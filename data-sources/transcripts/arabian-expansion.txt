PANTHEON REGISTRY — FIGURE-AUTHORING TRANSCRIPT
Tradition: Arabian (pre-Islamic, Hijazi and North Arabian)
Eras (registered, exact): pantheon-era; mu-allaqat-poetic; south-arabian-kingdoms; modern-collection
Author pass: the Arabian key held 12 records. This adds 25.

================================================================================
THE GAP — FOUR GODS, EIGHT POETS, AND NOT ONE OTHER IDOL
================================================================================

The Arabian key held **twelve records**:

    Hubal, Allat, al-'Uzza, Manat      four Hijazi deities
    Imru' al-Qais, 'Antara, Hatim      eight figures of the Mu'allaqat
    al-Tai, 'Abla, 'Adi, Hujr,         and the poetic tradition
    Shaddad, Zabiba

The four deities are **exactly the four the Qur'an argues with** — Hubal at the
Ka'ba, and the three "daughters of Allah" of Surat al-Najm. Beyond them the key
held **no idol at all**: not the five of Surat Nuh, not Isaf and Na'ila on the
two hills of the Meccan rite, not Manaf, whom the sources say was once the most
important deity in Mecca.

And it held no **people of the cult** — no Amr ibn Luhayy, who in the tradition's
own telling brought Hubal to Mecca; no Qusayy ibn Kilab, who set Isaf and Na'ila
by Zamzam; and not one **kahin**, though the kahins were the religious leaders of
pre-Islamic Hijazi spirituality. Eight poets and no priests.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS LANE IS NOT — A SCOPE CORRECTION MADE BEFORE AUTHORING
================================================================================

This lane began from the assumption that the Arabian key was missing the South
Arabian and Nabataean pantheons too. **The sweep disproved that before a line was
written.** The corpus already holds them under their own keys:

    Sabaean     Almaqah, Athtar, Shams, Sin, Wadd, 'Amm       (6)
    Nabataean   Dushara, Allat, al-Uzza, Manat, Qaws          (5)
    Palmyrene   Bel, Baalshamin, Aglibol, Yarhibol, Malakbel  (6)
    Aksumite    Almaqah, Astar, Beher, Mahrem, Medr, ...      (7)

So this lane is scoped to the layer that genuinely has no key of its own: **the
Hijazi and North Arabian idols and the people of their cult.** Deities belonging
to the Nabataean or South Arabian keys are censused BLOCKED FOR THIS LANE, with
the blocker being the key and not the evidence — they are real and should be
ingested, just not here and not under this name.

================================================================================
THE SOURCE, AND ITS RELIABILITY, STATED UP FRONT
================================================================================

Nearly all of this batch rests on **Ibn al-Kalbi's Kitab al-Asnam** (early 9th c.
CE), which the corpus's four existing Arabian deity records already cite as a
primary source. It is the foundational Arabic compendium of Arabian idol-cult: an
itemised list of nearly thirty idols with their tribes and territories.

**It is also a source many historians today do not consider reliable** for
Arabian religion before Islam. Ibn al-Kalbi wrote in the early ninth century from
Arab oral tradition, about a religion that had been suppressed for two hundred
years, and with an evident interest in its suppression.

That caveat is carried **inside the source blob attached to every record in this
batch**, not buried in an audit — so it travels with the data rather than with
the paperwork.

================================================================================
BLOCK 1 — THE FIVE IDOLS OF QUR'AN 71:23
================================================================================

**Wadd, Suwa', Yaghuth, Ya'uq and Nasr** — the five idols Noah's people were
urged not to abandon, and the best-attested list of pre-Islamic Arabian gods
there is, since it stands in the Qur'an itself. The commentators give each a form:
a man, a woman, a lion, a horse and an eagle. The tribes are named too: Kalb,
Hudhayl, Murad and Madhhij, Hamdan, Himyar.

**Only one of the five resolved in the corpus, and it resolved to the wrong key.**
`Wadd` matched `sabaean_wadd` — the South Arabian moon-god — not the Qur'anic Wadd
of the Kalb at Dumat al-Jandal. That is the **fourth sweep failure mode** this
programme named two lanes ago, arriving now in its third consecutive tradition.

================================================================================
BLOCK 2 — THE MECCAN IDOLS AND THE PRE-ISLAMIC ALLAH
================================================================================

**Isaf and Na'ila** stood on as-Safa and al-Marwa, the two hills of the Meccan
running-rite, and guarded the well of Zamzam. The legend makes them a man and a
woman **petrified for committing adultery inside the Ka'ba** — so the Quraysh
worshipped, on their two most sacred hills, two people the story says were turned
to stone for defiling the sanctuary. They are authored as a pair, reciprocally
linked, because holding one would leave the rite with a single terminus.

**Manaf** was, the sources say, once the most important deity at Mecca. The corpus
held the four gods the Qur'an argues with and not the one they displaced.

**Allah, the pre-Islamic Meccan deity**, is this lane's most sensitive call and is
made carefully. The record is scoped **by its primary name** to the pre-Islamic
Meccan figure the sources describe, and records exactly what they say: a deity
some scholars count among those the Ka'ba was dedicated to, the guardian of
contracts, of little relevance in the Meccan religion, whose chief god was Hubal.
**The 1901 lunar-deity theory of Hugo Winckler is recorded only together with its
rejection** — opponents reject it as speculative and unsupported by any
archaeological or textual evidence from pre-Islamic Arabia — and this registry
does not carry it as a live reading.

================================================================================
BLOCK 3 — THE TRIBAL IDOLS OF THE BOOK OF IDOLS
================================================================================

Eight idols, each with a tribe and a place. **Dhu'l-Khalasa** was an oracle of the
Daws, Bajila and Khath'am at Tabala, and his temple was called **the Ka'ba of
Yemen**. **Dhu'l-Ka'bat**, of Bakr and Taghlib at Sandad, carries the same root —
two sanctuaries in one batch named against the Meccan one.

Several of these gods **were not made objects but found ones**: **Sa'd** of the
Kinana was a tall stone in open desert, and **al-Fals** of Tayy was an outcrop of
red granite on the black mountain of Jabal Aja that happened to take the shape of
a man. With the two petrified Meccans, that is four cults in this batch whose idol
is geology rather than sculpture.

**Dhu al-Kaffayn** is recorded with his destruction as precisely as his cult —
burned by al-Tufayl ibn 'Amr al-Dawsi. **Nuhm** survives mostly in the personal
name 'Abd-Nuhm. **Umyanis** and **Ri'am** are each authored on a single sentence,
and the records claim nothing beyond it.

================================================================================
BLOCK 4 — THE NORTH ARABIAN TRINITY
================================================================================

Every Arabian record before this batch was Hijazi or a poet. **The North Arabian
epigraphic layer — Lihyanite, Safaitic, Thamudic — had no representation at all.**

**Ruda, Nuha and Atarsamain** form a trinity in that material. Two entries in this
block are deliberately imprecise, because the evidence is:

* **Ruda's sex is recorded as `varies`**, because the sources say so explicitly:
  MALE in the Lihyanite and north-western inscriptions, where the name is written
  Rdw, and FEMALE among the Safaitic Bedouin. Picking one would have concealed the
  most interesting thing about the deity.
* **Atarsamain's sex is recorded as `unknown`**, because the evidence found names
  the figure only as the third of a trinity, and nothing in this pass settles it.

================================================================================
BLOCK 5 — THE PEOPLE OF THE PRE-ISLAMIC CULT
================================================================================

**Amr ibn Luhayy** is the man the tradition blames. He went from Mecca to the Sham
lands, saw idols worshipped at Ma'ab in the Balqa, was convinced that idols could
act as **intermediaries** for God, and brought Hubal home. **The corpus held the
chief idol of Mecca and not the man its own sources say fetched him.** His stated
reasoning is recorded as the sources give it — intermediaries, not replacements.

**Qusayy ibn Kilab**, chief of Mecca and leader of Quraysh, took the stones of
Isaf and Na'ila to Zamzam. Between him and Amr, the Ka'ba's idols acquire a
history with actors instead of a timeless pagan backdrop.

**Zarqa al-Yamama** saw riders a week away and was not believed in time; "sharper
than Zarqa al-Yamama" became a proverb, which is how her story survived at all.

**Satih and Shiqq** are the two kahins, consulted together by Rabi'a ibn Nasr and
giving the same prophecy. The sources are emphatic that the kahin was a religious
office and not a curiosity: the kahins were the religious leaders of pre-Islamic
Hijazi spirituality, gave imprecations and benedictions, alone offered the
rain-prayer *istisqa*, and had garments and saliva with healing power and sacred,
potent hair. **The corpus held eight pre-Islamic poets and not one of its priests.**

VERIFICATION NOTES
================================================================================

  * **No figure in this batch authors any parentIds**, so tier-classification
    drift is impossible by construction. The twenty gods are `deity` and the five
    people are `mortal`, matching the existing split in this key exactly.
  * All twenty-five carry the registered era `pantheon-era`. The key's other era,
    `mu-allaqat-poetic`, is specifically the poetic tradition and is NOT stretched
    to cover a cult-founder, a seeress or a soothsayer.
  * **Arabic script is supplied only where the name stands in a text this pass
    actually cites** — the five of Qur'an 71:23 — **or where the romanisation maps
    to a single unambiguous Arabic spelling.** Where a romanisation could map to
    more than one plausible consonantal skeleton (Umyanis, Ri'am, Dhu'l-Ka'bat,
    Atarsamain, Nuhm, Dhu al-Kaffayn, Ruda, Nuha, Satih, Shiqq) the field is left
    EMPTY rather than reconstructed. Fifteen of twenty-five records carry an
    Arabic form and ten do not.
  * Every unresolved etymology says "not resolved in the sources consulted".
  * Two records deliberately carry an imprecise `sex`: Ruda as `varies` and
    Atarsamain as `unknown`, in both cases because the evidence is what is
    imprecise.
  * Symmetric and paired relations inside the batch are reciprocated (Isaf/Na'ila,
    Satih/Shiqq, and the three-way North Arabian trinity). Every link to a
    PRE-EXISTING record — `arabian_hubal` and `sabaean_wadd` — uses a
    NON-SYMMETRIC kind written from this end only, so no pre-existing record is
    mutated.
  * The reliability caveat on the Kitab al-Asnam is carried in the source text
    attached to every record that rests on it.
