Author pass: Aztec/Mexica held 21 records. Every one of them was a deity —
             and the tradition's historical register, the tlatoque of
             Tenochtitlan, was empty. The registry carried ten named Mixtec
             lords and not one Mexica emperor.

================================================================================
THE GAP
================================================================================

A 60-name check across the WHOLE registry returned three hits and
**fifty-seven absences**. Two distinct holes:

**The pantheon.** Absent: Xolotl; Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli; Nanahuatzin and
Tecciztecatl, the two gods who jump into the fire at Teotihuacan to make the
fifth sun; Itzpapalotl; Cihuacoatl; Chicomecoatl and Xilonen, the mature and
the tender maize beside a Centeotl the registry already had; Patecatl, husband
of a Mayahuel the registry already had; Xochipilli and both his brothers;
Tonacatecuhtli and Tonacacihuatl, the pair the Aztec sources actually name
where modern writing says "Ometeotl".

**The rulers.** Acamapichtli, Huitzilihuitl, Chimalpopoca, Itzcoatl, Moctezuma
I, Axayacatl, Tizoc, Ahuitzotl, Moctezuma II, Cuitlahuac, Cuauhtemoc — the
entire tlatoani line — plus Nezahualcoyotl of Texcoco and Tlacaelel, who
arguably ran the state for fifty years without ever holding the title. None of
them was in the registry, in a corpus that carries Egyptian pharaohs,
Mesopotamian kings and ten Mixtec lords by their calendar names.

================================================================================
ONE IDENTITY DECISION UP FRONT — OMETEOTL
================================================================================

The registry already holds `aztec_ometeotl`. The names under which the Aztec
sources actually give the primordial pair are **Tonacatecuhtli and
Tonacacihuatl**, "Lord and Lady of Our Flesh"; Ometecuhtli and Omecihuatl,
"Two-Lord" and "Two-Lady", are the other pair of names for the same couple.
And whether a single fused deity called Ometeotl existed at all is a live
scholarly dispute — Richard Haly argued in 1992 that there was no Ometeotl,
Ometecuhtli or Omecihuatl among the Aztecs and that the whole figure is a
Trinity read into translated texts; James Maffie has answered him.

This pass authors **two records, not four**: Tonacatecuhtli and Tonacacihuatl,
each carrying Ometecuhtli/Omecihuatl among its aliases, each linked
`counterpart-of` to the existing Ometeotl record, and each carrying a
`variants[]` entry that states the dispute instead of resolving it.

AUTHORED FIGURES — VI. THE TLATOQUE OF TENOCHTITLAN
================================================================================

The registry's Aztec/Mexica records were twenty-one deities and no rulers, in a
corpus that carries Egyptian pharaohs, Mesopotamian kings and ten Mixtec lords
by their calendar names. This block adds the whole tlatoani line, plus
Nezahualcoyotl of Texcoco and Tlacaelel, who held the office of Cihuacoatl for
fifty years and is generally reckoned to have run the state from it.

Reign dates are given as the reference treatments give them. For the first two
rulers the codices disagree by as much as a generation — the Codex Chimalpahin,
the Codex Aubin and the Codex Chimalpopoca each give different years — and the
records state the disagreement rather than averaging it away.
