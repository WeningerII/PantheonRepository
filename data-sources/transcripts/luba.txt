PANTHEON REGISTRY — FIGURE-AUTHORING TRANSCRIPT
Tradition: Luba (Baluba) — the Luba peoples of the Upemba Depression / Katanga,
Democratic Republic of the Congo, and the Luba genesis epic (Nkongolo Mwamba /
Mbidi Kiluwe / Kalala Ilunga).
ID prefix: luba_
Eras (registered, exact): "primordial", "mythic", "dynastic"
  - "primordial" = the creator / high god (cosmic, timeless): Kabezya-Mpungu.
  - "mythic"     = the genesis-epic generation (Nkongolo, Mbidi Kiluwe, the sisters
                   Bulanda & Mabela, the diviner Mijibu wa Kalenga) — late 15th–16th c.
  - "dynastic"   = the founding hero-king Kalala Ilunga and the establishment of the
                   empire (popular chronology ~1585; Reefe places the foundation in the
                   later 16th c.).

================================================================================
CHIEF SOURCES
================================================================================
Anchor scholarship (the Luba genesis cycle is an ORAL charter; there is no single
"primary text," so the earliest/most authoritative recensions function as the
primary references):
  • Luc de Heusch, *Le roi ivre, ou l'origine de l'État* (1972) / Eng. *The Drunken
    King, or The Origin of the State* (Indiana Univ. Press, "African Systems of
    Thought," 1982) — the standard structural recension and analysis of the epic.
  • Thomas Q. Reefe, *The Rainbow and the Kings: A History of the Luba Empire to
    1891* (Univ. of California Press, 1981) — the historical reconstruction from
    oral tradition; chronology and the mulopwe institution.
  • Harold Womersley, *Legends and History of the Luba* (Crossroads Press / African
    Studies Center, UCLA, 1984) — collected Luba legend texts.
  • Mary Nooter Roberts & Allen F. Roberts, *Memory: Luba Art and the Making of
    History* (Museum for African Art, New York, 1996) — the lukasa/bow-stand/staff
    regalia, the mnemonic and Bambudye/Mbudye material, and the diviner Mijibu wa
    Kalenga / Bilumbu possession.
For the high god: the standard ethnographic descriptions of Baluba religion
(Kabezya-Mpungu / Mvidi Mukulu / Shakapanga; the four forces Sun, Moon, Darkness,
Rain; Mutshima, "the heart"; the bavidye spirits) — cited as secondary ethnography.

================================================================================
KEY AUTHORING & WIRING DECISIONS
================================================================================
1) GENEALOGY OF THE EPIC (the spine of the batch):
     Kabezya-Mpungu (creator, primordial) — withdrawn high god; NOT a kinsman of the
       epic figures (the charter is anthropogonic/political, not a divine pedigree),
       so no kin edge is drawn from him to the epic generation. Flagged SOLITARY.
     Nkongolo Mwamba (mythic) — autochthonous "red" tyrant-king; BROTHER of Bulanda
       and Mabela; maternal uncle and enemy of Kalala Ilunga.
     Mbidi Kiluwe (mythic) — the black prince-hunter from the east; SPOUSE of both
       Bulanda and Mabela (Nkongolo's sisters); FATHER of Kalala Ilunga.
     Bulanda (mythic) and Mabela (mythic) — Nkongolo's two sisters, Mbidi's wives,
       the mothers in the royal line; SIBLINGS of Nkongolo and of each other.
     Mijibu wa Kalenga (mythic) — the founding diviner-spirit who saves and counsels
       Kalala; ALLY of Kalala. Authored as a numen (a lesser spirit/being, not a god
       and not a simple mortal — every Bilumbu diviner incarnates him in possession).
     Kalala Ilunga (dynastic) — son of Mbidi Kiluwe and Bulanda; defeats and beheads
       Nkongolo and founds the empire. demigod (the half-divine prince-hero who
       inaugurates sacred kingship). type carries bulopwe by descent (inheritability
       "full" on the descent faculty).

2) WHICH SISTER IS KALALA'S MOTHER — a real source divergence, resolved by deferring
   to the named anchor (de Heusch). De Heusch's recension (*The Drunken King*) states
   that Mbidi's wives Bulanda and Mabela each bore a son: **Bulanda's son was Kalala
   Ilunga**, and **Mabela's son was Kisula**. A competing popular/Wikipedia tradition
   reverses this (Mabela → Kalala) and substitutes Tshibinda Ilunga (the Lunda founder)
   for Mabela's son. PER THE ACCURACY RULE, Kalala is wired as the son of **Bulanda**
   (de Heusch), and the divergence is documented in the notes of Kalala, Bulanda and
   Mabela. (Mabela's son Kisula is named only in passing and not individuated here.)

3) TYPES: deity = the creator high god (Kabezya-Mpungu) and the epic protagonists who
   are treated as more-than-mortal cosmic figures (Nkongolo the rainbow-serpent genius;
   Mbidi the celestial/lunar prince-hunter) — both authored as deity, the register the
   charter gives them. numen = Mijibu wa Kalenga (a spirit/genius). demigod = Kalala
   Ilunga (half-divine founder-hero). Bulanda and Mabela, the autochthonous princesses
   who are Nkongolo's own sisters and bear the half-divine line, are authored as deity
   to keep them in the same (autochthonous, more-than-human) register as their brother
   Nkongolo — they are figures of the charter, not historical mortals; the reason is
   recorded in their notes.

4) ERA ANCHORING follows the registry keys exactly: the creator = "primordial"; the
   whole genesis generation (Nkongolo, Mbidi, the sisters, the diviner) = "mythic";
   Kalala Ilunga and the foundation of the empire = "dynastic". Kalala's lifecycle runs
   mythic→dynastic (born in the epic generation; founds the state in the dynastic era).

5) NATIVE TERMS are given in Kiluba (Luba-Katanga) where attested: bulopwe (sacred
   kingship), mulopwe (sacred king), Muntu wa Malwa ("man of misfortune/woe," Nkongolo's
   epithet), Shakapanga / Mvidi Mukulu / Vidye Mukulu (the creator), Mutshima ("heart"),
   bavidye (spirits), bilumbu / Bambudye (diviners / the royal mnemonic association),
   lukasa ("the long hand," the memory board). Where a term is the modern scholarly gloss
   rather than an epic datum, it is attributed to the secondary literature.

FIGURES CONSIDERED BUT NOT AUTHORED (with reasons):
  • Tshibinda Ilunga (Ilunga Tshibinda) — firmly attested, but he is the founder-hero of
    the *Lunda* charter, not the Luba empire; and in de Heusch's Luba recension the second
    son of Mbidi (by Mabela) is **Kisula**, not Tshibinda (the Tshibinda identification is
    the Lunda/popular conflation). To avoid cross-tradition genealogical conflation in a
    Luba batch, Tshibinda is left for a Lunda pass and is noted in Mabela's notes.
  • Kisula — Mabela's son in de Heusch, but named only in passing with no individuated
    deeds/regalia; not authored to avoid padding. Noted in Mabela's notes.
  • Kabongo / Kasongo Nyembo (the historical twin-capital paramount chiefs), and named
    bavidye / nkishi spirit categories — real but later/categorical; deferred. This pass
    covers exactly the securely individuated figures of the genesis cycle and the high god.

SOLITARY:
  • luba_kabezya_mpungu — the withdrawn creator high god. The Luba charter is a political/
    anthropogonic narrative, not a divine genealogy; no specific named divine parent,
    sibling, spouse or child is attested for the creator, and he is NOT a kinsman of the
    epic generation. He is left without a kin edge rather than wired to a guessed relative.

RECIPROCAL RELATIONS (all symmetric ties are reciprocated WITHIN this batch):
  • Nkongolo ↔ Bulanda (sibling); Nkongolo ↔ Mabela (sibling); Bulanda ↔ Mabela (sibling)
  • Mbidi Kiluwe ↔ Bulanda (spouse); Mbidi Kiluwe ↔ Mabela (spouse)
  • Nkongolo ↔ Kalala Ilunga (enemy); Mijibu wa Kalenga ↔ Kalala Ilunga (ally)
  Directed parent edges (no reciprocal required; the father-of/mother-of relation is
  also recorded on the parents for legibility):
  • Kalala Ilunga — child of Mbidi Kiluwe (father) and Bulanda (mother).
