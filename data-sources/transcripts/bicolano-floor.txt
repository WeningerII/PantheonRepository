================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added four figures from the fragmentary Bicolano folk epic Ibalong that individuate the record beyond the already-registered Handyong and Oryol: the pioneer-hero Baltog and the monster he slays, the giant boar Tandayag; the third hero Bantong and the stone-cursing monster Rabot he destroys. These are securely attested in Espinas's edition of the epic and Eugenio's Philippine Folk Literature: The Epics, and are wired to the existing pantheon via Bantong's service under Handyong and slayer/enemy links among the new figures. I also added Bakunawa, the moon-devouring eclipse serpent, which is attested for the Bicol region in Lisboa's Vocabulario de la lengua Bicol. I deliberately excluded the popular "moon goddess" Haliya and the moon god Bulan: current scholarship shows the individuated Haliya-versus-Bakunawa narrative to be a modern embellishment of Lisboa's halea (a women's moon-song/game) rather than an attested deity, and Bulan functions in Bikol chiefly as the common noun for the moon without secure individuation, so authoring them would constitute dubious padding.
