================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added two genuinely individuated figures beyond the four ruling spirits and the high deity Ulaumanua. Sikameinan is a named female water spirit of southern Siberut, documented at length by Singh, Kaptchuk & Henrich (2021) and Darmanto (2024/25) as a 'small god' who afflicts those who violate meat-sharing norms and is appeased through public shamanic rites. Pageta Sabbau (also called Teteu, 'Grandfather') is the mythic first shaman and culture hero of Loeb (1929), Spina (1981) and Krutak (2012), who taught shamanism and tattooing and, imprisoned beneath the earth, causes earthquakes. I deliberately did NOT author muntogat, rakrak or sirubeiteteu (these proved to be social-organizational categories — clan/sib/common-descent — not individuated beings), the generic sikoinan class of waterhole guardians, or Maligai (attested only as a myth-title, its protagonist not confirmed as a named individual); both new figures stand outside the four-ruler genealogy in the sources and are therefore recorded as solitary.

In the myth recorded on the Pagai islands and Siberut, his people grew jealous of his magic and resolved to kill him: when they raised their first uma (communal longhouse) they sent him down to dig beneath the central post and then dropped the post onto him, sealing him in the ground. In revenge he threw down the house with an earthquake, and thereafter he is the being who shakes the earth. Because of this he is addressed by the honorific Teteu, 'Grandfather', and the two names are used interchangeably; his name is treated as taboo except in the gravest speech. The connection ties together several threads of Mentawai custom: tattooing was carried out after the punen lepa purification and on a special porch so that blood would not fall to the ground and summon Pageta Sabbau or Teteu, bringing an earthquake. He is thus at once a founding culture hero, the archetype of the sikerei, and the subterranean cause of seismic disaster."
