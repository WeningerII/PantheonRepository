Pantheon Registry transcript — Tinguian (Itneg) pantheon, well-attested core.
Tradition: Tinguian (Itneg), northern Luzon (Abra), Philippines.

Primary scholarship (the figures below are drawn entirely from these two monographs):
  - Fay-Cooper Cole, "Traditions of the Tinguian: A Study in Philippine Folk-Lore"
    (Field Museum of Natural History, Anthropological Series, Vol. XIV, No. 1, 1915).
  - Fay-Cooper Cole, "The Tinguian: Social, Religious, and Economic Life of a Philippine Tribe"
    (Field Museum of Natural History, Anthropological Series, Vol. XIV, No. 2, 1922).

Six figures authored: Kadaklan (the greatest sky-spirit/creator), Agemem (his wife), Kimat
(the lightning, his dog), Bagatulayan (director of the celestial realm), Apadel / Kalagang
(guardian of the pinaing spirit-stones), and Makaboteng / Sanadan (guardian of deer and wild hogs).
Existing corpus figures tinguian_gagayoma (star goddess) and tinguian_takyayen (demigod) are NOT
re-authored here; no symmetric relation between them and these six is attested in Cole.

Scholarly note on hierarchy. Cole's own ethnography presents KADAKLAN as "the greatest" of all the
spirits — the powerful male sky-spirit who created the earth, sun, moon, and stars — while BAGATULAYAN
is the spirit who directs the activities of the celestial (sky) realm. A body of later, post-Cole
popular synthesis (reflected in many present-day encyclopedic summaries) instead ranks Bagatulayan as
the supreme creator with Kadaklan subordinate. Because this batch is sourced to Cole, both figures are
recorded with their Cole-attested roles, and the divergence is flagged in each figure's notes rather
than resolved by fiat. No native-language domain/faculty "term" values are asserted beyond names whose
Itneg form is given by Cole (e.g. kimat 'lightning', kadaklan 'the greatest', makaboteng 'one who
frightens', pinaing/saksak spirit-stones); no terms were invented.
