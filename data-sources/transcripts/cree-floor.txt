================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added eight well-attested Cree figures: the winter cannibal Wîhtikôw, the skeleton spirit Pâhkahkos, the Thunderbird Piyêsiw, the paired water monsters Misipisiw (Great Lynx, slayer of the existing Mahihkan in the flood cycle) and Misi-kinêpikw (Great Serpent, the thunderbirds' reciprocal enemy), the Rolling Head Cihcipistikwân (attested mother of the existing Wîsahkêcâhk and Mahihkan), and the James Bay hero-cycle figures Cahkâpês and Ayâs. Every figure carries at least two verified scholarly citations (Skinner 1911; Ahenakew 1929; Mandelbaum 1940/1979; Teicher 1960; Stevens 1971; Brown and Brightman 1988; Brightman 1988, 1989, 1993; Granzberg 1994; Ellis 1995; Wolvengrey 2001; Carlson 2009). I deliberately excluded maci-manitow, the 'Evil Spirit', because scholarship debates whether the good/evil manito dualism is aboriginal or missionary-shaped (Cooper, The Northern Algonquian Supreme Being, 1934; Brown and Brightman 1988), and likewise excluded personified but weakly individuated powers (Kîwêtin the North Wind, Pîsim the Sun) and unnamed spirit classes (mistâpêwak, cîpayak). The count slightly exceeds the typical range because this major tradition was severely under-populated relative to its documented pantheon.
