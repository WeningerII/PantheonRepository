Pantheon Registry — Mansi (Vogul) Ob-Ugric pantheon, primordial / mythic-age core
==================================================================================

Tradition: Mansi (the Vogul of the Konda, Pelym, Sosva and middle Ob, western Siberia).
Era keys used: primordial and mythic-age (the two registered Mansi eras).

Scope of this batch. The corpus already carries three Mansi figures — the upper
sky-father (mansi_numi_torum), the World-Surveyor Man (mansi_mir_susne_khum) and
the dawn/birth goddess Kaltes-Ekwa (mansi_kaltes_ekwa). The brief identifies the
missing core as (a) the underworld lord, Numi-Torum's brother and adversary, and
(b) the other sons of Numi-Torum beyond the World-Surveyor, plus the earth-mother
"Lower-World woman" who is sometimes held distinct from Kaltes. This batch adds
four firmly-attested figures:

  • mansi_khul_ater   — Khul-Ater / Kul-otyr / Xul'-otyr, lord of the Lower World,
                        master of the kuľ (disease-spirits), brother and standing
                        adversary of Numi-Torum, and (in the earth-diver variant)
                        the diver who fetches up the first mud.
  • mansi_as_iki      — As-iki, "the old man of the Ob," reckoned the eldest son of
                        Numi-Torum and lord of the whole Ob (As) basin, god of the
                        river's water and fish.
  • mansi_polum_torum — Polum-Torum, the "Pelym god," a son of Numi-Torum, patron of
                        the game-animals and fish of the Pelym country; his own son
                        Polum-torum-pyg ("Son of the Pelym God") is the patron spirit
                        of the Sosva settlement of Verhneye Nildino.
  • mansi_joli_torum_syan — Joli-Torum-syan / Joli-Torum (Földanya, the "Lower-World /
                        Earth Mother"), consort of Numi-Torum who dwells upon the
                        earth among people; partly distinguished from, partly merged
                        with, Kaltes-Ekwa in the sources.

A note on the Khanty/Mansi split and on doubled names. The Khanty (Ostyak) and Mansi
(Vogul) share this pantheon almost entirely; the corpus accordingly carries the
Khanty forms Num-Torum, Kul-Otyr, Mir-susne-khum, Kaltes and Postajankt-iki, and
the Mansi forms Numi-Torum, Mir-susne-khum and Kaltes-Ekwa. The figures below are
authored under the Mansi label and cite Mansi (Vogul) attestation specifically:
Kul-otyr/Xul'-otyr is documented in Munkácsi's Vogul corpus and named in the Vakh-
Vasyugan and southern-Vogul traditions as Numi-Torum's underworld brother who rules
the kuľ (disease-spirits); As-iki ("Ob old man," As = the Ob) and Polum-Torum (the
Pelym/Polum god, the river-name Polum/Pulim) are the Vogul river-sons of Numi-Torum;
and Joli-Torum / Joli-Torum-śäń ("Lower-World mother," joli 'lower' + torum 'sky/god'
+ śäń 'mother') is the Vogul earth-mother consort. Primary documentation is Bernát
Munkácsi, Vogul Népköltési Gyűjtemény (Vogul Folklore Collection, 1892–1921) and his
synthesis "A vogul nép ősi hitvilága" (Nyelvtudományi Közlemények, 1900–1902);
K. F. Karjalainen, Die Religion der Jugra-Völker (FF Communications 41/44/63,
1921–1927); Uno Holmberg/Harva, Finno-Ugric [and] Siberian Mythology (The Mythology
of All Races IV, 1927); and Géza Róheim, Hungarian and Vogul Mythology (Monographs
of the American Ethnological Society XXII, 1954), with the modern Ob-Ugric synthesis
of Kulemzin & Lukina et al.

A note on the earth-mother (Joli-Torum-syan vs Kaltes). Two scholarly treatments
co-exist and the brief flags exactly this: the Hungarian academic tradition
(Munkácsi; the standard Ob-Ugric handbooks) names Numi-Torum's consort the Földanya
/ Joli-Torum (the "Lower-World / Earth Mother") who dwells upon the earth among
people, and makes Kaltes his sibling (in variants his wife); elsewhere Kaltes-Ekwa
is herself the wife and earth-mother (a by-name Torum-śäń). Joli-Torum-syan is
therefore authored as a distinct figure with an explicit equated-with edge to
mansi_kaltes_ekwa recording the partial overlap; the affair-with-Kul-otyr expulsion
myth belongs to the Kaltes complex and is cited there, not duplicated onto Joli-Torum.

Not authored (verified-then-omitted, to avoid padding):
  • Nej-Torum / Naj-Ekwa as a son of Numi-Torum — the fire figure Naj-Ekwa is
    attested rather as a sibling of Numi-Torum (with Hotal-Ekwa the Sun and
    Etposojka the Moon), not stably as a distinct named "son," so it is omitted
    here for want of a firm son-attestation.
  • The further territorial river-sons named in passing (Nyaras-Nay-Ekwa,
    Tagt-Kotil-Ojka, Aj-As-Ojka, Nyor-Ojka) — local patron-spirits, too thinly
    attested as primordial pantheon deities to author without padding; omitted.
  • Numi-Torum's unnamed "one daughter" — not stably a single named figure; omitted.
