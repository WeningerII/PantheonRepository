================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Tumbuka theology proper is essentially exhausted by the existing Chiuta (supreme creator, with aliases Leza/Mulungu/Mulengi) and Chikang'ombe (territorial snake spirit): the remaining spirit vocabulary — vimbuza, vyanusi, vilombo, mizimu — denotes unnamed spirit CLASSES (Friedson, Dancing Prophets, 1996; Vail in Schoffeleers, Guardians of the Land, 1979), which the brief excludes. The only genuinely individuated named figures further documented are the legendary founder-ancestors of the Chikulamayembe paramountcy: Mlowoka (Kakalala Gondwe) and his son Gonapamuhanya, both well attested in T. Cullen Young (1932), Vail (1972) and McCracken (2012), and the latter still venerated at an annual festival. Both are entered as mortal heroic ancestors, wired to the pantheon via Gonapamuhanya's subordination to Chiuta. Popular websites listing additional "Tumbuka deities" (e.g. Mlengi as a separate fertility god, or Chaminuka) were checked and rejected as AI-generated or misattributed Shona material.
