REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added one figure, Batanga (Fatanga), the named Bontoc man of Lanao whose daughter Fukan married the culture hero Lumawig; he is explicitly named in Cole's 'Lumawig on Earth' (1916) and rests on the underlying ethnographies of Jenks (1905) and Seidenadel (1909), with modern analysis in Bacwaden (1997), and I wired him into the existing pantheon as father-of the already-registered Fukan (thus father-in-law of Lumawig). No other individuated figure qualified: the remaining actors in the Lumawig cycle and the sun/moon headhunting myth are attested but explicitly unnamed in the sources (Fukan's elder sister, the beheaded son of Chal-chal, Lumawig's two sons a.k.a. the 'sons of Cayapon,' Kabunian's two other sons, and the brother-and-sister flood survivors), so authoring them would create spurious named entities. I deliberately excluded 'Bangan' and 'Obban,' which circulate in popular listicles as daughters of Lumawig and 'Bugan,' because that pairing derives from Ifugao/pan-Cordilleran material (Bugan is the Ifugao name for the hero's wife) and is not grounded in Bontoc ethnography. The Bontoc named record beyond the existing five is therefore effectively exhausted by this single addition.
