Pantheon Registry — Ambundu (Mbundu / Kimbundu-speaking, Angola): cosmological CORE
====================================================================================

Scope and method
----------------
This transcript adds the well-attested cosmological deities of the Ambundu (Mbundu),
the Kimbundu-speaking peoples of north-central Angola (Luanda eastward, between the
Dande and Kwanza rivers; historically the kingdoms of Ndongo and Matamba). It
complements the already-registered Sudika-Mbambi epic trio drawn from Héli
Chatelain, "Folk-Tales of Angola" (1894, Memoirs of the American Folk-Lore Society I):
  - ambundu_sun_moon_daughter  (Daughter of the Sun and Moon)  [deity]
  - ambundu_sudika_mbambi      (the thunder hero-twin)          [demigod]
  - ambundu_kabundungulu       (his twin)                        [demigod]

Primary verification anchors:
  * Héli Chatelain, "Folk-Tales of Angola" (1894) — the Kimbundu-text corpus; in
    particular the tale "How Ngunza defied Death" (the Ngunza Kilundu vs. Lord
    Kalunga-ngombe story), and the framing tale #13 "The Son of Kimanaueze and the
    Daughter of Sun and Moon."
  * Alice Werner, "Myths and Legends of the Bantu" (1933), Ch. II "Where Man Came
    From, and How Death Came," which states verbatim: "The Ambundu of Angola speak
    of Kalunga, a word which may mean either Death, the King of the Netherworld
    (usually called, why I do not know, Kalunga-ngombe, 'Kalunga of the cattle'), or
    the sea." Werner draws directly on Chatelain.
  * Joseph C. Miller, "Kings and Kinsmen: Early Mbundu States in Angola" (Oxford:
    Clarendon, 1976) — Mbundu/Ndongo religion (the otiose high god; ancestors; the
    *ilundu* / *kilundu* nature-spirits).
  * Molefi K. Asante & Ama Mazama (eds.), "Encyclopedia of African Religion" (SAGE,
    2009), s.v. "Nzambi" (the Bantu high-god name; Kikongo/Kimbundu).
  * For Kianda (modern Angolan/Kimbundu folklore, NOT in Chatelain): Óscar Ribas's
    Angolan ethnography and the living Luanda Island Feast tradition; reflected in
    modern Angolan letters (Pepetela, "O Desejo de Kianda").

Honesty notes on the Kongo/Mbundu boundary (per the brief):
  * "Nzambi (a Mpungu)" as the supreme creator is best documented for the Kongo
    (Bakongo); for the Mbundu the *name* Nzambi and its high-god/creator sense are
    shared Bantu currency (Chatelain's Kimbundu material; Werner; the SAGE entry),
    but the elaborate creation cosmology (Nzambici, mbûngi, the fire-spark kalûnga)
    is specifically KONGO and is NOT attributed here to the Ambundu. Miller further
    cautions that in Mbundu usage *nzambi/jinzambi* can also denote ancestral
    spirits — so the cosmology is recorded conservatively.
  * "Kalunga / Kalunga-ngombe" as Death / King of the Netherworld / the sea is
    directly and explicitly attested FOR THE AMBUNDU (Werner; Chatelain's Ngunza
    tale) and is the firmest figure here.
  * "Kianda" is attested for the Kimbundu/Mbundu specifically, but as a living
    water-spirit cult and folklore figure documented from the 20th c. onward, not
    in the 1894 Chatelain layer; it is recorded with era "mythic-present" and
    modern sourcing.

No source provides kinship genealogy linking these three cosmological figures to one
another or to the existing Chatelain trio; speculative parentage is therefore NOT
invented. See the SOLITARY flags after the JSON.

SOLITARY: ambundu_nzambi — No surviving Mbundu/Kimbundu source (Chatelain 1894; Miller 1976; SAGE Encyclopedia of African Religion 2009) assigns Nzambi a consort, parent, or offspring. The elaborate Kongo creation pairing (Nzambi / Nzambici) is deliberately NOT imported, and the high god is characteristically otiose and stands outside any divine family in the Mbundu record. Flagged solitary for kinship.

SOLITARY: ambundu_kalunga — Werner (1933) and Chatelain (1894, 'How Ngunza defied Death') present Kalunga / Kalunga-ngombe as a primordial cosmic power (Death / King of the Netherworld / the sea) with no attested kin, consort, or descent in Mbundu tradition. His relation to the dead is functional (ruler-of-the-dead), not a kin tie to any registered figure. Flagged solitary for kinship.

SOLITARY: ambundu_kianda — In Kimbundu folklore (Óscar Ribas; the Luanda Island Feast; Pepetela) Kianda is an autonomous water-spirit / class of water-spirits with no attested parentage, consort, or offspring; sources give cult practice rather than genealogy. Flagged solitary for kinship.
