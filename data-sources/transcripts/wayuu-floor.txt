================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added four individuated figures beyond the existing core (Maleiwa, Juyá, Pulowi, Wanülü). Wolunka is the well-attested culture heroine of the Wayuu origin cycle — daughter of the rain (and, in some tellings, Pulowi) whose pierced toothed vagina inaugurated menstruation and human birth (Wilbert & Simoneau 1986; Suárez González 2024; Paz Ipuana 1972). Kai (the Sun) and Kashi (the Moon) are the two named, personified celestial sentinels appointed by Maleiwa, wired to him by subordination and paired with each other (Perrin 1987; Paz Ipuana 1972). Ulepala is the legendary man of the Orpheus-like myth of the journey to Jepira, the land of the dead, documented by Perrin (1976, 1987). I deliberately excluded borderline nature-classes (e.g. Palaa/Sea, Mma/Earth, Shüliwala/Stars, the rainbow Kasipoluin, and the unnamed transforming twins) as insufficiently individuated named persons.
