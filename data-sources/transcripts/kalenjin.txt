Tradition: Kalenjin (Nandi, Kipsigis and related peoples of the Rift Valley, Kenya)
ID prefix: kalenjin_
Eras (registered, exact): "primordial", "mythic"
  - "primordial" = the supreme sky/sun deity (cosmic, timeless): Asis.
  - "mythic"     = the thunder gods and the folklore ogre: the Ilet beings and Chemosit.
Author pass: first authoring of the firmly-attested, individuated figures of Kalenjin
(chiefly Nandi) traditional religion and folklore. No prior Kalenjin figures exist in the
corpus (id-space kalenjin_ is empty), so there are no existing figures to reference and no
cross-batch reciprocals owed to the corpus; all symmetric ties are reciprocated in-batch.

================================================================================
RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISION
================================================================================

ANCHOR SOURCES (verified):
  • A. C. Hollis, *The Nandi: Their Language and Folk-lore* (Oxford: Clarendon Press,
    1909). The foundational ethnography, based on four years' fieldwork before the
    colonial disruptions of 1905–06. It is the primary attestation for Asis(ta) the
    sun-god, for the two thunders Ilet ne-mie and Ilet ne-ya (with the forked- vs
    sheet-lightning swords, the deep-pool/waterfall dwelling and the rainbow as Ilet's
    discarded garments), and for the ogre Chemosit (the famous description by Kitapmetit
    arap Kipet: "a devil which prowls the nganasa [hut settlement] on the darkest nights,
    seeking people, especially children, to devour; it is half like a man and half like a
    huge, ape-faced bird," standing on one leg, with nine buttocks and a red mouth that
    shines like a lamp).
  • G. W. B. Huntingford, *The Nandi of Kenya: Tribal Control in a Pastoral Society*
    (London: Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1953). Documents Nandi social/ritual order, the high
    god Asis(ta), the thunder being Ilat/Ilet, and the ancestral spirits (oiik).
  • The HRAF/encyclopaedia tradition synthesising both (the "Nandi and Other Kalenjin
    Peoples" culture summary; Encyclopedia.com; the consolidated Wikipedia articles
    "Kalenjin mythology" and "Kalenjin folklore"), which quote Hollis and Huntingford
    directly: "They believed in one god, with many names, identified with the sun … Thunder
    was another named supernatural being"; "the oiik (spirits of dead ancestors) were also
    believed able to intervene in human life."
  • For Kalenjin religion more broadly the literature associated with Taaita Toweett and
    B. E. Kipkorir (e.g. Kipkorir & Welbourn, *The Marakwet of Kenya*, 1973) is noted; the
    secure, citable data on the individuated divine/folklore figures below, however, rest
    on Hollis (1909) and Huntingford (1953), and the figures are sourced to those.

FOUR firmly-attested, individuated figures are authored, in one singleton plus one cluster:

1) ASIS (Asista) — the supreme being, identified with the SUN. Authored as a DEITY,
   temporal era PRIMORDIAL (the cosmic, timeless high god, "the force behind everything,"
   giver of all good things, who lives in the sky). The Nandi name *Asis* is the
   contracted/short form of *Asista*, "the sun"; the god carries many praise-names —
   Cheptalil/Cheptalel ("the shining one / the one who glitters"), Chepkelyensokol,
   Chepopkoiyo, Chebonomei/Chebonamuni — recorded by Hollis. He is a distant, omnipotent
   sky-and-sun god prayed to at dawn (facing the rising sun) and at dusk. No individually
   named divine parent, spouse or child is attested for Asis in the sources, so he is
   flagged SOLITARY rather than wired to a guessed relative. (The classes that surround him
   — the oiik ancestral spirits, and Ilet — are not his attested blood-kin.)

2) THE THUNDER BEINGS — ILET and its two individuated namesakes ILET NE-MIE and
   ILET NE-YA. Authored as a cluster, era MYTHIC.
     • ILET (Ilat; also Toiyoi) — the thunder-and-rain god/being; he inhabits deep pools
       and waterfalls, and the rainbow is his discarded garments (Hollis). Authored as a
       NUMEN (a named impersonal-leaning power of thunder/rain/lightning), the overarching
       thunder being of whom the two named thunders are the good and evil aspects.
     • ILET NE-MIE ("the good thunder") and ILET NE-YA ("the evil/bad thunder"). Hollis,
       and the synthesising literature after him, individuate these STRONGLY: "Among the
       southern sections of the Kalenjin, Ilat's dual nature is identified as two separate
       deities, Ilet ne-mie and Ilet ne-ya. Among the Nandi, Ilet ne-mie and Ilet ne-ya
       respectively are good and bad thunder-gods." The crashing of thunder near at hand is
       Ilet ne-ya trying to come to earth to kill people, while the distant rumbling is
       Ilet ne-mie protecting man by driving away "his name-sake"; forked lightning is the
       sword of Ilet ne-ya, sheet lightning the sword of Ilet ne-mie. Because the texts (a)
       call them "two separate deities," (b) make them mutual NAMESAKES, and (c) cast one
       as actively driving the other off, they are authored as two distinct beings.
       Authored as NUMEN each.
   WIRING DECISION (accuracy-first):
     - The two thunders are wired to each other as `sibling` (reciprocated in-batch), with
       the relation note recording the "name-sake / two separate deities" datum — the
       firmly-attested tie. They are NOT given a parent/child edge to each other, since the
       sources frame them as namesakes/twinned aspects, not as parent and offspring.
     - Each of the two is also wired to ILET by `aspect of` (the overarching being),
       reciprocated on Ilet by `aspect`. (No blood parent edge is asserted between Ilet and
       the two named thunders; "aspect" is the precise, attested relation — the one god's
       dual nature split into the two named thunders.)
     - No kin edge is asserted between the thunder beings and Asis: the sources name Asis
       and Ilat as the "two principal deities" side by side, but attest no genealogical link
       between them, so none is invented.
   inheritability for all thunder faculties = "none" (these are not blood/descent powers).

3) CHEMOSIT (Chemosin; cf. the conflated cryptid name Kerit) — the half-man, one-legged,
   nine-buttocked, club-/spear-carrying ogre of Nandi/Kalenjin folklore who devours people,
   above all children. Authored as a NUMEN (a named folklore monster-being), era MYTHIC.
   Hollis's description (from Kitapmetit arap Kipet) is the anchor: half man / half huge
   ape-faced bird; stands on a single leg; nine buttocks; mouth red and shining at night
   like a lamp; uses a spear-like stick as crutch and means of propulsion; at night it
   sings near dwellings, its glowing mouth taken by children for a dance, luring them out to
   be devoured; it is said to eat the brain of its victims. SOLITARY: no kin is attested.

NOT AUTHORED AS INDIVIDUATED FIGURES (with reasons):
  • The OIK / OIIK (singular oiindet / olindet; also musambwanik, sing. musambwanindet) —
    the spirits of dead ancestors who dwell in Koret-ap-oi ("the spirit-land," under the
    earth) and may intervene in human life through sacrificial offerings of meat or beer.
    These are an unindividuated CLASS of ancestral spirits (per the prompt's explicit
    note), not named individual beings, and so are deliberately NOT authored as figures.
    They are recorded here as the ancestral-spirit class that mediates between Asis and the
    living.
  • The NANDI BEAR as a "cryptid" — the modern cryptozoological "Nandi bear" is an alleged
    unknown animal, distinct from the folklore ogre; the firmly-attested traditional being
    is Chemosit (the demonic bogey), which is what is authored. The cryptid framing is not.
  • The ORKOIYOT (orkoiik) — the Nandi diviner-warlord office (the historical Maasai-derived
    Talai family) is a real institution but is a human ritual/political office, not a
    mythological figure, and is out of scope for this registry pass.
