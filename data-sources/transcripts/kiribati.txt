PANTHEON REGISTRY — FIGURE-AUTHORING TRANSCRIPT
Tradition: Kiribati (Gilbertese / Tungaru), Micronesia
Registered era vocabulary for Kiribati: primordial (only)
Author: figure-authoring researcher
Date: 2026-06-23

PURPOSE
The Kiribati creation cycle already registered in the corpus runs Nareau the Elder
(kiribati_nareau_elder, the spider-creator) -> Na Atibu (kiribati_naatibu, the First
Rock) -> Nareau the Younger (kiribati_nareau_younger, the World-Maker), who makes the
world out of the dismembered body of Na Atibu. This transcript adds the generation of
beings born of, or shaped within, that creation, and the great ancestral anti
("gods/spirits") whom the Gilbertese counted famous "from the earliest times."

SOURCE BASIS
The canonical corpus for Gilbertese mythology is Sir Arthur Grimble's field material,
collected 1916-1930 and published as:
  - Grimble, Arthur F. Tungaru Traditions: Writings on the Atoll Culture of the Gilbert
    Islands (ed. H. E. Maude, Univ. of Hawai'i Press / Pacific Islands Monograph Series,
    1989).
  - Grimble, Arthur F. "Myths from the Gilbert Islands" (Folklore, vol. 33, 1922).
  - Grimble, Arthur F. Return to the Islands (1957) and A Pattern of Islands (1952).
  - Grimble, Rosemary. Migrations, Myth and Magic from the Gilbert Islands: Early Writings
    of Sir Arthur Grimble (Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1972).
  - Maude, H. E. (ed.), and the Maude & Grimble Papers; The Book of Banaba (1994) for the
    Banaban (Ocean Island) recension of Tabakea.
Secondary syntheses consulted for cross-checking: Encyclopedia.com "Micronesian Religions:
Mythic Themes"; Oxford Reference (Dict. of Asian Mythology / World Mythology); Wikipedia
"Nareau", "Na Atibu", "Auriaria", "Nei Tituaabine", "Banaba".

GENEALOGY ESTABLISHED FROM THE SOURCES
From sand and water Nareau the Elder made the primal pair Na Atibu ("the Rock") and his
consort Nei Teukez (also written Nei Teakea). That pair "brought forth" the second
generation of beings: Te Ikawai (the Eldest), Nei Marena (the Between), Te Nao (the Wave),
Na Kika (the Octopus), Riiki / Ruki (the Eel) — and, in the registered line, Nareau the
Younger. Of these, RIIKI and NA KIKA are the two whose feats drive the making of the world
(Riiki prises sky from earth; Na Kika heaves up the land) and are authored here, wired as
children of the already-registered Na Atibu (kiribati_naatibu) and therefore siblings of
the already-registered Nareau the Younger.

The great ancestral anti famous "from the earliest times" — named together in Grimble as
TABUARIKI (the Thunderer), AURIARIA, RIIKI, NEI TITUAABINE, and NEI TEWENEI — are authored
as primordial-era deity-ancestors. AURIARIA (the red giant) and NEI TITUAABINE (the
vegetation goddess) are sister-and-husband; TABUARIKI (thunder/lightning) and NEI TEVENEI
(the comet/meteor) are husband-and-wife. TABAKEA, the primal turtle, is authored from the
Banaban (Ocean Island) recension in which he is the first of all beings; because that
recension is a parallel cosmogony to the registered Tarawan line (in which Nareau the Elder
is first), Tabakea is NOT wired as ancestor of the registered Nareau, and the relationship
is recorded in notes only.

NOTES ON TYPING / FIELDS
- era is "primordial" for every figure (the only value registered for Kiribati).
- type: the cosmogonic and ancestral gods are "deity"; Auriaria, the giant ancestral hero
  who led settlement and from whom chiefly lines descend, is entered as "demigod"
  (culture-hero) consistent with how Nareau the Younger is typed in the corpus.
- Native Gilbertese terms are given only where securely attested (Riiki, Na Kika, anti,
  kimoa, Karawa, Marawa, Na Iabu, Kai-n-tiku-aba, te ben, te non/te tarine, te kaina);
  Gilbertese is recorded in Latin orthography, so script = "Latin" and rom = the value.
- domains/faculties/materialCulture are populated as fully as the sources attest, each with
  a non-empty source reference, per the enrichment mandate.

SOLITARY:
- kiribati_tabakea — Authored from the Banaban (Ocean Island) recension, a parallel cosmogony to the registered Tarawan line. In that recension Tabakea is the FIRST being and the father of Nareau (with a brother, Na Kaa), which would contradict the already-registered genealogy in which Nareau the Elder is first; therefore Tabakea is deliberately left kinless rather than wired as parent of kiribati_nareau_elder / kiribati_nareau_younger, and his attested brother Na Kaa is not present in the corpus. Cited reason: Grimble & Maude, The Book of Banaba (1994); Grimble, Tungaru Traditions (ed. Maude, 1989). The Tabakea-as-father-of-Nareau tradition is recorded in his notes.

RECIPROCAL-NEEDED (add to existing corpus figures):
- kiribati_nareau_younger sibling kiribati_riiki  (add reciprocal 'sibling' relation to the existing figure kiribati_nareau_younger: Riiki the eel is a child of Na Atibu, hence brother of Nareau the Younger — Grimble, Tungaru Traditions, ed. Maude 1989).
- kiribati_nareau_younger sibling kiribati_na_kika  (add reciprocal 'sibling' relation to the existing figure kiribati_nareau_younger: Na Kika the octopus is a child of Na Atibu, hence brother of Nareau the Younger — Grimble, Tungaru Traditions, ed. Maude 1989).
- kiribati_nareau_younger enemy kiribati_tabuariki  (add reciprocal 'enemy' relation to the existing figure kiribati_nareau_younger: Nareau the Younger provokes the thunder-god Tabuariki to wrestle and breaks his arm — Grimble, Tungaru Traditions, ed.
