================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added two figures — Jíkuri (the personified peyote cactus, Lophophora williamsii) and Bakána (the visionary sedge/cactus being) — the two named, individuated sacred plant-persons that Rarámuri ethnography documents as full agents beyond the existing cosmic five. Jíkuri is explicitly called a "demi-god" and "protecting genius" by Lumholtz (1902) and, in his greatest form hikuli walula siríame, is held to be the twin brother of the creator Onorúame; because that is a symmetric kinship to an already-registered figure, the bond is recorded in prose and the two new plant-beings are instead wired to each other as the paired paramount curing plants (Bye 1979 ranks Bakána above peyote). Both figures rest on multiple independent scholarly sources (Lumholtz 1902; Bennett & Zingg 1935; Bye 1979; Levi 1999; Bonfiglioli; Paredes & Irigoyen-Rascón 1992). I deliberately did not author further candidates: the syncretic "sukristo"/"santi" (God's sons and daughters) and the bakánoa aquatic spirits are documented as plural classes rather than individuated named beings, so authoring them would violate the no-spirit-class rule.
