================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

Added the four figures the Mandan record genuinely individuates beyond the existing core: Old-Woman-Who-Never-Dies, the undying corn goddess of the waterfowl feasts (Maximilian 1843, quoted verbatim in Frazer 1922; Bowers 1950; Beckwith 1938; Colvin 2012), and the three corn-bringing brothers of the emergence tradition — Good Furred Robe, first corn priest, rain-robe owner, and founder of the Goose Society, with his brothers Cornhusk Earrings and Head for Rattle (No Hair on Him), whose skulls the corn bundle was said to contain as late as 1948 (Bowers 1950; Fenn 2014; Will & Hyde 1917; Lowie 1913). I declined to author the Sun, the Moon, "Black Medicine," and "Sweet Medicine," which the Encyclopedia of World Cultures (1991) lists among important sacred beings but which the verifiable ethnography does not individuate with distinct names, myths, or cults, and Maximilian's "Lord of Life" (Omahank-Numakschi), which scholarship identifies with the creator already in the corpus as First Creator. Bundle-myth heroes such as Charred Body, Grandson, and Packs Antelope were excluded as belonging to Hidatsa rather than Mandan tradition, and Foolish Woman and Scattered Corn are twentieth-century informants, not sacred beings.
