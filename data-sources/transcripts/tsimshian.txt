Figure-authoring batch — the well-attested CORE of the Tsimshian (Pacific
Northwest Coast) transformer cosmology. The corpus previously held only the
Asdiwal-saga lineage (hatsenas, skeena_daughter, asdiwal); the pivotal
transformer-trickster and the sky-beings of the daylight-theft were missing.
This adds four firmly attested figures from Franz Boas & Henry W. Tate,
"Tsimshian Mythology" (31st Annual Report of the Bureau of American Ethnology,
1909–1910; Washington, 1916, pp. 29–1037) and Boas, "Tsimshian Texts" (Nass
River Dialect; BAE Bulletin 27, 1902), centred on the named Raven cycle and the
episode "Txä'msEm and Lô·gôbolā'": Txä'msem (the Giant / Raven), his brother
Lô·gôbolā', the Chief of Heaven who hoarded the daylight in the box "Maa," and
the Chief of Heaven's Daughter who swallowed the transformed Raven (the cedar
leaf) and bore him. The two brothers are reciprocal siblings and rivals; the
sky-chief and his daughter are wired by the directed father/mother pair to
Txä'msem. Per the registered era scheme, the whole Raven creation/transformer
cycle is placed in `pre-flood`. The popular "Walks-All-Over-the-Sky" sun-origin
tale and the personal name "Laxha" were deliberately NOT folded into the
daylight-keeper, as the ethnographic sources do not support that identification
(Laxha is elsewhere a clamshell-emergence being; the light-keeper of the Raven
myth is named only by title). No restricted ceremonial (halait) material is used.

Four figures completing the Tsimshian transformer core — Txä'msem (the Giant /
Raven), his brother and rival Lô·gôbolā', the Chief of Heaven who hoarded the
daylight in the box Maa, and the sky-chief's daughter who bore the Raven — all
sourced to Boas & Tate, "Tsimshian Mythology" (1916), and Boas, "Tsimshian
Texts" (1902). The two brothers are reciprocal siblings/rivals; the sky-chief
and his daughter are wired to Txä'msem by the father/mother pair.
