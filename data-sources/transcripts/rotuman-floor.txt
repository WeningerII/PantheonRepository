================================================================================
REVIEWER NOTE
================================================================================

I added three genuinely individuated, well-attested legendary figures drawn from the canonical corpus of Rotuman oral tradition: Kirkirsasa, the tattooed woman of Tarkei who subdues a giant by making him laugh (the mythic charter of Rotuman female ritual clowning, analysed at length in Hereniko's Woven Gods), and the paired sisters Nujkau and Nujmaga, whose named tale stands in the Titifanua-Churchward collection and who are wired to each other by a reciprocal sibling relation. The pre-Christian divine tier (the Tagaroa/Tagroa Siria/Tairagoni fertility complex) and the founding ancestors (Raho, Tokainiua) are already exhausted by the existing corpus, and no further individuated deities could be corroborated; in particular I deliberately excluded the widely web-circulated harvest goddess "Saulei," which appears only in Wikipedia-derived content and could not be located in Gardiner (1898), Churchward, Howard, Hereniko, or any peer-reviewed ethnography. Sources consulted include Titifanua & Churchward's Tales of a Lonely Island, Churchward's serial "Rotuman Legends" (Oceania, 1937-39), Hereniko's Woven Gods (1995), Reed & Hames's Myths and Legends of Fiji and Rotuma (1967), and Gardiner's The Natives of Rotuma (1898).
