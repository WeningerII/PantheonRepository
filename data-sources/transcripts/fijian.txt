Fijian (iTaukei) pantheon — core figures still missing from the registry.

The five already-registered figures cover the supreme creator and several major
spheres: fijian_degei (Degei, the serpent kalou-vu and creator), fijian_dakuwaqa
(the shark-god of Cakaudrove), fijian_ratumaibulu (lord of the underworld and
agriculture), fijian_rokola (Degei's son, patron of carpenters and canoe-builders),
and fijian_veli (the little forest spirits). What is conspicuously absent is the
WAR / SEAFARING / NAKAUVADRA-CYCLE layer of the tradition. This batch supplies it.

I verified each figure against the named scholarship and the standard
ethnographic record. The decisive sources are Thomas Williams, Fiji and the
Fijians (1858) — the foundational missionary ethnography that records the two
classes of gods (kalou-vu "root-gods" and kalou-yalo "deified spirits"), the
bure-kalou (god-house/temple) system, the bete (priests), and the war-gods;
Basil Thomson, The Fijians: A Study of the Decay of Custom (1908); Lorimer
Fison, Tales from Old Fiji (1904) and his 1895 study "Ancestor Worship Among the
Fijians"; A. M. Hocart's Lau/Northern-States ethnography; R. A. Derrick, A
History of Fiji (1946); and the modern ANU Press synthesis Degei's Descendants:
Spirits, Place and People in Pre-Cession Fiji (ed. Paul Geraghty et al.).

Figures authored (5):
  1. fijian_daucina        — Daucina / Ndauthina, the lamp-bearer; god of light,
                             seafaring and seduction. (Williams 1858; Thomson 1908.)
  2. fijian_ravuravu       — Ravuravu, a war-god / patron of slayers, a kalou
                             cast down from the Skyland. (Williams 1858; the
                             "ravu" = "to club/kill" etymology is genuine Fijian.)
  3. fijian_cagawalu       — Cagawalu, the supreme war-god of Bau, whose cult rose
                             with the power of Bau and the chief Cakobau. (Williams
                             1858; Derrick 1946; Sahlins, Islands of History 1985.)
  4. fijian_nakausabaria   — one of the twin grandsons of Degei of the Nakauvadra
                             cycle: rebel war-bringers and patrons of canoe-building.
  5. fijian_nacirikaumoli  — the other twin (Cirinakaumoli / Thirikaumoli).
                             (Fison 1904/1895; Thomson 1908; Degei's Descendants.)

Verification notes that shaped the data (accuracy-first):
- DAUCINA's filiation. The brief floats Daucina as "a son of Degei." The sources
  do NOT firmly attest this: his parentage "varies by region and oral tradition,"
  and where a genealogy is given (the Cakaudrove "gone mai wai," child-from-the-sea
  origin) it is NOT Degei. The only Degei link is a loose modern "holy trinity"
  conceit. Per the hard rule to author only firmly-attested detail, I therefore do
  NOT assign parentIds ["fijian_degei"]; Daucina is entered kinless with a cited
  SOLITARY flag. (His name IS securely glossed: Fijian "cina" = lamp/torch, "dau-"
  = expert-at / habitual-doer, so Daucina = "the lamp-bearer.")
- THE TWINS' genealogy. The dominant, well-attested tradition (Fiji Times retelling
  of the Nakauvadra saga; Degei's Descendants; the figures on Fiji's Coat of Arms)
  makes Nakausabaria and Cirinakaumoli the TWIN GRANDSONS of Degei (a competing
  saga-variant makes them his sons; another names their father the Tui Nubulevu).
  Because their father is not a registered figure and "grandsons" — not "sons" — is
  the prevailing framing, I do NOT assert parentIds ["fijian_degei"] (which would
  misstate them as Degei's children). Their cited kinship is captured as a
  "twin sibling" bond to each other (reciprocal in-batch) and an "enemy" relation to
  fijian_degei (the war they waged against him), with the grandson genealogy noted.
  parentRoles records the contested descent.
- WAR-GOD material culture is real and attested: the bure-kalou (god-house/war-
  temple), into which war-clubs (i wau / totokia) heavy with mana were placed, and
  to which first-fruits and (for the great war-gods) human sacrifices were offered;
  the bete (priest) as the god's possessed medium. These populate materialCulture.
- TOKAIRABE was considered: attested only thinly, as a prayer-mediator "son" of
  Degei (Ngendei) rather than as an independent war-god, so I omit it rather than
  pad. The four war/seafaring/cycle figures above are the firmly-attested core.

Names are given English-leading with attested native terms; scripts are "Latin"
(Fijian is written in Latin orthography) and romanizations equal the value. Where
a specific native word is not securely attested I leave term.value "".
