================================================================================
RESEARCH SUMMARY AND AUTHORING DECISIONS
================================================================================

Two strata of Malagasy tradition are authored here. (1) The cosmogonic stratum: the
sky/earth creation pair Zanahary and Ratovantany, whose bargain over the human person —
soul from the sky, body from the clay/earth, each reclaimed at death — is the single
best-attested datum of pan-Malagasy belief (recognised, with local variation, across the
island). (2) The epic stratum: the hero Ibonia (Iboniamasiboniamanoro) and the two other
firmly individuated actors of his story — his betrothed Iampelasoamananoro and his
adversary Ravatovolovoay, the "Stone Man."

ANCHOR SOURCES (verified):
  • Jørgen Ruud, *Taboo: A Study of Malagasy Customs and Beliefs* (Oslo Univ. Press /
    George Allen & Unwin, 1960) — fady, the razana (ancestors), Zanahary, the soul/body
    division.
  • Lee Haring, *Ibonia: Epic of Madagascar* (Bucknell Univ. Press / Associated Univ.
    Presses, 1994) — the first full English translation of the Merina Ibonia (recorded
    1877 from an anonymous bard); and his *How to Read a Folktale: The 'Ibonia' Epic from
    Madagascar* (Open Book Publishers, 2013), an open-access edition with full apparatus.
  • Charles Renel, *Contes de Madagascar* (3 vols., 1910) and *Ancêtres et dieux* (Bull.
    de l'Académie malgache, 1920–21) — the creation myth (Ratovoantany the self-created
    earth-being modelling clay figures dried in the sun; Zanahary giving them life).
  • James Sibree, *The Great African Island: Chapters on Madagascar* (London, 1880) —
    the supreme being known as Zanahary ("the Creator") and Andriamanitra ("the Fragrant
    One"); names recognised all over the island.
  • Lars Dahle, *Specimens of Malagasy Folk-lore* (Antananarivo, 1877) — the early printed
    text of the Ibonia recitation (the "Ibonia" / "Rabonia" tantara).

KEY DECISIONS:
  • ANDRIAMANITRA / ANDRIANANAHARY are NOT authored as a distinct figure. The sources are
    explicit that Andriamanitra ("the Fragrant Lord/Prince"; from andriana + manitra,
    "fragrant," the quality ascribed to a dead noble) is a TITLE of the supreme being, used
    interchangeably with Zanahary, and that Andriananahary ("the Creator/Creating One") is
    "rarely used as a standalone name … mostly used as an epithet: 'Andriamanitra
    Andriananahary'" (Sibree 1880; 1911 Encyclopædia Britannica s.v. Madagascar). They are
    therefore folded into Zanahary.epithets, per the authoring instruction, rather than
    duplicated as a paired high-god.
  • RATOVANTANY is "the self-created / uncreated one" (the name is glossed in the
    literature as "self-created," and he "shot up like a plant from the ground"). He is
    therefore NOT a child of Zanahary; he has no attested divine parent or sibling. He and
    Zanahary are wired as reciprocal ALLIES / co-creators (the cosmogonic compact), not as
    kin. type = numen (a self-arising earth power / chthonic numen) rather than a peopled
    deity-figure, since he "was not a prominent deity to any Malagasy people" yet the myth is
    ubiquitous.
  • THE RAZANA (ancestors) are recorded as a venerated CLASS in Zanahary's and Ratovantany's
    notes/relations context, but are NOT authored as individuated figures (they are a
    generic collective, the cornerstone of Malagasy cult but not named persons).
  • IBONIA'S PARENTS — his mother Rasoabemananoro ("Beautiful-Rich," called Rasoa) and his
    putative father, the "Prince of the Center" (an andriambahoaka, a "prince of the
    people"), with the diviner Ranakombe who effects the conception — are firmly named in
    the epic but are NOT authored in this pass (the roster is the hero, his betrothed and his
    adversary). Per the validator rule "parentIds … resolve within the batch (or omit),"
    Ibonia's parentIds are therefore OMITTED and the parentage is recorded in his notes and
    lifecycle instead of asserting an unresolved edge.
  • The Ravatovolovoay gloss "Stone Man" is secure (the element vato = "stone" in Malagasy,
    Ra- the honorific); the full morphological reading is given as Haring's and attributed to
    him rather than over-parsed.

FIVE figures are authored: two primordial (Zanahary, Ratovantany) and three mythic
(Ibonia, Iampelasoamananoro, Ravatovolovoay).
