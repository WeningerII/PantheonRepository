Figure-authoring batch — the Blackfoot (Niitsitapi) divine core.

The corpus held only the Morning Star / Feather Woman / Star Boy lineage
(ipisowaahs, soatsaki, poia) and none of the central deities. This batch adds the
well-attested core of the Blackfoot pantheon — the creator-trickster Napi (Old
Man), the supreme Sun (Naato'si), his wife the Moon (Ko'komiki'somm), the Thunder
who gave the medicine pipe (Ksiistsikomm), Cold Maker (Aisoyimstan), and the
under-water Wind Maker — sourced to George Bird Grinnell, "Blackfoot Lodge Tales"
(1892) and "Blackfeet Indian Stories" (1913/1915); Clark Wissler & D. C. Duvall,
"Mythology of the Blackfoot Indians" (1908); and Walter McClintock, "The Old North
Trail" (1910). The Sun and Moon are wired as reciprocal spouses and as the father
and mother of the existing Morning Star (ipisowaahs). Niitsi'powahsin orthography
(Naato'si, Ko'komiki'somm, Aapisowaahs, Sspommitapiiksi) follows the Blackfoot
Confederacy and native-languages.org; the abstract creator Apistotoke is described
but, being explicitly never personified, is not entered as a figure.
