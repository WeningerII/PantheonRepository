================================================================================
THE GAP
================================================================================

An 81-name check across the WHOLE registry returned 10 hits and 71 absences.
Two of the ten hits are aliases already carried — "Setanta" on Cú Chulainn,
"Lugaid" on Lugh — and one is false: "Net" matched `egyptian_neith`. Two names
returned ABSENT that are in fact present, because the corpus files them with the
definite article: **the Dagda** and **the Morrígan**. That is a property of the
sweep tool and not of the corpus, and it is recorded here because the next lane
to sweep this tradition will hit it too.

**THE REGISTRY HAS AN ERA REGISTERED FOR THE FENIAN CYCLE AND NOT ONE FIGURE
IN IT.**

`fenian-cycle` is the sixth era in the Irish vocabulary. Nothing is filed there.
The Fenian or Fianna Cycle is one of the four great cycles of Irish literature,
it is the one that survived longest in living oral tradition on both sides of
the Irish Sea, and the corpus held **no Fionn mac Cumhaill**, no **Oisín**, no
**Oscar**, no **Diarmuid**, no **Gráinne**, no **Caoilte**, no **Niamh** and no
**Sadhbh**.

An empty registered era is the clearest possible statement that a corpus knows
what it is missing.

The rest is the same shape as every lane since 13:

- **The Táin is a story about a bull and the corpus did not hold the bull.**
  It held Medb, Ailill, Cú Chulainn, Fergus, Ferdiad and Findabair — everyone
  who fights over **Donn Cúailnge**, the Brown Bull of Cooley — and neither him
  nor **Finnbhennach**, the white-horned bull of Connacht that he kills at the
  end, in a fight that reshapes the landscape of Ireland.
- **The Morrígan without the other two.** The Morrígna are a trio — Badb, Macha
  and the Mórrígan, daughters of Ernmas — and the corpus held Ernmas and one
  daughter.
- **Ériu without Banba and Fódla.** The three sovereignty goddesses meet the
  Milesians on three mountains, each asking that the land be named after her;
  the corpus held the one who won. Lane 14's five-of-six-idols pattern, in a
  set of three.
- **No Deirdre.** The most famous love story in the literature, and the corpus
  held Conchobar, who wants her, and Fergus, who goes into exile over her, and
  not her or **Naoise**.
- **Dian Cécht without Miach and Airmed**, who are the reason Nuada — whom the
  corpus holds — gets his hand back and his kingship with it.
- **Nuada restored without Bres**, the half-Fomorian king whose tyranny in the
  interval is what the second battle of Mag Tuired is fought to end.
- **No Amergin**, the poet who speaks the first poem composed in Ireland.

================================================================================
WHAT THIS BATCH DOES NOT INGEST
================================================================================

**The Fianna, the Tuatha Dé Danann, the Fomorians and the Fir Bolg are
collectives.** They are peoples and war-bands, not persons. Named members are
records; the groups are REJECT rows.

**The Salmon of Knowledge is an animal that is eaten**, and the hazel nuts it
ate are nuts. Both are REJECT rows on the rule that has held since lane 13,
which is also why this batch does not ingest Bran and Sceolang, Fionn's hounds.
The two BULLS are ingested, and the transcript says why on their records.

AUTHORED FIGURES — III. THE TWO BULLS THE TÁIN IS ABOUT
================================================================================

The corpus held Medb, Ailill, Cú Chulainn, Fergus, Ferdiad and Findabair — the
people who fight over a bull — and not the bull. The Táin Bó Cúailnge is *The
Cattle Raid of Cooley*; the cattle are the plot.

These two are ingested where five named horses were rejected in lanes 13, 14 and
15, and the ground is the one those rejections stated: those horses were
identified by a name and an owner. These two have a quarrel of their own, fight
each other the length of Ireland, change the landscape doing it, and one of them
kills the other and then dies of it. The war is fought to acquire them and the
war ends when they have settled it themselves.
