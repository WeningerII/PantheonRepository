Mangaian (Cook Islands) pantheon — authored from scratch from William Wyatt Gill's classic record, the Mangaian cosmos being entirely absent from the registry. Primary source throughout: William Wyatt Gill, Myths and Songs from the South Pacific (London: Henry S. King & Co., 1876), supplemented by Gill, Historical Sketches of Savage Life in Polynesia; with Illustrative Clan Songs (Wellington: Government Printer, 1880).

## Research synthesis

I authored 12 firmly-attested figures of the Mangaian core, all verified against Gill's record and cross-checked against the standard handbooks (Roland B. Dixon, Oceanic Mythology, 1916; E. R. Tregear, The Maori-Polynesian Comparative Dictionary, 1891; Robert D. Craig, Dictionary of Polynesian Mythology, 1989). All carry origin "canon", tradition "Mangaian", ids prefixed mangaian_, and eras drawn only from primordial / mythic / mythic-prehistoric.

Key findings and decisions:

- COSMOGONY: Gill's Mangaian universe is the hollow of a vast coconut shell; its interior is Avaiki (the underworld), at whose narrow base — where the imaginary shell's sides almost meet, so that her knees touch her chin — dwells the primal mother Vari-ma-te-takere ("the very beginning"). She had no parents; I flag her SOLITARY at the apex of the genealogy (her descendants are wired). She plucked six children from her sides — three from the right (males: Vatea, Tinirau, Tango), three from the left (females: Tumu-te-ana-oa the Echo, Raka lord of winds, Tu-metua the loved one kept beside her). I author Vatea and Tinirau (firmly attested, narratively central); Tango, Tumu-te-ana-oa, Raka and Tu-metua are real but minor — I cite them in Vari's notes rather than authoring thin stubs (the brief asks for ~7–10 firmly-attested core figures).

- VATEA / AVATEA = "noon"; Vari's first-born, plucked from her right side; a being split vertically, the right half a man and the left half a fish; the father of gods and men. He took to wife Papa, whom Gill gives as daughter of Timatekore ("Nothing-more") and Tamaiti-ngava-ringavari; Timatekore and Tamaiti are cited in Papa's notes, not authored.

- TANGAROA and RONGO are the twin sons of Vatea and Papa. Gill records the famous precedence dispute: Tangaroa was the first-born (the elder, here typed accordingly), with light/fair (yellow) hair — so that the Mangaians at first took Europeans for his children — and his portion is everything red (red taro, red fish); but Vatea favoured the younger Rongo, who was given precedence and became the great god of Mangaia (agriculture, the taro, and war). Tangaroa in pique emigrated (to Rarotonga and Aitutaki). I wire them as reciprocal twin siblings, both children of Vatea+Papa.

- TANE, TANGIIA and TONGA-ITI (Motoro) are further sons / tribal gods; Gill names Rongo as elder brother to Tonga-iti (the lizard-god), to Tangiia and to Tāne. Wired as sons of Vatea+Papa and siblings of the twins.

- INA / INA-MOE-AITU is the Mangaian form of the pan-Polynesian Hina: she beats out tapa (cloth) in the moon, and is the heroine of the Tuna (eel) tale from which the first coconut sprang. Her divine parentage is not fixed in Gill's core Mangaian account (the eel-tale gives only the maiden Ina), so I flag her SOLITARY with that cited reason.

- MAUI is the youngest, the fire-bringer and sun-snarer, typed demigod. In the Mangaian legend his parents are Ru (supporter of the heavens) and Buataranga (guardian of the lower world); he wrests the secret of fire from the fire-god Mauike in Avaiki and snares the sun Ra. Ru and Buataranga are not in this batch, so Maui carries no within-batch kin — flagged SOLITARY with that cited reason (parents named in notes).

- MIRU (Miru-kura, "the red") is the hag of Avaiki who receives the souls of the (common) dead, stupefies them with kava and cooks them in her oven; the Tapairu are her daughters. No parentage is given her in Gill — flagged SOLITARY.

- TINIRAU, second child of Vari (plucked from her right side), is the lord/guardian of all fish, himself half-fish; wired as Vari's child and Vatea's brother.

faculties are kept distinct from domains (verb-phrase powers vs. spheres) and from materialCulture (attested objects: Rongo's sacred drum and his marae Akaoro and Orongo; Maui's fire and the sun-snare; Ina's tapa-mallet and cloth; the coconut). Every figure carries ≥1 citation, and every domain / faculty / materialCulture entry a non-empty reference. Inheritability is "none" throughout (these powers are not represented as heritable in the source). Native Mangaian/Rarotongan terms are given only where I could verify a real word; the `term` object is omitted otherwise (no invented forms).
