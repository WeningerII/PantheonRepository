Figure-authoring batch — smallest-first sweep (corrected): completing the
Tagalog pantheon.

Verified scope before authoring: the Tagalog (pre-colonial Philippine) pantheon
is a genuinely small, finite set — unlike a literate empire's open-ended god-list.
The corpus holds only Bathala and his three daughters (Mayari, Tala, Hanan); the
attested pantheon adds ~10 more deities named in the early Spanish accounts
(Juan de Plasencia, "Customs of the Tagalogs," 1589; the Boxer Codex, c. 1590;
Pedro de San Buenaventura, "Vocabulario de lengua Tagala," 1613) and systematized
by F. Landa Jocano ("Outline of Philippine Mythology," 1969). The deities are
attested in the primary lists; the kinship among them follows Jocano's 20th-c.
reconstruction, noted as such. Full deities take the registered Tagalog era
'primordial'; Bathala's demigod son Apolaki takes 'heroic-age' (as his sisters do).
